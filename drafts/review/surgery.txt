
=== Surgery / Acute abdomen, appendicitis, obstruction
Q_C_002 [M] A 45-year-old man on long-term NSAIDs develops sudden, severe, generalized abdominal pain. He has a rigid, board-like abdomen with absent bowel sounds. Which finding on an erect chest X-ray best confirms the likely diagnosis?
    A. Multiple air-fluid levels
   *B. Free gas under the diaphragm
    C. Ground-glass appearance
    D. Sentinel loop
   key=B ref=Bailey & Love 27e (Peptic ulcer / Peritonitis)
   concept: Sudden severe pain with board-like rigidity indicates a perforated peptic ulcer causing peritonitis. An erect chest X-ray shows free gas under the diaphragm (pneumoperitoneum) in most cases. Air-fluid levels suggest obstruction; a sentinel loop suggests pancreatitis.
Q_C_003 [M] A 70-year-old chronically constipated man presents with gross abdominal distension, absolute constipation and colicky pain. Abdominal X-ray shows a massively dilated inverted 'U' (coffee-bean) loop arising from the pelvis and pointing to the right upper quadrant. What is the most likely diagnosis?
   *A. Sigmoid volvulus
    B. Intussusception
    C. Caecal volvulus
    D. Paralytic ileus
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love 27e (Intestinal obstruction)
   concept: Sigmoid volvulus is common in elderly, constipated patients; the twisted loop gives a 'coffee-bean' (omega) sign. If there is no gangrene or peritonitis, initial management is endoscopic decompression with a flatus tube; recurrent cases need sigmoid resection.

=== Surgery / Breast lumps & carcinoma breast
Q_C_009 [M] A 48-year-old woman presents with a discrete breast lump. According to the standard 'triple assessment', which combination should be used to evaluate it?
   *A. Clinical examination + imaging (mammography/USG) + biopsy (core or FNAC)
    B. Clinical examination + tumour markers + chest X-ray
    C. Mammography + CT + bone scan
    D. FNAC + CA 15-3 + PET scan
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love 27e (The breast)
   concept: Triple assessment = clinical examination + imaging (mammography and/or ultrasound) + pathology (core biopsy preferred; FNAC acceptable). It maximizes diagnostic accuracy for any breast lump. Mammography suits older women; ultrasound suits younger, dense breasts.

=== Surgery / Gallstones, pancreatitis
Q_C_007 [M] A 45-year-old man with heavy alcohol use has severe epigastric pain radiating to the back with vomiting. Which serum enzyme is the MOST specific for diagnosing acute pancreatitis?
   *A. Serum lipase
    B. Serum amylase
    C. Alkaline phosphatase
    D. Lactate dehydrogenase
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love 27e (The pancreas)
   concept: Both amylase and lipase rise in acute pancreatitis, but lipase is more specific and remains elevated longer. Gallstones and alcohol are the two commonest causes. Amylase may also rise in perforation, mesenteric ischaemia and salivary disease.

=== Surgery / Hernias (inguinal, femoral)
Q_C_005 [H] An elderly woman with a small femoral hernia develops fever and localized tenderness over the lump, but continues to pass stool and flatus with no abdominal distension. At operation only part of the circumference of the bowel wall (the antimesenteric border) is trapped and gangrenous. This is best described as:
   *A. Richter's hernia
    B. Littre's hernia
    C. Maydl's hernia
    D. Sliding hernia
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love 27e (Hernias)
   concept: In a Richter's hernia only part of the bowel-wall circumference is trapped, so it can strangulate and become gangrenous WITHOUT features of intestinal obstruction, a dangerous pitfall classically seen with femoral hernias. Littre's = Meckel's diverticulum in the sac; Maydl's = a W-shaped loop.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_001 [E] A hernia is best defined as which of the following?
   *A. An abnormal protrusion of a viscus, or part of a viscus, through a weakness or defect in the wall of the cavity that normally contains it
    B. A localised collection of pus within the layers of the abdominal wall musculature
    C. A saccular dilatation of an artery secondary to weakening of its wall
    D. A full-thickness outpouching of the bowel wall into its own lumen
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: A hernia is the abnormal protrusion of a viscus or part of a viscus through a weakness or defect in the wall of the cavity that contains it, most often the abdominal wall. Option B describes an abscess, C an aneurysm and D a diverticulum/intraluminal lesion, none of which is a hernia. Every hernia classically has a sac (peritoneum), coverings and contents.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_002 [E] An indirect inguinal hernia enters the inguinal canal through the deep inguinal ring. In relation to the inferior epigastric vessels, its neck lies:
    A. Medial to the inferior epigastric vessels, through Hesselbach's triangle
    B. Directly through the conjoint tendon, medial to the vessels
   *C. Lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, through the deep inguinal ring
    D. Below the inguinal ligament, medial to the femoral vein
   key=C ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: An indirect inguinal hernia follows the path of the processus vaginalis and enters the deep (internal) ring, which lies lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; hence its neck is lateral to them. A direct hernia bulges medial to the vessels through Hesselbach's triangle (options A and B), and option D describes a femoral hernia. This vessel relation is the key operative landmark separating the two inguinal types.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_003 [M] A direct inguinal hernia protrudes through Hesselbach's (inguinal) triangle. Which option correctly states its boundaries?
   *A. Medial: lateral border of rectus abdominis; lateral: inferior epigastric vessels; inferior (base): inguinal ligament
    B. Medial: inferior epigastric vessels; lateral: rectus abdominis; base: pectineal ligament
    C. Medial: inguinal ligament; lateral: lacunar ligament; base: pectineus muscle
    D. Medial: rectus abdominis; lateral: sartorius; base: iliac crest
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Hesselbach's triangle is bounded medially by the lateral border of rectus abdominis, laterally by the inferior epigastric vessels and inferiorly (its base) by the inguinal ligament; a direct inguinal hernia pushes through its floor (transversalis fascia). The remaining options transpose these structures or substitute femoral-canal and unrelated muscular boundaries, so they are incorrect.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_004 [E] On examination, a groin swelling is found to lie above and medial to the pubic tubercle. This location is most consistent with which lesion?
    A. Femoral hernia
    B. Obturator hernia
    C. Saphena varix
   *D. Inguinal hernia
   key=D ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: Relation to the pubic tubercle is the classic bedside discriminator: an inguinal hernia lies above and medial to the pubic tubercle, whereas a femoral hernia lies below and lateral to it. An obturator hernia presents deep in the thigh/pelvis and a saphena varix lies at the saphenofemoral junction with a cough thrill, so neither fits this landmark. This single sign guides the whole differential.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_005 [H] Assertion (A): Femoral hernias have a strong tendency to strangulate. Reason (R): The medial boundary of the femoral canal is the femoral vein. Select the most appropriate option.
    A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
   *C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Femoral hernias strangulate readily because the femoral ring/canal is bounded by rigid structures, notably the sharp lacunar (Gimbernat's) ligament medially, the inguinal ligament anteriorly and the pectineal (Cooper's) ligament posteriorly, giving a narrow unyielding neck. The assertion is therefore true, but the reason is false: the femoral vein is the LATERAL boundary, not the medial one. True assertion with a false reason gives option C.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_006 [M] Which statement correctly describes a sliding hernia (hernia-en-glissade)?
   *A. A retroperitoneal viscus (e.g. sigmoid colon, caecum or urinary bladder) forms part of the wall of the sac itself
    B. The sac characteristically contains a Meckel's diverticulum
    C. Only part of the circumference of the bowel wall is caught within the sac
    D. Two bowel loops lie in the sac while the connecting loop strangulates inside the abdomen
   key=A ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: In a sliding hernia a retroperitoneal viscus (sigmoid colon on the left, caecum on the right, or the urinary bladder) slides down to form part of the wall of the sac rather than merely lying inside it. Option B describes Littre's hernia, C describes Richter's hernia and D describes Maydl's hernia. Recognising a sliding hernia is important to avoid inadvertently opening or injuring the viscus during sac dissection.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_007 [H] In a Maydl's hernia (hernia-in-W), which loop of bowel is at greatest risk of strangulation?
    A. The proximal loop lying within the sac
    B. The distal loop lying within the sac
   *C. The intermediate connecting loop that lies back within the abdominal cavity
    D. No loop strangulates because the contents remain freely reducible
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Maydl's hernia contains two loops within the sac arranged in a W, with an intervening (intermediate) loop that lies back inside the abdomen; it is this hidden intra-abdominal connecting loop whose blood supply is compromised and which strangulates. The intra-sac loops may appear viable, so the danger is missing the gangrenous loop that has retracted into the peritoneal cavity, making C correct.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_008 [M] A Littre's hernia is characterised by the presence of which structure within the hernia sac?
    A. The vermiform appendix
    B. The urinary bladder
    C. Only part of the circumference of the bowel wall
   *D. A Meckel's diverticulum
   key=D ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: Littre's hernia is defined by a Meckel's diverticulum lying within the hernia sac. Option A (appendix) describes Amyand's hernia, B (bladder) points to a sliding hernia, and C (only part of the bowel circumference trapped) is Richter's hernia. A Littre's hernia may present with a localised abscess, fistula or diverticulitis rather than complete intestinal obstruction, which can delay diagnosis.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_009 [H] During open repair of a right inguinal hernia in a 40-year-old man, the surgeon finds the vermiform appendix lying within the hernia sac. This is best termed:
    A. Littre's hernia
    B. Richter's hernia
   *C. Amyand's hernia
    D. Maydl's hernia
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: The presence of the vermiform appendix (inflamed or not) within a hernia sac, usually a right inguinal hernia, is termed Amyand's hernia. Littre's hernia contains a Meckel's diverticulum, Richter's traps only part of the bowel circumference, and Maydl's is the W-shaped double-loop hernia. Management of an Amyand's hernia depends on the degree of appendiceal inflammation, ranging from simple reduction to appendicectomy.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_010 [M] A Spigelian hernia protrudes through a defect at which anatomical site?
    A. The linea alba between the xiphisternum and the umbilicus
    B. The inferior lumbar (Petit's) triangle
    C. The obturator canal
   *D. The spigelian fascia along the linea semilunaris, typically at or below the level of the arcuate line
   key=D ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: A Spigelian hernia herniates through the spigelian fascia along the linea semilunaris (the lateral border of the rectus sheath), characteristically at or just below the arcuate line where the posterior rectus sheath is deficient. It is typically interparietal, lying deep to the intact external oblique, which makes it hard to palpate. Options A, B and C describe epigastric, lumbar and obturator hernias respectively.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_011 [H] An emaciated elderly woman presents with small-bowel obstruction and pain radiating down the medial aspect of the thigh to the knee, which eases when she flexes the hip. Which hernia is most likely?
   *A. Obturator hernia, with the Howship-Romberg sign
    B. Spigelian hernia
    C. Femoral hernia
    D. Inferior lumbar hernia
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: An obturator hernia passes through the obturator canal and classically affects thin, elderly, multiparous women (the little old lady's hernia). It presents with small-bowel obstruction plus the Howship-Romberg sign, pain along the medial thigh to the knee from obturator nerve compression that is relieved by hip flexion and worsened by extension/abduction. Its deep location causes late diagnosis, giving high strangulation and mortality rates.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_012 [M] The inferior lumbar triangle, a site of Petit's lumbar hernia, is bounded by which of the following?
    A. Quadratus lumborum, internal oblique and the 12th rib
   *B. Latissimus dorsi, external oblique and the iliac crest
    C. Rectus abdominis, inguinal ligament and the inferior epigastric vessels
    D. Sacrospinalis, serratus posterior inferior and the 12th rib
   key=B ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: The inferior lumbar (Petit's) triangle is bounded by latissimus dorsi posteromedially, the external oblique anterolaterally and the iliac crest as its base; it is a site of lumbar hernia. Option A describes the superior lumbar (Grynfeltt-Lesshaft) triangle (12th rib, quadratus lumborum/internal oblique), which is actually the more common lumbar hernia site. Options C and D describe unrelated regions.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_013 [E] A 1-year-old child has a small, easily reducible umbilical hernia noted since birth and is otherwise well. What is the most appropriate advice to the parents?
    A. Urgent operation to prevent imminent strangulation
    B. Immediate mesh repair of the defect
   *C. Reassurance and observation, as most close spontaneously by about 2 years of age, with repair reserved for persistence beyond about 4-5 years, large defects or complications
    D. Defer any herniotomy until after puberty regardless of symptoms
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Paediatric umbilical hernias are common, usually reduce easily and close spontaneously in the great majority by about 2 years of age, so reassurance and observation are appropriate. Elective repair is reserved for persistence beyond roughly 4-5 years, a large or symptomatic defect, or complications. Strangulation is rare in children, so urgent surgery or immediate mesh repair is unwarranted, and mesh is avoided in small children.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_014 [M] A pantaloon (saddle-bag) hernia is characterised by:
    A. A sac containing both the testis and a loop of bowel
   *B. Coexisting direct and indirect sacs on the same side, straddling the inferior epigastric vessels
    C. A simultaneous femoral and inguinal sac on the same side
    D. Bilateral indirect inguinal hernias
   key=B ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: A pantaloon (saddle-bag or dual) hernia has coexisting direct and indirect sacs on the same side that straddle the inferior epigastric vessels like the two legs of a pair of trousers. It is not a testis-containing sac, a combined femoral-inguinal hernia, or bilateral disease. Both the medial (direct) and lateral (indirect) components must be identified and dealt with at operation to prevent recurrence.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_015 [M] A man with a long-standing reducible inguinal hernia presents with a sudden painful, tense, tender, irreducible swelling that has lost its cough impulse, together with vomiting and abdominal distension. The most appropriate management is:
    A. Apply a truss and discharge with clinic review
    B. List for elective repair in 6 weeks
    C. Attempt firm manual reduction under sedation and manage as an outpatient
   *D. Proceed to urgent (emergency) surgical exploration
   key=D ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Sudden pain, tenderness, tenseness, irreducibility with loss of cough impulse and features of obstruction (vomiting, distension) in a previously reducible hernia indicate strangulation, an ischaemic emergency requiring urgent operative exploration. A truss, elective delay or forced manual reduction is contraindicated: forced reduction risks perforation, peritonitis or reduction-en-masse (returning ischaemic bowel with its constricting neck into the abdomen).
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_016 [H] Assertion (A): A strangulated hernia is a surgical emergency. Reason (R): In strangulation the intestinal lumen is obstructed but the blood supply to the contents remains intact. Select the most appropriate option.
    A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
   *C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=C ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: A strangulated hernia is indeed a surgical emergency, so the assertion is true. The reason, however, is false: strangulation means the blood supply to the contents IS compromised, leading to ischaemia and gangrene. Luminal obstruction with an initially intact blood supply describes an OBSTRUCTED (not strangulated) hernia. A true assertion with a false reason gives option C.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_017 [M] The Lichtenstein repair for inguinal hernia is best described as:
    A. A pure-tissue, multilayered continuous suture repair using no prosthetic material
   *B. An open, tension-free repair that reinforces the posterior wall with a prosthetic (polypropylene) mesh
    C. A laparoscopic totally extraperitoneal mesh placement
    D. Approximation of the conjoint tendon to the inguinal ligament with interrupted non-absorbable sutures
   key=B ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: The Lichtenstein repair is the archetypal open tension-free hernioplasty, reinforcing the posterior inguinal wall with a flat prosthetic (usually polypropylene) mesh sutured over the defect. Option A describes the Shouldice repair, D the Bassini repair and C the laparoscopic TEP approach. The tension-free principle underlies its low recurrence rate compared with pure-tissue (sutured) repairs, making it a standard open technique.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_018 [M] Among the pure tissue (non-mesh) repairs of inguinal hernia, which technique is associated with the lowest recurrence rate?
   *A. Shouldice repair
    B. Bassini repair
    C. Halsted repair
    D. Marcy repair
   key=A ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: Among pure-tissue (non-mesh) repairs, the Shouldice repair, a multilayered continuous double-breasting of the transversalis fascia and conjoint structures, has the lowest recurrence rate (around 1% in specialist hands) and is superior to the Bassini, Halsted and Marcy repairs. Tension-free mesh techniques (Lichtenstein or laparoscopic) generally achieve even lower recurrence and are now more widely used in adults.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_019 [H] Assertion (A): In a totally extraperitoneal (TEP) repair the peritoneal cavity is routinely opened. Reason (R): A transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) repair begins by entering the peritoneal cavity before dissecting the preperitoneal space. Select the most appropriate option.
    A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
   *D. A is false but R is true
   key=D ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: In a TEP repair the peritoneal cavity is NOT opened: the mesh is placed entirely in the preperitoneal (extraperitoneal) space, so the assertion is false. The reason is true, because TAPP does begin by entering the peritoneal cavity and then reflecting the peritoneum to reach the preperitoneal plane. A false assertion with a true reason gives option D. TEP avoids intraperitoneal entry and its adhesion/visceral-injury risks.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_020 [E] A truss for an inguinal hernia is most appropriately indicated in which situation?
    A. A strangulated hernia awaiting theatre
   *B. A reducible hernia in a patient who is unfit for, or declines, surgery
    C. An irreducible femoral hernia
    D. As the definitive treatment of choice in all young, fit patients
   key=B ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: A truss is a mechanical appliance that holds a reducible hernia reduced; its main legitimate role is palliation in a patient who is unfit for or refuses surgery. It is contraindicated in irreducible, obstructed or strangulated hernias, where it may worsen the situation, and it is not the treatment of choice in fit patients, who should undergo operative repair. It must only be applied once the hernia is fully reduced.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_021 [H] A 3-year-old boy has an uncomplicated indirect inguinal hernia. Which operation is appropriate?
   *A. Herniotomy, that is high ligation and excision of the sac, alone
    B. Lichtenstein mesh repair
    C. Shouldice multilayered tissue repair
    D. McEvedy high approach with Cooper's ligament repair
   key=A ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — The Paediatric Surgery / Hernias
   concept: In children an inguinal hernia is almost always indirect and arises from a patent processus vaginalis, with a structurally normal canal and posterior wall. Therefore herniotomy, meaning high ligation and excision of the sac at the deep ring, alone is curative. Herniorrhaphy, prosthetic mesh (Lichtenstein/Shouldice) and femoral (McEvedy) techniques are unnecessary and inappropriate in this age group and are avoided.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_022 [M] A strangulated femoral hernia needs emergency surgery and is likely to require bowel resection. Which surgical approach gives the best access for this?
    A. Lockwood low (infra-inguinal) approach
    B. Lotheissen trans-inguinal approach
    C. Kocher's subcostal approach
   *D. McEvedy high (supra-inguinal / preperitoneal) approach
   key=D ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia
   concept: For a strangulated femoral hernia likely to need bowel resection, the McEvedy high (supra-inguinal/preperitoneal) approach is preferred because it gives excellent access to the sac neck and to the bowel for resection and anastomosis. The Lockwood low and Lotheissen trans-inguinal approaches are better suited to elective repair of an uncomplicated femoral hernia, and Kocher's incision is used for biliary surgery.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_023 [M] A soft, bluish, compressible groin swelling disappears completely on lying down, transmits a cough impulse with a palpable fluid thrill, and is associated with varicose veins in the same limb. The most likely diagnosis is:
    A. Femoral hernia
   *B. Saphena varix
    C. Psoas abscess
    D. Enlarged inguinal lymph node
   key=B ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall / Veins
   concept: A saphena varix is a saccular dilatation of the long saphenous vein at the saphenofemoral junction: a soft, bluish, compressible groin swelling that disappears instantly on lying down or leg elevation, transmits a cough impulse with a fluid thrill (Cruveilhier's sign) and is associated with varicose veins. A femoral hernia is firmer and less reducible, a lymph node is firm and discrete, and a psoas abscess tracks down from spinal disease.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_024 [M] Which nerve traverses the inguinal canal lying on top of the spermatic cord (outside the cord coverings) and is commonly injured during inguinal hernia repair?
    A. Iliohypogastric nerve
   *B. Ilioinguinal nerve
    C. Genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve
    D. Femoral branch of the genitofemoral nerve
   key=B ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Hernia / Surgical Anatomy of the Inguinal Region
   concept: The ilioinguinal nerve traverses the inguinal canal lying on top of the spermatic cord, outside the cord coverings, and exits through the superficial ring to supply the upper scrotum/labium and adjacent thigh; it is frequently injured or entrapped during hernia repair. The genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve runs within the cord (supplying cremaster), while the iliohypogastric and the femoral branch of the genitofemoral do not run on the cord in the canal.
Q_GEN_SURG_HERNIA_025 [H] Six months after an open inguinal hernia repair, a man reports persistent burning pain and paraesthesia in the groin radiating to the root of the scrotum and medial thigh, with a patch of numbness in the same area. The most likely cause is:
    A. Ischaemic orchitis
    B. Recurrence of the hernia
   *C. Injury or entrapment of the ilioinguinal nerve
    D. Mesh infection
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — Hernias, Umbilicus and Abdominal Wall
   concept: Persistent burning pain, paraesthesia and a patch of numbness in the groin radiating to the scrotal root and medial thigh after inguinal hernia repair typically reflect ilioinguinal nerve injury or entrapment (in scar, suture or mesh), the commonest cause of chronic post-herniorrhaphy (inguinodynia) groin pain. === Surgery / Thyroid swellings & surgery
Q_GEN_SURG_THYROID_001 [M] During thyroidectomy the recurrent laryngeal nerve must be identified. Which statement about its surgical course is correct?
    A. It runs on the anterior surface of the thyroid lobe and is unrelated to the inferior thyroid artery
    B. It descends into the larynx alongside the superior thyroid artery at the upper pole
   *C. It ascends in the tracheo-oesophageal groove, has a variable relation to the inferior thyroid artery, and is most vulnerable near the ligament of Berry
    D. It always passes superficial (anterior) to every branch of the inferior thyroid artery
   key=C ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — The Thyroid Gland
   concept: The recurrent laryngeal nerve ascends in the tracheo-oesophageal groove and its relation to the inferior thyroid artery is variable — it may pass anterior to, posterior to, or between the branches — so the artery cannot be used as an infallible guide. It is most at risk close to the ligament of Berry, where it lies intimately applied to the gland just before entering the larynx behind the cricothyroid joint. Options A, B and D state fixed or false relations and are wrong.
Q_GEN_SURG_THYROID_002 [M] To protect the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve while mobilising the upper pole of the thyroid, the surgeon should:
   *A. Ligate the branches of the superior thyroid artery individually, close to (on) the upper pole of the gland
    B. Mass-ligate the superior thyroid pedicle high up, well away from the gland
    C. Ligate the inferior thyroid artery in continuity on the carotid sheath
    D. Divide the middle thyroid vein first and avoid touching the upper pole
   key=A ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Thyroid Gland
   concept: The external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve runs with the superior thyroid artery towards the cricothyroid muscle and lies close to the vessels near the upper pole. Ligating the arterial branches individually and low, right on the gland capsule, keeps the tie away from the nerve; a high mass ligature (option B) risks catching the nerve. The inferior thyroid artery (option C) relates to the recurrent laryngeal nerve, not this one, and option D ignores the pole altogether.
Q_GEN_SURG_THYROID_003 [E] The thyroid gland develops embryologically as a median endodermal downgrowth. From which site does it originate?
    A. From the third pharyngeal pouch, which also forms the inferior parathyroid glands
   *B. From the foramen cecum at the junction of the anterior two-thirds and posterior one-third of the tongue, descending via the thyroglossal duct
    C. From the ultimobranchial body derived from the fourth pharyngeal pouch
    D. From the second pharyngeal arch mesoderm anterior to the hyoid bone
   key=B ref=Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery — The Thyroid Gland
   concept: The median thyroid anlage arises as a diverticulum from the foramen cecum, located at the junction of the anterior two-thirds and posterior one-third of the tongue, then descends in front of the hyoid and larynx along the thyroglossal duct, which normally atrophies. Failure of descent gives a lingual thyroid and a persistent tract gives a thyroglossal cyst; the pyramidal lobe is its remnant. The parafollicular C-cells (option C, ultimobranchial body) and the parathyroids (option A) have separate origins.
Q_GEN_SURG_THYROID_004 [M] A 12-year-old has a smooth, midline anterior neck swelling that moves upward on protrusion of the tongue and on swallowing. Which operation prevents recurrence?
    A. Simple excision of the cyst alone through a transverse incision
    B. Hemithyroidectomy with excision of the pyramidal lobe
    C. Marsupialisation of the cyst and packing
   *D. Sistrunk operation — excision of the cyst, the whole tract, and the central (body) portion of the hyoid bone
   key=D ref=SRB's Manual of Surgery — Thyroglossal Cyst
   concept: A midline swelling that rises with tongue protrusion is a thyroglossal cyst, because the tract is attached to the foramen cecum through the hyoid bone. Simple excision (option A) leaves the tract behind and recurs. The Sistrunk operation removes the cyst, the entire tract up to the foramen cecum and the central portion of the hyoid bone, which the tract passes behind, giving the lowest recurrence rate. Marsupialisation and hemithyroidectomy are inappropriate for this lesion.
