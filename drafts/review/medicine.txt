Q_c1_080 [H] A patient has arterial pH 7.28, HCO3 12 mEq/L and a calculated anion gap of 24 (a HIGH anion-gap metabolic acidosis). Which of the following is a recognized cause?

   *B. Diabetic ketoacidosis
    C. Type II (proximal) renal tubular acidosis
    D. Excessive normal-saline infusion
   key=B ref=Harrison's 21e, Acid-Base Disorders

=== Medicine / Diabetes mellitus: diagnosis, complications, mgmt
Q_c1_075 [M] A 19-year-old type-1 diabetic presents with vomiting, deep rapid breathing, fruity breath, glucose 480 mg/dL, ketonuria and pH 7.1. After starting IV fluids and insulin, which electrolyte must be closely monitored and replaced to prevent a fatal arrhythmia?
    A. Calcium
   *B. Potassium
    C. Magnesium
    D. Phosphate
   key=B ref=Harrison's 21e, Diabetic Ketoacidosis
   concept: In diabetic ketoacidosis, insulin drives potassium into cells and can precipitate dangerous hypokalemia. Potassium must be monitored and replaced (insulin is withheld if K+ is below 3.3 mEq/L) to prevent cardiac arrhythmias.
Q_c1_076 [M] The earliest clinical indicator of diabetic nephropathy, which warrants starting an ACE inhibitor or ARB for renoprotection, is?
    A. Overt proteinuria (more than 3.5 g/day)
   *B. Microalbuminuria
    C. A raised serum creatinine
    D. Oliguria
   key=B ref=Harrison's 21e, Diabetic Nephropathy
   concept: Microalbuminuria (30-300 mg/day albumin) is the earliest sign of diabetic nephropathy. ACE inhibitors or ARBs reduce intraglomerular pressure and slow progression, and are started at this stage.

=== Medicine / Pulmonary TB: diagnosis & regimens
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_001 [E] Mycobacterium tuberculosis retains carbol fuchsin despite decolourization with acid-alcohol during Ziehl-Neelsen staining. This acid-fast property is chiefly attributable to which component of its cell wall?
    A. Peptidoglycan cross-linked with lysine
    B. Lipoteichoic acid
   *C. Mycolic acids
    D. Lipopolysaccharide (endotoxin)
   key=C ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Tuberculosis (microbiology); Ananthanarayan & Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology — Mycobacterium
   concept: Acid-fastness results from the thick, waxy mycolic acid layer of the mycobacterial cell wall, which resists decolourization by acid-alcohol so bacilli retain carbol fuchsin and appear red against a methylene-blue counterstain. Peptidoglycan and lipoteichoic acid are Gram-positive wall features; LPS/endotoxin is Gram-negative. This lipid-rich wall also underlies slow growth and disinfectant resistance.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_002 [M] A sputum sample is sent for mycobacterial culture. Compared with solid Löwenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium, the automated liquid MGIT (Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube) system chiefly offers which advantage?
    A. It detects rifampicin resistance directly without drug-susceptibility testing
   *B. It yields a positive result faster, typically within 1-2 weeks
    C. It is more specific because contamination never occurs
    D. It does not require a biosafety level 3 laboratory
   key=B ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Laboratory diagnosis; WHO liquid culture policy
   concept: Liquid MGIT culture detects growth (via fluorescence from oxygen consumption) in roughly 1-2 weeks versus 2-8 weeks for solid LJ medium, and is more sensitive, so WHO/NTEP prefer liquid culture where feasible. However, liquid systems have higher contamination rates, still require BSL-3 containment, and culture alone does not identify drug resistance — that needs phenotypic DST or molecular tests. LJ remains cheaper and useful for colony morphology.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_003 [H] Sputum smear microscopy is far less sensitive than culture for diagnosing pulmonary tuberculosis. Approximately what minimum bacillary load per millilitre of sputum is generally required for a smear to be reliably positive?
   *A. About 10,000 bacilli/mL
    B. About 10 bacilli/mL
    C. About 1 bacillus/mL
    D. About 100 bacilli/mL
   key=A ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Tuberculosis (diagnosis); API Textbook of Medicine
   concept: Smear microscopy (Ziehl-Neelsen or fluorescent) needs roughly 5,000-10,000 acid-fast bacilli per mL of sputum to be consistently positive, which is why smear-negative but culture-positive or CBNAAT-positive disease is common. Culture can detect as few as 10-100 viable bacilli/mL, and CBNAAT around 130 bacilli/mL, making both far more sensitive. This threshold explains the value of molecular testing in paucibacillary and HIV-associated TB.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_004 [M] A 40-year-old man with 4 weeks of cough submits sputum for CBNAAT (GeneXpert MTB/RIF). Besides confirming the presence of Mycobacterium tuberculosis, this single assay simultaneously detects resistance to which drug?
    A. Isoniazid
    B. Ethambutol
   *C. Rifampicin
    D. Pyrazinamide
   key=C ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Molecular diagnostics; WHO Xpert MTB/RIF policy
   concept: GeneXpert MTB/RIF (CBNAAT) is a cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test that detects M. tuberculosis DNA and rpoB gene mutations conferring rifampicin resistance, giving results in about 2 hours. It does NOT detect isoniazid, ethambutol or pyrazinamide resistance — those need line probe assay or phenotypic DST. Rifampicin resistance serves as a surrogate marker for likely MDR-TB and triggers further DST under NTEP's Universal DST policy.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_005 [M] Under NTEP, the first-line Line Probe Assay (LPA) is used on smear-positive specimens or cultures. It rapidly detects resistance to which combination of drugs?
    A. Fluoroquinolones and second-line injectables only
    B. Rifampicin and ethambutol
    C. Isoniazid and pyrazinamide
   *D. Rifampicin and isoniazid
   key=D ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Line Probe Assay; WHO LPA policy update
   concept: First-line LPA (GenoType MTBDRplus) detects rifampicin resistance (rpoB) and isoniazid resistance (katG for high-level, inhA for low-level) within 1-2 days. Second-line LPA (MTBDRsl) detects fluoroquinolone and second-line injectable resistance. LPA thus adds isoniazid resistance information that CBNAAT (rifampicin only) cannot provide, helping distinguish isoniazid mono/poly-resistant TB from MDR-TB and guide regimen selection.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_006 [M] A tuberculin skin test (Mantoux) is performed with 5 TU of PPD injected intradermally. Which statement about its correct interpretation is TRUE?
    A. Erythema diameter is measured at 24 hours
   *B. Induration is measured transversely at 48-72 hours
    C. A positive test confirms active tuberculosis disease
    D. Prior BCG vaccination never affects the result
   key=B ref=NTEP guidelines; Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Tuberculosis (TST)
   concept: The Mantoux test is read at 48-72 hours by measuring the transverse diameter of INDURATION (not erythema). It indicates infection/immune sensitisation, not active disease, and may be positive from prior BCG or non-tuberculous mycobacteria (false positive) or falsely negative in HIV, miliary TB, malnutrition or immunosuppression. Cutoffs vary by risk group (>=5 mm in HIV/close contacts, >=10 mm in others in India). Verify programme cutoffs against current NTEP guidance.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_007 [M] Choose the correct option. Assertion (A): Interferon-gamma release assays (IGRAs) are preferred over the tuberculin skin test for detecting TB infection in BCG-vaccinated individuals. Reason (R): IGRAs use the antigens ESAT-6 and CFP-10, which are absent from the BCG vaccine strain and from most non-tuberculous mycobacteria.
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true, but R is false
    D. A is false, but R is true
   key=A ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Tuberculosis (IGRA); CDC/WHO IGRA guidance
   concept: IGRAs (QuantiFERON-TB Gold, T-SPOT.TB) measure interferon-gamma release from T-cells stimulated by M. tuberculosis-specific antigens ESAT-6 and CFP-10, encoded in the RD1 region deleted from BCG and absent from most NTM. Hence IGRAs are not confounded by prior BCG vaccination, unlike the TST, so R correctly explains A. Like the TST, however, IGRA cannot distinguish latent infection from active disease.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_008 [M] A paediatric chest radiograph shows a peripheral subpleural parenchymal focus in the right lower zone together with enlarged ipsilateral hilar lymph nodes. This combination is best described as:
    A. A cavitary lesion of the right upper lobe (post-primary TB)
    B. Miliary mottling throughout both lungs
   *C. The Ghon complex (primary TB)
    D. A tuberculoma with rim calcification
   key=C ref=Davidson's Principles and Practice of Medicine — Tuberculosis; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: Primary TB typically produces a peripheral parenchymal Ghon focus (often mid/lower zones) plus draining hilar/mediastinal lymphadenopathy — together the Ghon (primary) complex; when calcified it becomes the Ranke complex. Post-primary (reactivation) TB classically favours the apical/posterior segments of upper lobes with cavitation. Miliary TB shows diffuse millet-seed nodules. Recognising primary versus post-primary patterns aids age-appropriate diagnosis in children.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_009 [M] Under the NTEP daily fixed-dose combination regimen for a new case of drug-sensitive pulmonary TB, the continuation phase consists of which drugs and duration?
    A. Isoniazid + rifampicin for 2 months

    D. Isoniazid + rifampicin for 7 months
   key=B ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Treatment of drug-sensitive TB
   concept: In the NTEP daily regimen, new drug-sensitive pulmonary TB is treated with a 2-month intensive phase of HRZE followed by a 4-month continuation phase of HRE — ethambutol is retained through the continuation phase (unlike older thrice-weekly regimens that used only HR). Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_010 [M] A 60-kg adult with newly diagnosed drug-sensitive pulmonary TB is started on the NTEP daily FDC regimen. In the intensive phase, how many adult four-drug FDC tablets (HRZE) should he receive each day?
    A. 2 tablets
    B. 3 tablets
   *C. 4 tablets
    D. 5 tablets
   key=C ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Weight-band dosing
   concept: NTEP daily FDC dosing is weight-band based: 25-39 kg = 2 tablets, 40-54 kg = 3, 55-69 kg = 4, and >=70 kg = 5 tablets daily. A 60-kg patient falls in the 55-69 kg band and receives 4 adult HRZE tablets daily in the intensive phase (and 4 HRE tablets in the continuation phase). Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_011 [M] Under NTEP outcome definitions, a bacteriologically confirmed pulmonary TB patient who completes treatment and is documented smear- or culture-negative at the end of treatment (and on at least one earlier occasion) is classified as:
   *A. Cured

   key=A ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Treatment outcome definitions
   concept: 'Cured' applies to a bacteriologically confirmed patient who is smear/culture-negative at the end of treatment AND on at least one previous occasion. Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_012 [E] A 30-year-old malnourished man on antitubercular therapy develops numbness and burning in both feet. Which drug is responsible, and which agent prevents this adverse effect?
    A. Ethambutol; prevented by zinc
    B. Rifampicin; prevented by folic acid
    C. Pyrazinamide; prevented by allopurinol
   *D. Isoniazid; prevented by pyridoxine
   key=D ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Antituberculous drugs; KD Tripathi Pharmacology
   concept: Isoniazid causes dose-dependent peripheral neuropathy by interfering with pyridoxine (vitamin B6) metabolism and increasing its excretion; risk is higher in malnutrition, alcoholism, diabetes, pregnancy, HIV and renal failure. Prophylactic pyridoxine (10 mg/day, more for treatment) prevents and treats it. Ethambutol causes optic neuritis, rifampicin causes orange secretions/hepatotoxicity, and pyrazinamide causes hyperuricemia — none is the cause here.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_013 [E] Choose the correct option. Assertion (A): Orange-red discolouration of urine in a patient on rifampicin indicates hepatotoxicity and mandates immediately stopping the drug. Reason (R): Rifampicin can cause drug-induced hepatitis.
    A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true, but R is false
   *D. A is false, but R is true
   key=D ref=KD Tripathi Pharmacology — Antitubercular drugs; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: Orange-red discolouration of urine, sweat, tears and other secretions is a harmless, expected effect of rifampicin (it may permanently stain soft contact lenses) and is NOT a sign of hepatotoxicity, so the drug should be continued with reassurance — the Assertion is false. The Reason is true: rifampicin genuinely can cause drug-induced hepatitis, which is detected by symptoms and rising transaminases/bilirubin, not by urine colour.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_014 [M] Choose the correct option. Assertion (A): Pyrazinamide can precipitate arthralgia and acute gout in patients on antitubercular therapy. Reason (R): Pyrazinamide is a bactericidal drug that is most active against intracellular bacilli in an acidic environment.
    A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
   *B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true, but R is false
    D. A is false, but R is true
   key=B ref=KD Tripathi Pharmacology — Pyrazinamide; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: Both statements are individually true, but the Reason does not explain the Assertion. Pyrazinamide causes hyperuricemia because its metabolite pyrazinoic acid inhibits renal tubular secretion of uric acid, precipitating arthralgia/gout — this is unrelated to its separate, true property of being bactericidal against intracellular bacilli in acidic (phagolysosomal) conditions. Hence the answer is 'both true, R not the correct explanation'.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_015 [E] Streptomycin, an aminoglycoside occasionally used in TB treatment, most characteristically causes which pair of adverse effects?
    A. Optic neuritis and red-green colour blindness
    B. Peripheral neuropathy and seizures
   *C. Ototoxicity (eighth cranial nerve) and nephrotoxicity
    D. Hyperuricemia and photosensitivity
   key=C ref=KD Tripathi Pharmacology — Streptomycin; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: Streptomycin damages the vestibulocochlear (eighth cranial) nerve causing vestibular disturbance and hearing loss, and is nephrotoxic — classic aminoglycoside toxicities that are largely dose- and age-related and often partly irreversible. It is contraindicated in pregnancy (fetal ototoxicity). Optic neuritis is ethambutol, neuropathy is isoniazid, and hyperuricemia is pyrazinamide — matching those toxicities to the wrong drug is a common exam trap.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_016 [H] WHO (2022) and NTEP now recommend a 6-month oral regimen for eligible rifampicin-resistant/MDR-TB known by the acronym BPaLM. Which four drugs does BPaLM comprise?
    A. Bedaquiline, pyrazinamide, levofloxacin, meropenem
    B. Bedaquiline, para-aminosalicylic acid, linezolid, moxifloxacin
    C. Bedaquiline, prothionamide, levofloxacin, minocycline
   *D. Bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid, moxifloxacin
   key=D ref=WHO Consolidated Guidelines on Drug-Resistant TB Treatment 2022; NTEP DR-TB guidelines
   concept: BPaLM = Bedaquiline + Pretomanid + Linezolid + Moxifloxacin, a WHO-recommended (2022) all-oral 6-month regimen for RR/MDR-TB, replacing longer injectable-containing regimens for eligible patients; where fluoroquinolone resistance is confirmed the 'M' is dropped (BPaL). Key toxicities include linezolid myelosuppression/neuropathy and bedaquiline/moxifloxacin QT prolongation. DR-TB regimens change frequently — verify eligibility and composition against current NTEP/WHO guidance.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_017 [H] According to the revised WHO 2021 definitions, extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (XDR-TB) is MDR/RR-TB with additional resistance to:
    A. Any second-line injectable agent only

Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_018 [H] A newly diagnosed HIV-positive patient (CD4 count 40 cells/µL) is found to have pulmonary TB. Regarding sequencing of therapy, the recommended approach is:
   *A. Start antitubercular treatment first, then initiate ART as early as within 2 weeks
    B. Start ART first and defer antitubercular treatment until viral suppression
    C. Start both simultaneously on day 1 in every patient regardless of CD4
    D. Withhold ART entirely until antitubercular treatment is completed
   key=A ref=NACO/NTEP TB-HIV guidelines; WHO HIV-associated TB guidelines; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: In TB-HIV coinfection, antitubercular treatment is started first and ART is added early: with CD4 <50 cells/µL, ART should begin within 2 weeks of starting ATT; with higher CD4, within 2-8 weeks. Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_019 [M] A household contact is offered TB preventive treatment (TPT) after active TB is excluded. The '3HP' regimen used in expanded NTEP TPT refers to:

Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_020 [H] A 25-year-old with weeks of fever, weight loss and dyspnoea has a chest radiograph showing innumerable uniform 1-3 mm nodules evenly distributed throughout both lung fields; fundoscopy may reveal choroidal tubercles. The most likely diagnosis is:
    A. Lobar bacterial pneumonia
    B. Post-primary cavitary tuberculosis
    C. Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis
   *D. Miliary tuberculosis
   key=D ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Miliary TB; API Textbook of Medicine
   concept: Miliary TB results from haematogenous dissemination of bacilli, producing myriad uniform millet-sized (1-3 mm) nodules diffusely across both lungs; choroidal tubercles on fundoscopy are highly suggestive. It is often smear-negative (paucibacillary), so diagnosis relies on imaging, biopsy/culture and clinical context, and it can be complicated by TB meningitis. Cavitary upper-lobe disease and lobar pneumonia produce quite different radiographic patterns.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_021 [M] A pregnant woman is diagnosed with drug-sensitive pulmonary TB. Which antitubercular drug is contraindicated in pregnancy owing to fetal ototoxicity?
   *A. Streptomycin
    B. Isoniazid
    C. Rifampicin
    D. Ethambutol
   key=A ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — TB in pregnancy; NTEP guidelines
   concept: Streptomycin (and other aminoglycosides) crosses the placenta and can cause irreversible fetal eighth-nerve damage (congenital deafness), so it is contraindicated in pregnancy. The standard first-line oral drugs — isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol — are considered safe, and TB should be treated promptly in pregnancy. Pyridoxine is co-administered with isoniazid. Untreated TB poses greater risk to mother and fetus than the first-line drugs.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_022 [H] A new smear-positive pulmonary TB patient is on the NTEP 6-month daily regimen. Under standard monitoring, the FIRST scheduled follow-up sputum smear examination is performed at:
    A. The end of the 1st month

    D. Only at the end of treatment (6th month)
   key=B ref=NTEP Technical and Operational Guidelines for TB Control in India 2023 — Treatment monitoring
   concept: Follow-up sputum smears assess response: the first is done at the end of the intensive phase (end of month 2). Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_023 [H] A patient on HRZE develops nausea and jaundice; serum ALT is 6 times the upper limit of normal. The most appropriate immediate step is to:
    A. Continue all drugs and add a hepatoprotective agent
    B. Stop only ethambutol and continue HRZ
    C. Halve the dose of all four drugs
   *D. Stop all hepatotoxic antitubercular drugs and monitor liver function
   key=D ref=ATS/IDSA hepatotoxicity statement; NTEP guidelines; Harrison's 21st ed
   concept: Antitubercular drug-induced hepatitis warrants stopping the hepatotoxic drugs (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide) when ALT is >=3x ULN with symptoms/jaundice or >=5x ULN without symptoms. Ethambutol is not hepatotoxic. Once transaminases and bilirubin normalise and symptoms resolve, drugs are reintroduced sequentially (rechallenge) under monitoring; a non-hepatotoxic bridging regimen may be used if treatment cannot be interrupted. Continuing or merely reducing doses risks fulminant hepatic failure.
Q_GEN_MED_RESP_TB_024 [H] In the DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course) strategy, the phrase 'directly observed treatment' specifically means that:

DOTS has five pillars: political/administrative commitment, diagnosis by quality-assured bacteriology, standardized supervised treatment with patient support, uninterrupted drug supply, and standardized recording/reporting with outcome monitoring.

Q_c1_102 [M] VIDEO-DESCRIBED: A ward video shows a patient who suddenly cries out and becomes rigid with all four limbs extended (tonic phase) for about 20 seconds, then develops rhythmic jerking of all limbs (clonic phase) with frothing at the mouth and tongue-biting, followed by a period of confusion and drowsiness. This semiology is most consistent with?
   *A. Generalized tonic-clonic (grand mal) seizure
    B. Absence (petit mal) seizure
    C. Focal aware seizure
    D. Psychogenic non-epileptic (pseudo) seizure
   key=A ref=Harrison's 21e, Seizures and Epilepsy
