
=== Ophthalmology / Cataract
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_001 [E] In the context of global blindness, cataract is best described as which of the following?
    A. The leading cause of irreversible childhood blindness
    B. A cause of blindness for which no surgical remedy currently exists
   *C. The commonest cause of treatable (reversible) blindness worldwide
    D. A refractive error correctable with spectacles alone
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Cataract)
   concept: Cataract is the leading cause of blindness worldwide and, because sight is restored by surgery, it is the commonest cause of treatable or reversible blindness (C). It is not irreversible (A), it has an excellent surgical remedy (B), and it is a media opacity rather than a refractive error correctable by spectacles (D). Recognising cataract as reversible is the rationale behind large-scale cataract-surgery blindness-control programmes.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_002 [E] Regarding the crystalline lens capsule, which statement is correct?
    A. It is thickest at the posterior pole and thinnest at the equator
   *B. It is the thickest basement membrane in the body and is thinnest at the central posterior pole
    C. It is composed mainly of type I collagen secreted by the iris
    D. It is a vascular membrane supplied by the long posterior ciliary arteries
   key=B ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Anatomy and Physiology (The Crystalline Lens)
   concept: The lens capsule is a true basement membrane (predominantly type IV collagen) secreted by the lens epithelium and is the thickest basement membrane in the human body; it is thickest in the pre-equatorial zones and thinnest at the central posterior pole, which explains why the posterior capsule is so fragile during surgery (B). It is not iris-derived type I collagen (C) and is avascular (D); option A reverses the true thickness pattern.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_003 [E] Assertion (A): The lens depends on the aqueous humour to meet its metabolic needs. Reason (R): The lens is avascular and devoid of any blood or nerve supply. Choose the correct option.
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true, but R is false
    D. A is false, but R is true
   key=A ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Anatomy and Physiology (The Crystalline Lens)
   concept: The lens is avascular and has no nerve supply; it therefore depends entirely on the aqueous humour (and, posteriorly, on the vitreous) for glucose, oxygen and metabolic exchange, and it discharges waste into the aqueous. Because this avascularity is precisely the reason the lens must rely on the aqueous, both statements are true and the reason correctly explains the assertion (A).
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_004 [E] The crystalline lens meets the bulk of its energy (ATP) requirement through which metabolic pathway?
    A. Aerobic oxidative phosphorylation in the lens nucleus
   *B. Anaerobic glycolysis
    C. Beta-oxidation of fatty acids
    D. The pentose phosphate (HMP) shunt as the principal ATP source
   key=B ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Anatomy and Physiology (Biochemistry of the Lens)
   concept: The lens is metabolically active yet relatively hypoxic, so roughly 80-85% of its glucose is metabolised by anaerobic glycolysis to generate ATP (B). Aerobic oxidative phosphorylation (A) is limited and confined mainly to the epithelium; the HMP/pentose-phosphate shunt (D) chiefly supplies NADPH for the antioxidant glutathione system rather than bulk ATP; and fatty-acid beta-oxidation (C) is not an operative lens pathway.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_005 [E] Eighteen months after uncomplicated extracapsular cataract surgery with a posterior chamber IOL, a patient reports gradual painless dimming of vision; slit-lamp shows a wrinkled, pearl-studded membrane behind the IOL. The definitive treatment is:
    A. Intraocular lens exchange
    B. Topical corticosteroids for six weeks
   *C. Nd:YAG laser posterior capsulotomy
    D. Intravitreal anti-VEGF injection
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (After-cataract)
   concept: A wrinkled, pearly, opacified posterior capsule months to years after extracapsular/phaco surgery is posterior capsular opacification (after-cataract) caused by proliferation of residual lens epithelial cells (Elschnig pearls and Soemmering ring). The definitive, quick, non-incisional treatment is Nd:YAG laser posterior capsulotomy, which clears the visual axis (C). IOL exchange (A), steroids (B) and anti-VEGF (D) do not clear a fibrosed opaque capsule.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_006 [M] An elderly man who has worn reading glasses for years is now able to read newsprint without them, although his distance vision has become blurred; the lens nucleus is brunescent. This 'second sight' phenomenon is due to:
    A. A hypermetropic shift from cortical spoke opacities
   *B. Index myopia from an increasing refractive index of the sclerosed nucleus
    C. Accommodative spasm of the ciliary muscle
    D. Astigmatism induced by a subluxated lens
   key=B ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye — The Crystalline Lens (Senile cataract)
   concept: Progressive nuclear sclerosis raises the refractive index of the lens nucleus, producing an acquired index myopia; this myopic shift lets a previously presbyopic or hypermetropic patient read again without glasses, the classic 'second sight', while distance vision blurs (B). It is not a hypermetropic shift (A), not ciliary accommodative spasm (C), and not astigmatism from lens subluxation (D).
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_007 [M] A patient complains of seeing several overlapping images of a single object with one eye; the extra images persist when the fellow eye is covered. The most likely explanation is:
   *A. Monocular polyopia due to irregular refraction by an early lens opacity
    B. Binocular diplopia from a sixth cranial nerve palsy
    C. Retinal detachment involving the macula
    D. Homonymous hemianopia from an occipital lobe lesion
   key=A ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Symptoms of cataract)
   concept: Multiple or overlapping images arising from a single eye that persist when the other eye is occluded constitute monocular diplopia/polyopia, caused by irregular refraction across zones of differing refractive index within an early cataract (A). Binocular diplopia (B) disappears on covering one eye; retinal detachment (C) and homonymous hemianopia (D) cause field or visual loss, not overlapping duplicate images.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_008 [M] Wedge-shaped (cuneiform) opacities radiating like spokes from the periphery toward the centre, with intervening water clefts and vacuoles, are characteristic of which morphological type of senile cataract?
    A. Nuclear sclerotic cataract
    B. Posterior subcapsular cataract
   *C. Cortical cataract
    D. Anterior polar cataract
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Morphological classification of cataract)
   concept: Cortical senile cataract begins as wedge-shaped (cuneiform) opacities and spokes extending from the periphery toward the centre, with water clefts and vacuoles forming between hydrated lens fibres (C). Nuclear cataract (A) is a central, brunescent opacity; posterior subcapsular cataract (B) is a plaque lying just in front of the posterior capsule; and anterior polar cataract (D) is a small central capsular opacity.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_009 [M] Regarding the iris-shadow (shadow) test performed with an obliquely thrown torchlight to help stage a senile cataract, which statement is correct?
    A. A well-formed iris shadow indicates a mature cataract
    B. Absence of an iris shadow is typical of an incipient cataract
    C. The test primarily assesses the depth of the posterior capsule
   *D. A crescentic iris shadow on the lens indicates an immature cataract, whereas it is absent in a mature cataract
   key=D ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye — The Crystalline Lens (Stages and examination of senile cataract)
   concept: In the oblique torch (shadow) test, an immature cataract still has clear cortex between the pupillary margin and the opacity, so a crescentic iris shadow falls on the greyish lens; in a mature cataract the opacity reaches the anterior capsule and no shadow is cast (D). Thus a present shadow means immature (not mature, A), absence is typical of mature (not incipient, B), and the test does not gauge posterior capsule depth (C).
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_010 [M] Assertion (A): In a Morgagnian cataract the liquefied cortex allows the dense nucleus to sink inferiorly within the capsular bag. Reason (R): The lens capsule is the thickest basement membrane in the body. Choose the correct option.
    A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
   *B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true, but R is false
    D. A is false, but R is true
   key=B ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Hypermature cataract)
   concept: A Morgagnian cataract is a hypermature cataract in which the cortex liquefies to milky fluid and the heavy nucleus sinks to the bottom of the capsular bag, so the assertion is true; the lens capsule is indeed the thickest basement membrane in the body, so the reason is also true. However, capsular thickness has nothing to do with why the nucleus sinks, so the reason does not explain the assertion (B).
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_011 [M] A 3-month-old infant with failure to thrive, hepatomegaly and bilateral central 'oil-droplet' lens opacities is investigated. The most likely underlying enzyme deficiency is:
    A. Alpha-galactosidase A
    B. Branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase
   *C. Galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase
    D. Phenylalanine hydroxylase
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Congenital and developmental cataract)
   concept: An oil-droplet cataract in an infant with hepatomegaly and failure to thrive points to classic galactosaemia caused by deficiency of galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase (GALT), leading to galactitol accumulation and osmotic lens changes that are reversible if a galactose-free diet is started early (C). Alpha-galactosidase A (A) causes Fabry disease, BCKAD (B) maple syrup urine disease, and phenylalanine hydroxylase (D) phenylketonuria; none produce the classic oil-droplet cataract.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_012 [M] A neonate born to a mother who had a febrile rash with lymphadenopathy in the first trimester has bilateral pearly-white nuclear cataracts, a continuous machinery murmur and fails the newborn hearing screen. The most likely aetiology of the cataract is:
   *A. Congenital rubella syndrome
    B. Congenital toxoplasmosis
    C. Lowe (oculocerebrorenal) syndrome
    D. Birth trauma
   key=A ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye — The Crystalline Lens (Congenital cataract)
   concept: First-trimester maternal rubella causes congenital rubella syndrome, whose classic triad is cataract (a pearly-white nuclear opacity), sensorineural deafness and cardiac defects such as a patent ductus arteriosus (machinery murmur), often with a salt-and-pepper retinopathy (A). Toxoplasmosis (B) typically causes chorioretinitis, Lowe syndrome (C) adds glaucoma and renal disease, and birth trauma (D) would be unilateral and acquired.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_013 [M] A neonate is found to have a dense unilateral congenital cataract that obscures the fundus. Which management principle is most appropriate?
    A. Defer surgery until 2 years of age to allow the eye to grow
    B. Manage conservatively with atropine and spectacles indefinitely
    C. Surgery is unnecessary because congenital cataracts rarely affect vision
   *D. Operate early (ideally within about the first 6 weeks), then provide optical correction and vigorous amblyopia therapy
   key=D ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Management of congenital cataract)
   concept: A dense unilateral congenital cataract deprives the developing visual cortex of form vision and rapidly causes irreversible stimulus-deprivation amblyopia, so during this critical period the lens should be removed early (about the first 6 weeks), followed by prompt aphakic optical correction (contact lens or IOL) and aggressive amblyopia therapy (D). Deferring (A), conservative atropine/spectacles (B) or withholding surgery (C) all risk permanent amblyopia.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_014 [M] Two weeks after being struck in the eye by a cricket ball, a young man has a stellate, feathery posterior subcapsular opacity, and a ring of iris pigment is imprinted on the anterior lens capsule. This pigment ring is termed:
    A. Kayser-Fleischer ring
    B. Fleischer ring
    C. Soemmering ring
   *D. Vossius ring
   key=D ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Traumatic cataract)
   concept: Blunt ocular trauma classically produces a rosette-shaped (stellate, feathery) subcapsular cataract, and imprinting of the pupillary-margin iris pigment onto the anterior lens capsule produces a circular pigment ring called the Vossius ring (D). The Kayser-Fleischer ring (A) is corneal copper in Wilson disease, the Fleischer ring (B) encircles a keratoconus cone, and the Soemmering ring (C) is residual equatorial lens matter after capsule rupture.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_015 [M] Before cataract surgery the surgeon calculates intraocular lens power. The two principal biometric measurements required for standard IOL power formulae are:
   *A. Axial length of the globe and corneal curvature (keratometry)
    B. Intraocular pressure and central corneal thickness
    C. Anterior chamber cell grade and pupil diameter
    D. Colour vision score and contrast sensitivity
   key=A ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Biometry and IOL power calculation)
   concept: Standard IOL power formulae (such as SRK and its successors) require the eye's axial length, measured by A-scan ultrasound biometry or optical biometry (e.g. the IOLMaster), together with the corneal power obtained by keratometry (A). Intraocular pressure and pachymetry (B), anterior chamber cell grade and pupil size (C), and colour or contrast testing (D) are not inputs to IOL power calculation; axial length error is the largest source of refractive surprise.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_016 [M] Which feature is characteristic of phacoemulsification cataract surgery?
    A. A 10-12 mm limbal incision with removal of the lens within its intact capsule
    B. Delivery of the whole nucleus intact through a scleral tunnel without ultrasound
   *C. Ultrasonic fragmentation and aspiration of the nucleus through a small (~2-3 mm) incision after a continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis
    D. Routine removal of the entire posterior capsule as part of the procedure
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Cataract surgery: phacoemulsification)
   concept: Phacoemulsification uses an ultrasonic probe to fragment and aspirate the nucleus through a small (~2-3 mm) self-sealing incision after fashioning a continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis, leaving the posterior capsule intact to support a foldable IOL (C). A large incision with intact-capsule removal describes ICCE (A), intact nuclear delivery through a scleral tunnel describes manual SICS/ECCE (B), and the posterior capsule is deliberately preserved, not removed (D).
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_017 [M] During phacoemulsification there is sudden deepening of the anterior chamber, a pupillary snap and loss of followability of the nuclear fragments. This most commonly signals which intraoperative complication, the commonest of cataract surgery?
    A. Suprachoroidal (expulsive) haemorrhage
   *B. Posterior capsule rupture
    C. Descemet membrane detachment
    D. Zonular dialysis with capsular contraction
   key=B ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Complications of cataract surgery)
   concept: Posterior capsule rupture is the commonest intraoperative complication of cataract surgery; sudden anterior chamber deepening, a pupillary snap and loss of nuclear followability are classic signs, and it predisposes to vitreous loss, dropped nucleus, and later cystoid macular oedema or endophthalmitis (B). Expulsive haemorrhage (A) is rare and dramatic, while Descemet detachment (C) and zonular dialysis (D) are less common and present differently.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_018 [H] An elderly woman with an immature senile cataract presents with sudden ocular pain, coloured haloes and a red eye. The lens appears swollen, the anterior chamber is shallow and the intraocular pressure is markedly raised. The type of cataract responsible is:
    A. Morgagnian hypermature cataract
    B. Nuclear sclerotic cataract
    C. Posterior polar cataract
   *D. Intumescent (swollen) cataract precipitating phacomorphic angle-closure glaucoma
   key=D ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Glaucoma (Lens-induced glaucomas)
   concept: An immature senile cataract that imbibes water becomes intumescent (swollen), increasing lens thickness, shallowing the anterior chamber and pushing the iris forward; this can precipitate acute secondary angle-closure, i.e. phacomorphic glaucoma, with pain, haloes and a high IOP (D). A Morgagnian (hypermature) cataract (A) tends to cause phacolytic open-angle glaucoma, whereas nuclear (B) and posterior polar (C) cataracts do not swell the lens in this way.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_019 [H] An elderly patient with a long-standing hypermature cataract develops a painful red eye with very high IOP; the anterior chamber shows numerous cells and iridescent particles, and gonioscopy reveals an open angle. The mechanism of this secondary glaucoma is:
   *A. Leaked high-molecular-weight lens proteins engulfed by macrophages that obstruct the open trabecular meshwork (phacolytic glaucoma)
    B. Pupillary block from a swollen lens closing the angle
    C. Neovascularisation of the angle from chronic ocular ischaemia
    D. Steroid-induced trabecular meshwork dysfunction
   key=A ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Glaucoma (Lens-induced glaucomas)
   concept: In phacolytic glaucoma a hypermature (often Morgagnian) cataract leaks high-molecular-weight soluble lens proteins through an intact but permeable capsule; these proteins and the macrophages that engulf them obstruct an open trabecular meshwork, raising IOP acutely (A). It is not a pupillary-block angle-closure (B), not neovascular (C) and not steroid-induced (D); definitive treatment is prompt cataract extraction after controlling the pressure.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_020 [H] The secondary angle-closure glaucoma produced when an intumescent, swollen cataractous lens pushes the iris-lens diaphragm forward is best termed:
    A. Phacolytic glaucoma
   *B. Phacomorphic glaucoma
    C. Phacoantigenic (phacoanaphylactic) glaucoma
    D. Pigmentary glaucoma
   key=B ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Glaucoma (Lens-induced glaucomas)
   concept: Phacomorphic glaucoma is a secondary angle-closure glaucoma that occurs when an intumescent, swollen cataractous lens increases in thickness and pushes the iris-lens diaphragm forward, mechanically closing the drainage angle (B). Phacolytic glaucoma (A) is an open-angle glaucoma from leaked lens proteins, phacoantigenic reaction (C) is a lens-induced uveitis, and pigmentary glaucoma (D) is unrelated to cataract.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_021 [H] Assertion (A): Phacoanaphylactic (lens-induced) uveitis is a granulomatous inflammation that follows rupture of the lens capsule from trauma or surgery. Reason (R): It is an IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity reaction to lens proteins. Choose the correct option.
    A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
   *C. A is true, but R is false
    D. A is false, but R is true
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Uvea and Lens (Lens-induced uveitis)
   concept: Phacoanaphylactic (phacoantigenic, lens-induced) uveitis is a granulomatous, zonal inflammatory response to lens proteins exposed after capsule rupture from trauma or surgery, so the assertion is true. Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_022 [H] A male infant presents with bilateral congenital cataracts and congenital glaucoma, together with hypotonia, developmental delay and features of renal (Fanconi-type) tubular dysfunction. The most likely diagnosis is:

   concept: Lowe (oculocerebrorenal) syndrome is an X-linked disorder of the OCRL gene affecting male infants, characterised by the combination of congenital cataract, congenital glaucoma and neurological involvement (hypotonia, intellectual disability), plus proximal renal (Fanconi-type) tubular dysfunction (D). Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_023 [H] A 35-year-old man with frontal balding, temporalis wasting, difficulty releasing his grip after a handshake and percussion myotonia is found on slit-lamp to have fine, multicoloured iridescent crystalline lens opacities ('Christmas-tree' cataract). The underlying disorder is:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_024 [H] In the SRK (Sanders-Retzlaff-Kraff) regression formula for intraocular lens power, P = A - 2.5L - 0.9K, the terms 'L' and 'K' respectively denote:

    B. Limbal white-to-white distance and anterior chamber depth

    D. Refractive index of the lens and pupil size
   key=C ref=A.K. Khurana — Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Diseases of the Lens (Biometry and IOL power calculation)
   concept: In the SRK formula P = A - 2.5L - 0.9K, P is the emmetropic IOL power, A is the lens-specific A-constant, L is the axial length of the eye in millimetres, and K is the average keratometry (corneal dioptric power) (C). Q_GEN_OPHTH_CATARACT_025 [H] Three days after uneventful cataract surgery a patient returns with severe ocular pain, marked reduction of vision, lid oedema, circumcorneal congestion and a hypopyon. The most appropriate immediate step, and the commonest causative organism, are:
    A. Observation with topical lubricants; organism usually Pseudomonas
    B. Increase topical steroids only; organism usually fungal
    C. Nd:YAG capsulotomy; organism usually Propionibacterium acnes

Observation (A), steroids alone (B) or capsulotomy (C) would be dangerous.
Q_S_013 [E] A 68-year-old man has gradually progressive, painless dimming of vision in both eyes with glare. Examination shows a grayish-white opacity of the lens. What is the definitive treatment of a visually significant senile cataract?
    A. Topical corticosteroid drops
   *B. Surgical extraction with intraocular lens implantation
    C. Long-term miotic eye drops
    D. Oral antioxidant vitamin supplements
   key=B ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology
   concept: There is no proven medical therapy that reverses a cataract. The definitive treatment of a visually significant cataract is surgery - phacoemulsification (or extracapsular cataract extraction) with implantation of a posterior-chamber intraocular lens.

=== Ophthalmology / Diabetic retinopathy, retinal detachment
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_001 [M] The earliest histopathological change in the retina in diabetic retinopathy is:
   *A. Selective loss of retinal capillary pericytes (mural cells)
    B. Thinning of the capillary basement membrane
    C. Proliferation of retinal Müller glial cells
    D. Occlusion of the central retinal artery
   key=A ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Diseases of the Retina (Diabetic Retinopathy)
   concept: Chronic hyperglycaemia damages retinal capillaries; the earliest change is selective loss of pericytes (mural cells) together with basement-membrane thickening and endothelial injury. Pericyte loss weakens the capillary wall, producing microaneurysms and impaired autoregulation. The basement membrane thickens (not thins), Müller-cell change is not the initiating event, and central retinal artery occlusion is an unrelated vascular emergency.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_002 [E] Which is the earliest clinically visible ophthalmoscopic lesion of non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy?
    A. Neovascularization of the optic disc
    B. Hard exudates at the macula
   *C. Microaneurysms
    D. Tractional retinal detachment
   key=C ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye — Diseases of the Retina
   concept: Microaneurysms are the earliest clinically visible lesion of non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy, seen as tiny discrete red dots typically temporal to the fovea, arising from focal capillary-wall outpouchings after pericyte loss. Hard exudates and cotton-wool spots appear later; neovascularization defines the proliferative stage; tractional detachment is an advanced complication. On fluorescein angiography microaneurysms appear as pinpoint hyperfluorescent dots.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_003 [E] Cotton-wool spots seen on fundoscopy represent:
    A. Lipid and lipoprotein deposits in the outer plexiform layer
   *B. Microinfarcts of the retinal nerve-fibre layer with accumulated axoplasmic debris
    C. Saccular outpouchings of retinal capillaries
    D. Rupture of superficial retinal capillaries into the nerve-fibre layer
   key=B ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Diseases of the Retina
   concept: Cotton-wool spots are fluffy white superficial lesions representing microinfarcts of the nerve-fibre layer, where interrupted axoplasmic flow causes swollen axons (cytoid bodies) to accumulate. They are not lipid: hard exudates are lipid/lipoprotein in the outer plexiform (Henle) layer; saccular capillary outpouchings are microaneurysms; superficial capillary rupture gives flame-shaped haemorrhages. Cotton-wool spots occur in diabetes, hypertension, HIV and vascular occlusions.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_004 [H] According to the ETDRS 4-2-1 rule, severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy is diagnosed when a patient has any ONE of the following:
    A. Neovascularization of the disc greater than one-third disc area
    B. Hard exudates within 500 µm of the foveal centre
   *C. Diffuse intraretinal haemorrhages/microaneurysms in all 4 quadrants, OR venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, OR intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in ≥1 quadrant
    D. Microaneurysms confined to a single quadrant
   key=C ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Diabetic Retinopathy (ETDRS classification)
   concept: The ETDRS 4-2-1 rule defines severe NPDR by ANY of: diffuse intraretinal haemorrhages/microaneurysms in all 4 quadrants, OR venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, OR IRMA in ≥1 quadrant; having two of these features indicates very severe NPDR, carrying a high risk of progression to proliferative disease within a year. Disc neovascularization already denotes PDR, and exudates near the fovea define clinically significant macular oedema, a separate axis.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_005 [M] Panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) improves proliferative diabetic retinopathy chiefly by:
    A. Directly cauterizing the new vessels on the optic disc
    B. Sealing peripheral retinal breaks to prevent detachment
    C. Draining subretinal fluid to flatten the macula
   *D. Ablating ischaemic peripheral retina, lowering the VEGF drive so neovascularization regresses
   key=D ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye — Retina (management of diabetic retinopathy)
   concept: Panretinal photocoagulation destroys ischaemic peripheral retina, the source of VEGF and other angiogenic factors; reducing this angiogenic drive causes existing retinal and disc new vessels to regress and prevents vitreous haemorrhage and neovascular glaucoma. It does not directly cauterize individual new vessels, does not seal breaks (that is retinopexy), and is not the primary treatment for macular oedema, where focal/grid laser or anti-VEGF are used.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_RETINA_006 [M] A patient with centre-involving diabetic macular oedema and reduced vision is best managed with:
    A. Immediate panretinal photocoagulation
   *B. Intravitreal anti-VEGF injection
    C. Systemic oral corticosteroids
    D. Early pars plana vitrectomy

=== Ophthalmology / Glaucoma
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_001 [E] Aqueous humour, which maintains intraocular pressure, is actively secreted by which ocular structure?
    A. Trabecular meshwork
    B. Corneal endothelium
   *C. Non-pigmented epithelium of the ciliary processes
    D. Lens epithelium
   key=C ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Chapter on Glaucoma (Aqueous humour dynamics)
   concept: Aqueous humour is secreted mainly by the non-pigmented (inner) epithelium of the ciliary processes (pars plicata) through active transport (Na-K ATPase and carbonic anhydrase), supplemented by ultrafiltration and diffusion. The trabecular meshwork is the site of drainage, not production; the corneal endothelium regulates corneal deturgescence; and the lens epithelium plays no role in aqueous formation.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_002 [E] The accepted statistical normal range of intraocular pressure in the adult population, and the pattern of its diurnal variation, is best described as:
   *A. 10-21 mmHg, with a diurnal peak typically in the early morning hours
    B. 5-10 mmHg, peaking only around midnight
    C. 22-30 mmHg, with no diurnal variation
    D. 10-21 mmHg, identical at every hour of the day
   key=A ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye, 22nd ed. — Glaucoma (Intraocular pressure)
   concept: The mean IOP is about 16 mmHg and the normal range is taken as 10-21 mmHg (mean plus or minus two standard deviations). IOP shows a diurnal rhythm, usually varying 3-5 mmHg in normal eyes and more in glaucoma, and characteristically peaks in the early morning. A fluctuation greater than 5-8 mmHg is suspicious, and persistent readings above 21 mmHg warrant evaluation.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_003 [E] In the normal human eye, the majority of aqueous humour drains from the anterior chamber via which pathway?
    A. Uveoscleral (ciliary body to suprachoroidal) route
   *B. Trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm
    C. Diffusion across the corneal stroma
    D. Active pumping by the retinal pigment epithelium
   key=B ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Glaucoma (Aqueous outflow)
   concept: The conventional (trabecular) pathway accounts for roughly 80-90% of aqueous outflow: aqueous passes through the trabecular meshwork into Schlemm's canal, then via collector channels and aqueous veins to the episcleral veins. The uveoscleral (unconventional) route drains the remaining 10-20%, is relatively pressure-independent, and is the pathway enhanced by prostaglandin analogues; the cornea and RPE play no drainage role.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_004 [E] A 4-month-old infant is brought with persistent watering, marked intolerance to light and squeezing of the eyelids; both corneas appear enlarged and hazy. What is the most likely diagnosis?
    A. Congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction
    B. Ophthalmia neonatorum
    C. Retinoblastoma
   *D. Primary congenital glaucoma (buphthalmos)
   key=D ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Congenital and developmental glaucomas
   concept: The classic triad of primary congenital glaucoma is epiphora, photophobia and blepharospasm. Because an infant's sclera is distensible, raised IOP enlarges the globe (buphthalmos) and cornea (diameter over 12 mm), causing corneal haze and horizontal breaks in Descemet's membrane (Haab's striae). Nasolacrimal duct obstruction gives watering and discharge with a normal cornea; ophthalmia neonatorum gives purulent discharge; retinoblastoma presents with leukocoria.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_005 [E] Goldmann applanation tonometry, the clinical gold standard for measuring intraocular pressure, is based on which physical principle?
    A. Poiseuille's law of laminar flow
   *B. Imbert-Fick law (pressure equals force divided by the area of applanation)
    C. Snell's law of refraction
    D. Bernoulli's principle
   key=B ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye, 22nd ed. — Tonometry
   concept: Goldmann applanation tonometry flattens a fixed corneal area of 3.06 mm diameter. By the Imbert-Fick principle, the pressure inside a dry, thin-walled sphere equals the applied force divided by the applanated area. The 3.06 mm diameter is chosen so that corneal rigidity and the tear-film surface tension cancel out, and the force in grams multiplied by ten gives IOP in mmHg. Readings are influenced by central corneal thickness.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_006 [M] Latanoprost, a prostaglandin analogue commonly used as first-line therapy in primary open-angle glaucoma, lowers intraocular pressure mainly by:
    A. Decreasing aqueous production by the ciliary body
    B. Increasing episcleral venous pressure
   *C. Increasing uveoscleral outflow of aqueous humour
    D. Constricting the pupil to open the trabecular meshwork
   key=C ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Medical management of glaucoma
   concept: Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost, travoprost, bimatoprost) act on FP receptors and remodel the ciliary muscle extracellular matrix through matrix metalloproteinases, thereby enhancing the pressure-independent uveoscleral outflow. They are potent, once-daily first-line agents. Characteristic side effects include conjunctival hyperaemia, increased iris and periocular pigmentation, eyelash lengthening (hypertrichosis) and cystoid macular oedema in predisposed eyes.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_007 [M] A patient is found to have a thin central corneal thickness of 480 microns. Compared with the true intraocular pressure, Goldmann applanation tonometry in this eye will most likely:

    C. Measure the true IOP exactly
    D. Be completely unaffected by corneal thickness

   concept: Goldmann tonometry is calibrated for an average central corneal thickness of about 520-545 microns. A thin cornea offers less resistance to applanation, so the measured pressure reads lower than the true IOP (underestimation) — an important reason why thin-cornea ocular hypertensives progress and are under-treated. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_008 [M] On gonioscopy using the Shaffer grading system, which grade denotes an angle that is closed (no angle structures visible) and carries the highest risk of angle closure?

   concept: The Shaffer system grades the angle by the angular width between the iris and trabecular meshwork: Grade 4 (35-45 degrees, wide open), Grade 3 (20-35 degrees, open), Grade 2 (about 20 degrees, closure possible), Grade 1 (about 10 degrees, closure probable) and Grade 0 (0 degrees, closed, with iris touching the meshwork). Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_009 [M] A 68-year-old with open-angle glaucoma shows, on slit-lamp examination, greyish-white flaky dandruff-like deposits on the anterior lens capsule in a target pattern and along the pupillary margin. The most likely diagnosis is:

    C. Phacolytic glaucoma
    D. Neovascular glaucoma

   concept: Pseudoexfoliation syndrome deposits fibrillar grey-white basement-membrane-like material on the anterior lens capsule (a central disc plus a peripheral band separated by a clear zone), the pupil margin, zonules and trabecular meshwork; meshwork obstruction raises IOP. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_010 [M] A young myopic man has raised IOP; gonioscopy shows dense homogeneous pigmentation of the trabecular meshwork and slit-lamp shows a vertical spindle-shaped pigment deposit on the corneal endothelium. The most likely diagnosis is:

   concept: In pigment dispersion syndrome a concave, posteriorly bowed iris rubs against the lens zonules, liberating pigment that deposits on the corneal endothelium as a vertical Krukenberg spindle and heavily pigments the trabecular meshwork, producing pigmentary glaucoma. Mid-peripheral iris transillumination defects are characteristic, distinguishing it from the flaky deposits of pseudoexfoliation.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_011 [M] Assertion (A): Topical timolol should be avoided in a patient with bronchial asthma. Reason (R): Timolol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker that can precipitate bronchospasm and bradycardia through systemic absorption. Select the correct option:
   *A. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=A ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye, 22nd ed. — Antiglaucoma drugs (beta-blockers)
   concept: Timolol, a non-selective beta-blocker, lowers IOP by reducing aqueous secretion from the ciliary epithelium. Systemic absorption through the nasolacrimal mucosa can cause bronchospasm (beta-2 blockade), bradycardia and heart block (beta-1 blockade); hence it is contraindicated in asthma or COPD, sinus bradycardia, second- or third-degree heart block and decompensated heart failure. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_012 [M] Oral acetazolamide lowers intraocular pressure primarily by which mechanism?
   *A. Inhibiting carbonic anhydrase to reduce aqueous humour secretion
    B. Increasing trabecular outflow facility
    C. Osmotically dehydrating the vitreous body
    D. Increasing uveoscleral outflow
   key=A ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors
   concept: Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (oral acetazolamide and topical dorzolamide or brinzolamide) block carbonic anhydrase in the ciliary epithelium, reducing bicarbonate formation and thereby aqueous production, which lowers IOP. Acetazolamide is a sulphonamide; systemic effects include paraesthesiae, metabolic acidosis, hypokalaemia, renal stones and malaise, and it should be avoided in sulpha allergy and sickle-cell disease. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_013 [M] A diabetic with proliferative retinopathy now has a painful red eye, IOP of 44 mmHg, new vessels on the iris (rubeosis) and a fibrovascular membrane crossing the angle on gonioscopy. The diagnosis is:

    B. Phacomorphic glaucoma
    C. Pseudoexfoliation glaucoma
   *D. Neovascular glaucoma
   key=D ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Neovascular glaucoma
   concept: Neovascular glaucoma follows retinal ischaemia (proliferative diabetic retinopathy, central retinal vein occlusion, ocular ischaemic syndrome), which drives VEGF release and rubeosis iridis. A fibrovascular membrane grows over the angle, first causing a secondary open-angle picture and then contracting to produce synechial angle closure with a high, painful IOP. Management targets the underlying ischaemia with pan-retinal photocoagulation and anti-VEGF agents, along with IOP control and drainage surgery.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_014 [M] Which visual field defect is most characteristic of early to moderate glaucomatous optic nerve damage?

   key=B ref=Parsons' Diseases of the Eye, 22nd ed. — Perimetry in glaucoma
   concept: Glaucoma damages the arcuate retinal nerve-fibre bundles, so field defects arc around fixation from the blind spot along the nerve-fibre distribution: paracentral scotomas, arcuate (Bjerrum) scotomas and a nasal step (Roenne's) from asymmetric involvement across the horizontal raphe. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_015 [M] Chronic use of topical corticosteroids can raise intraocular pressure mainly by:

    D. Increasing episcleral venous pressure
   key=C ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Steroid-induced glaucoma
   concept: Corticosteroids raise IOP by increasing trabecular outflow resistance: they cause accumulation of glycosaminoglycans and extracellular matrix, reduce phagocytosis of debris and induce myocilin (TIGR) in meshwork cells, producing an open-angle picture. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_016 [H] An elderly patient with a long-standing, now swollen (intumescent) mature cataract presents with acute pain and IOP of 46 mmHg; the anterior chamber is shallow only in that eye and gonioscopy shows a closed angle. The diagnosis is:

   concept: Phacomorphic glaucoma is a secondary angle-closure glaucoma: an intumescent mature or hypermature lens pushes the iris-lens diaphragm forward, shallowing the anterior chamber and closing the angle with a sudden rise in IOP. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_018 [M] What is the definitive treatment to relieve and prevent recurrence of primary angle closure caused by pupillary block?

   *C. Laser peripheral iridotomy

   concept: In pupillary-block angle closure, aqueous is trapped behind the iris and bows it forward to close the angle. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_019 [H] An elderly patient with a hypermature (Morgagnian) cataract develops acute pain and IOP of 40 mmHg; the anterior chamber shows heavy flare with floating white particles, yet gonioscopy reveals an open angle. The diagnosis is:

    C. Acute anterior uveitis
    D. Neovascular glaucoma
   key=B ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Phacolytic (lens-protein) glaucoma
   concept: In phacolytic glaucoma, high-molecular-weight lens proteins leak through the intact but permeable capsule of a hypermature cataract; protein-laden macrophages and the free proteins clog an open trabecular meshwork, raising IOP as an open-angle secondary glaucoma. The clue is dense anterior-chamber flare with a hypermature lens and an open angle. Treatment is IOP control followed by cataract extraction. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_020 [H] Which class of drug is contraindicated (should be avoided) in an eye at risk of, or with, primary angle closure because it can precipitate an acute attack?

   concept: Mydriatics and anticholinergics (atropine, homatropine, tropicamide, and systemic anticholinergics, some antihistamines and tricyclics) dilate the pupil to a mid-dilated position that maximises iris-lens contact and peripheral iris crowding, precipitating pupillary-block angle closure in a narrow angle; adrenergic mydriatics carry a similar risk. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_021 [H] Regarding hyperosmotic agents used to rapidly lower intraocular pressure, which statement is correct?

   concept: Hyperosmotic agents (intravenous mannitol, oral glycerol, oral isosorbide) raise plasma osmolality and draw water from the vitreous to lower IOP acutely, for example in acute angle closure. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_023 [H] A 60-year-old has glaucomatous optic disc cupping and arcuate field loss, but repeated readings show IOP of 14-16 mmHg with open angles and a normal central corneal thickness; the disc shows a splinter haemorrhage at its margin. The most likely diagnosis is:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_024 [H] A man sustained blunt trauma to one eye from a cricket ball several years ago; that eye now has raised IOP, and gonioscopy shows an abnormally deep, widened ciliary-body band with a torn iris root over part of the angle. The diagnosis is:

    D. Pseudoexfoliation glaucoma

   concept: Blunt ocular trauma can tear the face of the ciliary body between the longitudinal and circular muscle fibres (angle recession), seen on gonioscopy as a segment of abnormally widened and deepened ciliary-body band with a torn iris root. Q_GEN_OPHTH_GLAUCOMA_025 [H] The preferred initial (definitive) treatment of primary congenital glaucoma is:

    C. Enucleation
   *D. Angle surgery — goniotomy or trabeculotomy
   key=D ref=Khurana's Comprehensive Ophthalmology, 7th ed. — Management of congenital glaucoma
   concept: Primary congenital glaucoma results from maldevelopment of the angle (trabeculodysgenesis) that obstructs outflow. === Ophthalmology / Refractive errors
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_001 [E] In an emmetropic eye at complete physiological rest (accommodation fully relaxed), where do parallel rays of light coming from infinity come to a focus?
   *A. Exactly on the retina (fovea)
    B. In front of the retina, in the vitreous
    C. Behind the retina
    D. On the retinal pigment epithelium behind the choroid
   key=A ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Optics and Refraction (Errors of Refraction)
   concept: Emmetropia is the ideal state in which the axial length and total refractive power are balanced, so parallel rays from infinity focus precisely on the retina without any accommodative effort; the far point lies at infinity. A focus in front of the retina defines myopia and a focus behind it defines hypermetropia, while the RPE lies deep to the neurosensory retina and is never the image plane.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_002 [E] Which structure contributes the greatest amount of static refractive (dioptric) power to the resting human eye?
    A. The aqueous humour
   *B. The cornea (~43 D)
    C. The crystalline lens (~19 D)
    D. The vitreous humour
   key=B ref=Khurana, Theory and Practice of Optics and Refraction — Physiological Optics
   concept: The cornea (mainly its air–tear interface) is the principal refracting surface, providing about 43–44 D of the eye's total ~58–60 D. The crystalline lens adds only ~15–20 D at rest but is the sole variable element that changes power during accommodation. Aqueous and vitreous humour have refractive indices close to their surroundings and contribute negligible refracting power.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_003 [M] A myopic eye in which the total dioptric power of the refracting media is normal but the anteroposterior axial length is longer than normal is best classified as:
    A. Curvatural myopia
    B. Index myopia
   *C. Axial myopia
    D. Positional myopia
   key=C ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Errors of Refraction (Myopia)
   concept: Axial myopia is due to an increased anteroposterior length with normal refracting power; roughly each 1 mm of extra axial length adds about 3 D of myopia, and most simple and pathological myopia is axial. Curvatural myopia results from steeper corneal/lens curvature, index myopia from a rise in refractive index (e.g. nuclear sclerosis or hyperglycaemia), and positional myopia from anterior displacement of the lens.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_004 [E] The far point (punctum remotum) of an uncorrected myopic eye lies:
    A. At infinity
    B. Behind the eye, as a virtual point
    C. At the anterior focal point of the cornea
   *D. At a finite distance in front of the eye
   key=D ref=Khurana, Theory and Practice of Optics and Refraction — Refractive Errors
   concept: In myopia the far point is a real point situated a finite distance in front of the eye; the greater the myopia the nearer this point (a −2 D myope's far point is 0.5 m, a −4 D myope's is 0.25 m). In an emmetrope the far point is at infinity, whereas in a hypermetrope it is virtual and lies behind the eye. Knowing the far point lets one calculate the concave lens power needed for correction.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_005 [M] A young man with well-corrected daytime distance vision complains that objects appear blurred and he tends to under-estimate distances only while driving at night. The most likely explanation is:
   *A. Night (twilight) myopia from pupil dilation and dark-focus of accommodation
    B. Early nuclear sclerotic cataract
    C. Accommodative spasm that is relieved in the dark
    D. Against-the-rule astigmatism
   key=A ref=Khurana, Theory and Practice of Optics and Refraction — Myopia (Night Myopia)
   concept: In dim light the pupil dilates, increasing spherical aberration, and accommodation drifts to an intermediate resting dark-focus of about 1–1.5 D; together with the Purkinje shift this produces a relative myopia at night that a small extra concave (minus) correction can relieve. Early cataract and against-the-rule astigmatism blur vision by day too, and accommodative spasm is worsened, not relieved, in the dark.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_006 [M] Which type of myopia is physiological in nature, usually appears around puberty, rarely exceeds −6 D, gives good corrected acuity, and is not associated with degenerative fundus changes?

    C. Pathological (degenerative) myopia
    D. Index myopia
   key=B ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Errors of Refraction (Types of Myopia)
   concept: Simple myopia is a physiological (correlation-type) variation with onset near puberty, magnitude usually below −5 to −6 D, normal corrected acuity and no degenerative fundus changes. Pathological myopia is progressive and high (often beyond −6 to −8 D) with staphyloma, lacquer cracks and chorioretinal degeneration; congenital myopia is present from birth and often high, while index myopia arises from lens sclerosis in later life.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_007 [H] A patient with −14 D pathological myopia has a raised, circular, pigmented macular lesion representing organised subretinal haemorrhage arising from choroidal neovascularisation. This lesion is known as:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_008 [M] The full amount of hypermetropia that becomes evident only after complete paralysis of accommodation by cycloplegia is termed:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_010 [H] A myopic patient has a far point 1 metre in front of the eye and a near point at 20 cm. What is his amplitude of accommodation?

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_012 [M] A 50-year-old emmetrope needs reading glasses. Which near addition (add) is most appropriate for comfortable reading at about 33 cm?

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_013 [M] In with-the-rule astigmatism, the meridian with the greatest refractive power and the appropriate correcting lens are:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_014 [M] A patient has irregular astigmatism with an uneven anterior corneal surface after a healed corneal ulcer. Which correction gives the best visual result?

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_017 [H] A patient wears a +5.00 D spherical spectacle lens that is decentred 4 mm from the visual axis. What is the induced prismatic power?

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_019 [M] On the duochrome (red-green) test a patient reports that the black letters on the RED half are clearer and blacker than those on the green half. This indicates the eye is:
    A. Over-minused — add plus power
   *B. Under-minused / relatively myopic — add minus power
    C. Perfectly corrected — no change needed
    D. Amblyopic — no refractive change is possible
   key=B ref=Khurana, Theory and Practice of Optics and Refraction — Subjective Refraction (Duochrome Test)
   concept: The duochrome test exploits the eye's chromatic aberration: longer red wavelengths focus behind and shorter green wavelengths in front of the retina. If the red side is clearer the overall focus lies anterior to the retina (the myopic/under-minused side), so minus power should be added; if green is clearer, add plus (mnemonic RAM-GAP). The endpoint is reached when both halves appear equally clear.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_020 [E] A patient's unaided visual acuity is 6/36 but improves to 6/9 when tested through a pinhole. This most strongly suggests:
    A. A dense nuclear sclerotic cataract
    B. Advanced glaucomatous optic atrophy
   *C. An uncorrected refractive error
    D. Age-related macular degeneration
   key=C ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Clinical Methods (Pinhole Test)
   concept: A pinhole admits only central paraxial rays and eliminates the blur circle produced by ametropia, so vision that improves with a pinhole indicates a refractive error correctable by glasses. Little or no improvement points instead to a media opacity such as dense cataract, or to organic macular or optic-nerve disease. The pinhole thus helps separate refractive from non-refractive causes of reduced acuity.
Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_021 [M] During plane-mirror streak retinoscopy performed at 1 metre, the retinal reflex moves in the SAME direction as the streak (a "with" movement). Before applying the working-distance correction, this indicates the eye is:

    C. Irregularly astigmatic and cannot be neutralised

   key=D ref=Khurana, Theory and Practice of Optics and Refraction — Retinoscopy
   concept: A "with" movement means the far point lies behind the retinoscope peephole, so the eye is hypermetropic, emmetropic, or myopic by less than the working-distance dioptre, and plus lenses are added to neutralise it. Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_023 [M] A soft contact lens wearer who habitually sleeps in her lenses presents with a painful red eye, photophobia, and a corneal stromal infiltrate with an overlying epithelial defect. The most sight-threatening diagnosis is:

Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_024 [H] A 28-year-old with stable −13 D myopia and a thin central cornea of 480 µm wishes to be spectacle-independent. LASIK is inadvisable here because of insufficient residual stromal bed. The most appropriate option is:

    B. Radial keratotomy

   key=C ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Refractive Surgery (Phakic IOL / ICL)
   concept: High myopia combined with a thin cornea exceeds the safe ablation limit for LASIK or PRK, risking iatrogenic ectasia and too little residual stroma, so a phakic intraocular lens (implantable collamer lens, ICL) is preferred because it corrects large errors without removing corneal tissue and is reversible. Q_GEN_OPHTH_REFRACT_025 [H] An adult with +1.00 D hypermetropia in the right eye and +6.00 D in the left develops troublesome asthenopia and a difference in the size of the two retinal images when wearing spectacles. The optical correction that best minimises this aniseikonia is:
    A. Higher-powered spectacle lenses
    B. A balancing prism in the spectacles
    C. A bifocal spectacle add
   *D. Contact lenses
   key=D ref=Khurana, Comprehensive Ophthalmology — Errors of Refraction (Anisometropia and Aniseikonia)
   concept: This is anisometropia (a 5 D interocular difference) causing aniseikonia, because a spectacle lens sits well in front of the eye and magnifies or minifies the retinal image (about 1–2% per dioptre) and creates differential prism on eccentric gaze.
