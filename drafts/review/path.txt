
=== Pathology / Acute & chronic inflammation, healing/repair
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_001 [E] A pleural fluid aspirate has a specific gravity of 1.025, a high protein content (fluid/serum protein ratio greater than 0.5) and abundant inflammatory cells. This fluid is best classified as:
    A. A serous transudate
    B. Lymph draining from obstructed lymphatics
   *C. An inflammatory exudate
    D. Chyle
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: An exudate is a protein-rich, cell-rich extravascular fluid with specific gravity above 1.020 (and a high LDH); it results from increased vascular permeability during inflammation. A transudate is a protein-poor ultrafiltrate (specific gravity below 1.012) produced by hydrostatic/oncotic imbalance, as in heart failure or hypoalbuminaemia, with few cells. Lymph and chyle differ in origin; chyle is milky and triglyceride-rich from lymphatic disruption. The high protein and cellularity here define an exudate.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_002 [E] The initial, transient, low-affinity interaction (rolling) of neutrophils along activated venular endothelium in acute inflammation is mediated chiefly by which class of molecules?
   *A. Selectins binding sialylated oligosaccharides such as sialyl-Lewis X
    B. Integrins binding ICAM-1
    C. PECAM-1 (CD31) homotypic interactions
    D. Cadherins at endothelial adherens junctions
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Rolling is mediated by selectins: E-selectin on endothelium (induced by TNF and IL-1), P-selectin from Weibel-Palade bodies (redistributed by histamine and thrombin) and leukocyte L-selectin, which bind sialyl-Lewis X-containing glycoproteins. Firm adhesion is a later step mediated by leukocyte integrins (LFA-1, Mac-1) whose affinity is upregulated by chemokines, binding endothelial ICAM-1 and VCAM-1. Transmigration (diapedesis) uses PECAM-1 (CD31). Cadherins hold cells together at junctions and are not recruitment molecules.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_003 [E] The four classic cardinal signs of acute inflammation are rubor, tumor, calor and dolor. The fifth sign, added later by Virchow, is:
   *A. Functio laesa (loss of function)
    B. Pallor
    C. Sclerosis
    D. Necrosis
   key=A ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: Celsus described four cardinal signs: rubor (redness, from vasodilation and increased blood flow), tumor (swelling, from exudate and oedema), calor (heat, from increased blood flow) and dolor (pain). Virchow added functio laesa, loss of function of the inflamed part. Redness and heat are driven by vasodilator mediators such as histamine, prostaglandins and nitric oxide; pain is produced by bradykinin and prostaglandin E2 sensitizing nerve endings, plus mechanical pressure from the exudate.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_004 [E] Accumulation of thin, protein-poor, cell-poor fluid such as that within a burn blister or a mild viral pleural effusion is characteristic of which morphologic pattern of acute inflammation?
    A. Fibrinous inflammation
   *B. Serous inflammation
    C. Suppurative inflammation
    D. Pseudomembranous inflammation
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Serous inflammation is an outpouring of watery, low-protein fluid derived from plasma or from serosal mesothelial cell secretion, as seen in a skin blister or mild body-cavity effusion. Fibrinous inflammation occurs with greater vascular permeability, allowing large fibrinogen molecules to escape and polymerize into fibrin, typically on serosal surfaces such as the pericardium. Suppurative inflammation produces pus (neutrophils and liquefactive necrosis), and pseudomembranous inflammation forms a necroinflammatory membrane.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_005 [E] Phagocytosis of a bacterium is greatly enhanced when the microbe is coated by opsonins. Which of the following acts as a major opsonin?
    A. C5a
    B. Histamine
    C. Leukotriene B4
   *D. C3b
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: The principal opsonins are C3b (and its cleavage product iC3b) from complement, the Fc portion of IgG antibody, and plasma lectins such as mannose-binding lectin; they coat microbes and bind leukocyte receptors (complement receptors CR1/CR3 and Fc-gamma receptors) to promote engulfment. C5a is a potent chemoattractant and anaphylatoxin, not an opsonin. Histamine mediates early vasodilation and increased permeability. Leukotriene B4 is a strong neutrophil chemoattractant and activator but does not opsonize.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_006 [M] A neonate has delayed separation of the umbilical cord (beyond 30 days), recurrent bacterial skin and mucosal infections without formation of pus, and a markedly elevated peripheral neutrophil count. The most likely underlying defect is:
   *A. Deficiency of the beta-2 integrin subunit (CD18) impairing firm leukocyte adhesion
    B. Deficiency of NADPH oxidase impairing the respiratory burst
    C. Defective microtubule-mediated phagolysosome fusion
    D. Isolated myeloperoxidase deficiency
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 arises from mutation of the beta-2 integrin subunit CD18 (shared by LFA-1 and Mac-1), so neutrophils cannot firmly adhere to ICAM-1 or transmigrate. The result is recurrent infections with no pus despite blood neutrophilia, and delayed umbilical cord separation. NADPH oxidase deficiency is chronic granulomatous disease (a killing defect). Defective phagolysosome fusion is Chediak-Higashi. Myeloperoxidase deficiency is usually mild. LAD type 2 instead affects the selectin ligand sialyl-Lewis X.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_007 [M] Among complement fragments generated during activation, which one is BOTH a potent anaphylatoxin AND the most powerful complement-derived neutrophil chemoattractant?
    A. C3b
    B. C1q
   *C. C5a
    D. C5b-9 (membrane attack complex)
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: C5a is the most potent complement-derived chemoattractant and a strong anaphylatoxin: it triggers mast-cell histamine release, increases vascular permeability, and activates neutrophil adhesion and the oxidative burst. C3a and C4a are anaphylatoxins but are weaker and are not chemotactic. C3b and iC3b are opsonins. C5b-9 assembles into the membrane attack complex, a lytic transmembrane pore. C1q initiates the classical pathway by binding antigen-antibody complexes.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_008 [M] Within minutes of a bee sting, the affected skin becomes red, swollen and warm. The mediator responsible for this immediate, transient increase in venular permeability, released chiefly from mast cells, is:
    A. Bradykinin
   *B. Histamine
    C. Leukotriene C4
    D. Interleukin-1
   key=B ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: Histamine is stored preformed in mast cell granules (and in basophils and platelets) and is released on degranulation triggered by IgE cross-linking, C3a/C5a or physical stimuli. It mediates the immediate transient phase of increased permeability by causing endothelial cell contraction in postcapillary venules, along with arteriolar vasodilation. Bradykinin produces pain and permeability but via the slower kinin cascade. The cysteinyl leukotrienes C4/D4/E4 cause a more sustained permeability increase. IL-1 is a cytokine mediating endothelial activation and the systemic acute-phase response, not immediate permeability.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_009 [M] A patient with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease develops bronchospasm after taking aspirin, because cyclooxygenase inhibition shunts arachidonic acid toward the lipoxygenase pathway. Which drug directly inhibits the 5-lipoxygenase ENZYME (rather than the leukotriene receptor)?
    A. Celecoxib
    B. Montelukast
    C. Aspirin
   *D. Zileuton
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase, blocking synthesis of all leukotrienes (LTB4 and the cysteinyl leukotrienes C4/D4/E4). Montelukast and zafirlukast antagonize the cysteinyl-leukotriene receptor CysLT1 rather than the enzyme. Aspirin and celecoxib inhibit cyclooxygenase: aspirin irreversibly acetylates COX-1 and COX-2, whereas celecoxib is COX-2 selective. In aspirin sensitivity, COX blockade diverts arachidonate into the lipoxygenase pathway, increasing bronchoconstrictor leukotrienes and precipitating bronchospasm.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_010 [M] A patient on broad-spectrum antibiotics develops profuse diarrhoea; colonoscopy shows adherent yellow-white plaques composed of fibrin, mucus, necrotic epithelium and neutrophils overlying an ulcerated mucosa. This pattern of inflammation is termed:
    A. Serous inflammation
    B. Granulomatous inflammation
   *C. Pseudomembranous inflammation
    D. Fibrinoid necrosis
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Pseudomembranous inflammation is a superficial necroinflammatory membrane of fibrin, sloughed necrotic epithelium, mucus and inflammatory cells overlying an ulcerated surface. The classic cause is Clostridioides difficile colitis (toxins A and B) after antibiotic use, and Corynebacterium diphtheriae in the pharynx. Serous inflammation is thin effusion; granulomatous inflammation is a chronic pattern with epithelioid macrophages; fibrinoid necrosis is a bright eosinophilic vessel-wall change in vasculitis and malignant hypertension, not a mucosal membrane.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_011 [M] A localized, walled-off collection of pus within a tissue, most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus, is best described as:
   *A. An abscess
    B. An ulcer
    C. A serous effusion
    D. A granuloma
   key=A ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: An abscess is a focal collection of purulent exudate (neutrophils, liquefied necrotic cells and oedema fluid) walled off within a tissue, typically produced by pyogenic organisms such as Staphylococcus aureus. The centre undergoes liquefactive necrosis and may be surrounded by preserved neutrophils, granulation tissue and fibrosis forming a pyogenic membrane. An ulcer is a local excavation of an epithelial surface from sloughing of inflamed necrotic tissue. A serous effusion is thin fluid, and a granuloma is an organized collection of epithelioid macrophages characteristic of chronic inflammation.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_012 [M] Which growth factor is the principal driver of angiogenesis (formation of new capillaries) during the development of granulation tissue in wound healing?
    A. Transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-beta)
   *B. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)
    C. Platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) acting alone
    D. Epidermal growth factor (EGF)
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: VEGF (particularly VEGF-A) is the key promoter of angiogenesis: it stimulates endothelial proliferation and migration and increases vascular permeability, and is upregulated by hypoxia through HIF-1-alpha. TGF-beta is the dominant fibrogenic cytokine, stimulating fibroblast synthesis of collagen and fibronectin while suppressing matrix degradation, thereby driving scar formation, and it is also anti-inflammatory. PDGF recruits and activates fibroblasts and smooth muscle and contributes to angiogenesis but is not the principal endothelial mitogen. EGF stimulates epithelial and fibroblast proliferation.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_013 [M] By the end of the remodeling phase (about 3 months), approximately what proportion of the original unwounded tensile strength does a sutured skin wound typically recover?
    A. About 10%
    B. About 30%
   *C. About 70 to 80%
    D. Essentially 100%
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Wound tensile strength is only about 10% of normal at 1 week, when the wound is held largely by sutures; it then rises rapidly over the next 4 weeks and slows, reaching a plateau of roughly 70 to 80% of unwounded skin by about 3 months, and it essentially never regains full 100% strength. This recovery reflects not only collagen accumulation but remodeling: replacement of the initial type III collagen by stronger cross-linked type I collagen, mediated by matrix metalloproteinases and lysyl oxidase cross-linking.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_014 [M] A dark-skinned patient develops a raised, firm scar at the site of an ear piercing that extends well beyond the original wound margins and recurs after excision. This lesion is best described as:
    A. Hypertrophic scar
    B. Contracture
    C. Wound dehiscence
   *D. Keloid
   key=D ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: A keloid is exuberant scar tissue with excess collagen (predominantly type III, later type I) that grows beyond the boundaries of the original wound, does not regress, and tends to recur after excision; it is more frequent in darker-skinned individuals with a genetic predisposition. A hypertrophic scar is also raised but remains confined within the wound margins and may regress with time. Contracture is exaggerated wound shrinkage driven by myofibroblasts, limiting movement across joints or after burns. Wound dehiscence is mechanical rupture of a wound.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_015 [M] Alternatively activated (M2) macrophages, induced by interleukins 4 and 13, differ from classically activated (M1) macrophages chiefly in that M2 macrophages predominantly:
    A. Produce nitric oxide and reactive oxygen species to kill microbes
   *B. Promote tissue repair, angiogenesis and fibrosis with anti-inflammatory IL-10 and TGF-beta
    C. Are activated by interferon-gamma and microbial lipopolysaccharide
    D. Secrete IL-12 to drive Th1 differentiation
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: M1 (classically activated) macrophages are induced by interferon-gamma from Th1 and NK cells and by microbial products such as LPS acting through Toll-like receptors; they secrete IL-1, IL-12 and TNF, generate reactive oxygen species and nitric oxide, and are microbicidal and pro-inflammatory. M2 (alternatively activated) macrophages are induced by IL-4 and IL-13 from Th2 cells; they secrete IL-10 and TGF-beta, dampen inflammation, and promote tissue repair, angiogenesis and fibrosis. The M1-to-M2 shift helps terminate inflammation and begin healing.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_016 [M] Histologically, granulation tissue is characterized by which combination of components?
    A. Epithelioid macrophages and multinucleate giant cells
    B. Central caseous necrosis rimmed by lymphocytes
   *C. Proliferating fibroblasts and new thin-walled capillaries (neovascularization)
    D. Sheets of neutrophils within liquefied tissue
   key=C ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: Granulation tissue, the hallmark of tissue repair, consists of proliferating fibroblasts laying down loose connective tissue together with new leaky capillaries formed by angiogenesis, plus scattered macrophages and other inflammatory cells, giving a soft, pink, granular appearance. It must not be confused with a granuloma, which is an aggregate of epithelioid macrophages with or without giant cells seen in granulomatous inflammation, nor with caseating tuberculous granulomas or suppurative (pus-filled) inflammation. Over time granulation tissue matures into a fibrous collagenous scar.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_017 [M] A peripheral blood differential showing a marked absolute eosinophilia is MOST classically associated with which of the following?
    A. Acute pyogenic bacterial infection
   *B. Helminthic parasitic infestation and type-I hypersensitivity or allergy
    C. Acute viral infection
    D. Typhoid fever
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Eosinophilia is characteristic of helminthic parasitic infections and of IgE-mediated allergic and atopic disorders such as asthma, hay fever and drug reactions, driven largely by IL-5. Acute pyogenic bacterial infection typically causes neutrophilic leukocytosis with a left shift toward immature band forms. Most acute viral infections produce lymphocytosis. Typhoid fever (Salmonella), some other viral infections and overwhelming sepsis characteristically cause leukopenia or neutropenia. Recognizing these leukocyte response patterns aids differential diagnosis.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_018 [H] A child with partial oculocutaneous albinism, recurrent pyogenic infections, mild bleeding tendency and peripheral neuropathy has neutrophils containing giant cytoplasmic granules on a blood smear. The underlying defect is in:
    A. NADPH oxidase
    B. The beta-2 integrin subunit (CD18)
    C. Complement component C3
   *D. The lysosomal trafficking regulator (LYST), impairing phagolysosome fusion
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Chediak-Higashi syndrome is an autosomal-recessive defect in the LYST (lysosomal trafficking regulator) gene, causing defective fusion of lysosomes and granules with phagosomes. This produces giant abnormal granules in leukocytes and other cells, impaired bacterial killing with recurrent pyogenic infections, partial albinism from a melanosome defect, a bleeding tendency from a platelet dense-granule defect, and neurological abnormalities. NADPH oxidase deficiency is chronic granulomatous disease and CD18 deficiency is LAD-1; neither produces giant granules or albinism, and C3 deficiency causes pyogenic infection without these features.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_019 [H] A boy has recurrent infections with catalase-positive organisms (Staphylococcus aureus, Aspergillus, Serratia, Burkholderia) and forms granulomas; a dihydrorhodamine (DHR) flow-cytometry test is abnormal. The defective process is:
   *A. Generation of the respiratory (oxidative) burst by NADPH oxidase
    B. Leukocyte rolling mediated by selectins
    C. Phagolysosome fusion
    D. Antibody-dependent opsonization
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Chronic granulomatous disease results from mutations in the phagocyte NADPH oxidase complex (most often X-linked gp91phox, encoded by CYBB), so phagocytes cannot generate superoxide and hydrogen peroxide for oxygen-dependent killing. Patients suffer recurrent infections with catalase-positive organisms, which destroy their own hydrogen peroxide and thereby deny the phagocyte a borrowed microbial substrate, and they form granulomas. Diagnosis rests on an abnormal dihydrorhodamine-123 oxidation test or nitroblue tetrazolium test. Selectin, LYST and antibody defects are unrelated to the oxidative burst.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_020 [H] Regarding eicosanoids derived from arachidonic acid, which pairing of mediator with its predominant vascular and platelet action is CORRECT?
    A. Thromboxane A2 causes vasodilation and inhibits platelet aggregation
    B. Prostacyclin (PGI2) causes vasoconstriction and promotes platelet aggregation
   *C. Thromboxane A2 causes vasoconstriction and promotes platelet aggregation
    D. Leukotriene B4 is a potent vasodilator
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Thromboxane A2, produced by platelets via cyclooxygenase and thromboxane synthase, causes vasoconstriction and promotes platelet aggregation. Prostacyclin (PGI2), produced by endothelium, has the opposite effect, causing vasodilation and inhibiting platelet aggregation; the TXA2/PGI2 balance regulates haemostasis and vascular tone. Prostaglandins E2, D2 and I2 are vasodilators that also potentiate oedema, pain and fever. Leukotriene B4 is a potent chemoattractant and neutrophil activator rather than a vasodilator, while the cysteinyl leukotrienes C4/D4/E4 cause vasoconstriction, bronchospasm and increased permeability.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_021 [H] Following injury, which of the following cell types is PERMANENT (terminally differentiated and non-dividing) and therefore heals predominantly by scar rather than by regeneration?
    A. Intestinal crypt epithelium
    B. Hepatocytes
    C. Proximal renal tubular epithelium
   *D. Cardiac myocytes
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Cells are classified by proliferative capacity. Labile cells divide continuously and regenerate readily (surface epithelia of gut and skin, and bone marrow). Stable or quiescent cells rest in G0 but divide when stimulated (hepatocytes, renal tubular epithelium, endothelium and fibroblasts) and regenerate after injury. Permanent cells are terminally differentiated and non-dividing (neurons, cardiac myocytes and skeletal muscle); consequently a myocardial infarct heals by fibrous scar rather than muscle regeneration. Skeletal muscle shows only limited regeneration via satellite cells.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_022 [H] During scar maturation the initially deposited type III collagen is progressively replaced by type I collagen. The enzymes primarily responsible for degrading old collagen during this remodeling are:
    A. Lysyl oxidase
   *B. Zinc-dependent matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)
    C. Prolyl hydroxylase
    D. Myeloperoxidase
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Extracellular matrix remodeling depends on matrix metalloproteinases (collagenases, gelatinases and stromelysins), zinc-dependent enzymes secreted by fibroblasts, macrophages and neutrophils, whose activity is restrained by tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs). They degrade type III collagen so it can be replaced by cross-linked type I collagen, increasing tensile strength. Lysyl oxidase is a copper-dependent enzyme that cross-links collagen; prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases require vitamin C to hydroxylate procollagen; myeloperoxidase is a microbicidal enzyme of neutrophils, not a collagenase.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_023 [H] During the systemic acute-phase response driven mainly by interleukin-6, plasma levels of most acute-phase proteins rise. Which plasma protein instead DECREASES (a negative acute-phase reactant)?
   *A. Albumin
    B. C-reactive protein
    C. Fibrinogen
    D. Hepcidin
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Positive acute-phase reactants, whose hepatic synthesis rises under IL-6 (with IL-1 and TNF), include C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A, fibrinogen, hepcidin, haptoglobin, ferritin and complement components. Elevated fibrinogen makes red cells form rouleaux, raising the erythrocyte sedimentation rate; hepcidin sequesters iron and causes the anaemia of chronic disease. Albumin and transferrin are negative acute-phase reactants, whose hepatic synthesis falls during inflammation, contributing to the hypoalbuminaemia seen in chronic illness.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_024 [H] The pathway by which bacterial lipopolysaccharide produces fever culminates in which final mediator acting on the hypothalamic thermoregulatory centre?
    A. Interleukin-1 acting directly on skeletal muscle
   *B. Prostaglandin E2 synthesized via cyclooxygenase
    C. Bradykinin
    D. Histamine
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Chapter 3: Inflammation and Repair
   concept: Exogenous pyrogens such as LPS stimulate leukocytes to release endogenous pyrogens (IL-1, IL-6 and TNF), which act on the hypothalamus, especially the preoptic area and organum vasculosum, to induce cyclooxygenase and raise prostaglandin E2. PGE2 resets the hypothalamic thermostat to a higher set-point, producing fever. Antipyretics such as aspirin and other NSAIDs reduce fever precisely by inhibiting cyclooxygenase and thus PGE2 synthesis. Bradykinin and histamine mediate vascular permeability and pain rather than the febrile set-point.
Q_GEN_PATH_GEN_INFLAMM_025 [H] Which pairing of a multinucleate giant cell type with its characteristic setting is CORRECT?
    A. Langhans giant cell has nuclei scattered randomly throughout the cytoplasm
    B. Aschoff giant cell is characteristically seen in foreign-body reactions
    C. Foreign-body giant cell has nuclei arranged in a peripheral horseshoe
   *D. Touton giant cell has a ring of nuclei surrounding central foamy lipid-rich cytoplasm and is seen in xanthomas
   key=D ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed. — Chapter 6: Inflammation and Healing
   concept: Touton giant cells have a wreath or ring of nuclei encircling central lipid-laden foamy cytoplasm, typical of xanthomas, fat necrosis and xanthogranulomas. Langhans giant cells have nuclei arranged peripherally in a horseshoe or ring pattern, classic of tuberculosis and other granulomas. Foreign-body giant cells have nuclei scattered haphazardly and engulf foreign material. Aschoff giant cells (derived from Anitschkow cells) are found in the Aschoff bodies of rheumatic carditis, not in foreign-body reactions, making option D the only correct pairing.
Q_c1_004 [M] Lung biopsy in pulmonary tuberculosis shows granulomas with central caseation and Langhans giant cells. Which cytokine, secreted mainly by Th1 lymphocytes, is most important in activating macrophages to form epithelioid cells?
    A. Interleukin-4
    B. Interleukin-5
   *C. Interferon-gamma
    D. Interleukin-10
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran 10e, Ch.3 (Inflammation)
   concept: Granuloma formation is a type IV (delayed) hypersensitivity response. Th1 cells secrete IFN-gamma, the key macrophage-activating cytokine that drives transformation into epithelioid and Langhans giant cells. IL-4/IL-5 drive Th2/eosinophilic responses; IL-10 is anti-inflammatory.

=== Pathology / Anemias (micro/macro/hemolytic)
Q_c1_011 [M] A 60-year-old strict vegetarian has macrocytic anemia with hypersegmented neutrophils AND paresthesias with loss of vibration and proprioception. Which laboratory finding differentiates vitamin B12 deficiency from folate deficiency?
    A. Elevated serum homocysteine
   *B. Elevated serum methylmalonic acid
    C. Presence of hypersegmented neutrophils
    D. Macrocytosis (high MCV)
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran 10e, Ch.14 (Megaloblastic Anemia)
   concept: Both B12 and folate deficiency raise homocysteine and cause megaloblastic changes. Only B12 deficiency raises methylmalonic acid and only B12 deficiency causes neurological disease (subacute combined degeneration of the cord).
Q_c1_012 [M] IMAGE-DESCRIBED: A 10-year-old with jaundice, splenomegaly and anemia has a peripheral smear showing small, round, densely staining red cells lacking central pallor, and increased osmotic fragility. The underlying defect involves which structure?
   *A. Red-cell membrane cytoskeletal proteins (spectrin/ankyrin)
    B. Beta-globin chain synthesis
    C. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase
    D. Pyruvate kinase enzyme
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran 10e, Ch.14 (Hemolytic Anemias)
   concept: Hereditary spherocytosis results from defects in membrane cytoskeletal proteins (spectrin, ankyrin, band 3), producing spherocytes with increased osmotic fragility that are trapped and destroyed in the spleen. Splenectomy is curative for symptoms.

=== Pathology / Atherosclerosis, MI, vasculitis
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_001 [E] Which of the following is the earliest lesion of atherosclerosis, composed mainly of lipid-laden macrophages (foam cells) within the intima and known to appear even in childhood?
   *A. Fatty streak
    B. Fibrofatty (fibrous) plaque
    C. Complicated plaque with surface ulceration
    D. Atheromatous (atherosclerotic) aneurysm
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Atherosclerosis)
   concept: The fatty streak is the earliest recognizable lesion, seen even in infants/children, and consists of intimal foam cells (lipid-laden macrophages and smooth muscle cells) with T cells; it is not necessarily obstructive and not all progress. The fibrous plaque is the advanced intimal lesion with a fibrous cap over a lipid core. Complicated plaques (calcification, ulceration, hemorrhage, thrombosis) and aneurysms are late complications, not the earliest lesion.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_002 [M] The foam cells that characterize atherosclerotic plaques are derived predominantly from which of the following cell types after they accumulate intracellular lipid?
    A. Neutrophils and mast cells
    B. Endothelial cells and pericytes
   *C. Macrophages and smooth muscle cells
    D. Plasma cells and fibroblasts
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Pathogenesis of Atherosclerosis)
   concept: Foam cells arise mainly from macrophages that engulf oxidized LDL via scavenger receptors (not down-regulated by intracellular cholesterol, so uptake is unchecked) and from intimal smooth muscle cells that migrate from the media and also ingest lipid. Neutrophils, mast cells, endothelial cells, pericytes, plasma cells and fibroblasts are not the source of the classic lipid-laden foam cell, making the other options incorrect.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_003 [M] Assertion (A): Endothelial injury/dysfunction is regarded as the cornerstone initiating event in the response-to-injury hypothesis of atherosclerosis. Reason (R): A dysfunctional endothelium shows increased permeability to lipoproteins and enhanced leukocyte (monocyte) adhesion.
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Response-to-Injury Hypothesis)
   concept: The modern response-to-injury model holds that chronic endothelial injury/dysfunction is the initiating lesion. Dysfunctional endothelium becomes more permeable (allowing LDL entry and its oxidation) and up-regulates adhesion molecules (VCAM-1) that recruit monocytes, which become intimal macrophages/foam cells. Thus both statements are true and R correctly explains the mechanism by which endothelial dysfunction drives plaque formation, making option A correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_004 [M] In systemic atherosclerosis, which vessel is characteristically the most severely and extensively involved?
    A. Renal arteries
    B. Pulmonary arteries
    C. Radial (upper extremity) arteries
   *D. Abdominal aorta, below the renal arteries
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Sites and Consequences of Atherosclerosis)
   concept: The usual order of severity is: abdominal aorta (especially the infrarenal segment) > coronary arteries > popliteal arteries > internal carotid > circle of Willis. The infrarenal abdominal aorta is the classic site of predilection and of atherosclerotic aneurysm. Pulmonary arteries are generally spared unless there is pulmonary hypertension, and upper-limb (radial) vessels are typically spared; renal ostia may be involved but are not the most severely affected.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_005 [H] Assertion (A): A vulnerable (unstable) atherosclerotic plaque is more prone to rupture than a stable plaque. Reason (R): Vulnerable plaques characteristically have a thin fibrous cap, a large lipid-rich necrotic core, and increased inflammatory (macrophage) content.
    A. A is true but R is false
   *B. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    C. A is false but R is true
    D. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Plaque Vulnerability and Rupture)
   concept: Vulnerable plaques rupture more readily precisely because of their structure: a thin fibrous cap, a large soft lipid/necrotic core, and dense macrophage infiltration. Macrophages secrete matrix metalloproteinases that degrade collagen in the cap, while reduced smooth muscle collagen synthesis further weakens it. Stable plaques have thick fibrous caps and small lipid cores. Both statements are true and R is the correct mechanistic explanation of A.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_006 [M] A 58-year-old man develops crushing chest pain at rest with ST-segment elevation, and angiography shows acute total occlusion of a coronary artery. The immediate pathologic event most responsible for acute transmural myocardial infarction in this setting is:
    A. Gradual concentric luminal narrowing by a stable fibrous plaque
    B. Monckeberg medial calcification of the coronary artery
   *C. Disruption (rupture/erosion) of an atherosclerotic plaque with superimposed thrombosis
    D. Vasculitic aneurysm of the coronary artery
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Ischemic Heart Disease / Acute Plaque Change)
   concept: Most acute coronary syndromes/MIs result from acute plaque disruption (rupture or erosion) that exposes the thrombogenic lipid core and subendothelial collagen, triggering platelet aggregation and occlusive thrombosis. Stable fibrous plaques cause fixed narrowing and stable/exertional angina, not sudden total occlusion. Monckeberg calcifies the media without narrowing the lumen, and coronary vasculitic aneurysm is a rare cause, making C the best answer.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_007 [E] Ring-like calcific deposits confined to the tunica media of medium-sized muscular arteries in a person over 50, producing no luminal narrowing and usually clinically insignificant, are characteristic of:
    A. Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
    B. Atherosclerosis
    C. Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis
   *D. Monckeberg medial calcific sclerosis
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Arteriosclerosis)
   concept: Monckeberg medial calcific sclerosis is calcification limited to the media of muscular arteries in patients typically older than 50; because it does not encroach on the lumen it rarely causes ischemia, though it may be palpable or visible radiographically. Hyaline and hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis affect small arterioles (hypertension/diabetes and malignant hypertension respectively), and atherosclerosis is an intimal disease of large/medium elastic and muscular arteries.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_008 [H] A 42-year-old man presents with BP 220/130 mmHg, severe headache, and papilledema. Renal biopsy shows arterioles with concentric, laminated ('onion-skin') thickening of the wall, some with fibrinoid necrosis. This vascular change is:
    A. Hyaline arteriolosclerosis
   *B. Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis
    C. Monckeberg medial calcific sclerosis
    D. Atheroembolic (cholesterol embolus) disease
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Hypertensive Vascular Disease)
   concept: Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis is the lesion of malignant/accelerated hypertension: concentric laminated 'onion-skin' thickening of arteriolar walls, often accompanied by fibrinoid necrosis (necrotizing arteriolitis). Hyaline arteriolosclerosis is the homogeneous, pink, hyaline wall thickening of benign hypertension and diabetes. Monckeberg is medial calcification of larger muscular arteries, and atheroembolism produces cholesterol clefts, not onion-skinning, so B is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_009 [M] A patient dies 3 hours after the onset of an acute myocardial infarction. Which finding is most likely on routine light microscopy of the infarcted myocardium?
   *A. Essentially no change (at most early wavy fibers and hypereosinophilia)
    B. Dense interstitial neutrophilic infiltrate
    C. Well-formed granulation tissue
    D. Dense collagenous scar
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Morphologic Evolution of Myocardial Infarction)
   concept: In the first 4-6 hours there is essentially no change on routine light microscopy; the infarct is detectable only by special stains such as TTC (which shows loss of formazan). The earliest histologic clues are wavy fibers at the border and hypereosinophilia beginning around 4-12 hours. Coagulative necrosis with neutrophils appears at 1-3 days, granulation tissue at 7-10 days, and dense scar over weeks, so at 3 hours option A is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_010 [H] At autopsy of a patient who died 2 days after an acute myocardial infarction, the microscopic appearance of the infarct is most characteristically:
    A. Contraction band necrosis as the only finding
    B. Macrophage-rich phagocytosis of debris with early granulation tissue
    C. Dense fibrous (collagenous) scar
   *D. Coagulative necrosis with a heavy neutrophilic infiltrate
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Microscopic Evolution of MI)
   concept: At 1-3 days the infarct shows established coagulative necrosis (loss of nuclei and cross-striations) with a prominent interstitial neutrophilic infiltrate that peaks in this window. Macrophage-driven removal of dead myocytes and early granulation tissue characterize days 3-10, and dense fibrous scar forms over weeks. Contraction band necrosis is a marker of reperfusion, not a stand-alone 2-day finding, so coagulative necrosis with neutrophils (D) is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_011 [H] During which post-infarction window is the ventricular wall most susceptible to free-wall rupture, corresponding to maximal macrophage-mediated removal of necrotic myocytes?
    A. 0-6 hours
    B. 12-24 hours
   *C. 3-7 days
    D. 6-8 weeks
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Complications of Myocardial Infarction)
   concept: Free-wall rupture, septal rupture and papillary muscle rupture peak around 3-7 days, when macrophages have digested dead myocytes and the necrotic wall is maximally soft and weak but new collagen has not yet reinforced it. In the first hours the wall has not yet weakened, and by 6-8 weeks a dense collagenous scar has strengthened it, greatly reducing rupture risk, so 3-7 days (C) is the answer.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_012 [M] Well-developed granulation tissue with prominent neovascularization, macrophages, and early collagen at the margins of a myocardial infarct is most characteristic of which interval after infarction?
    A. 4-12 hours
    B. 24-48 hours
   *C. 7-10 days, extending into the second week
    D. About 2 months
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Healing of Myocardial Infarction)
   concept: Granulation tissue (new capillaries, macrophages, fibroblasts, and early collagen deposition) is best developed around 7-10 days and into the second week, marking the transition from debris removal to repair. At 4-12 and 24-48 hours the infarct still shows necrosis with a neutrophilic response, and by roughly 2 months a dense acellular collagenous scar has formed, so option C is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_013 [H] The gross appearance of a myocardial infarct as a yellow-tan, softened center surrounded by a hyperemic (red) border is most typical of an infarct that is approximately:
    A. 0-12 hours old (no gross change)
   *B. 3-7 days old
    C. 2-8 weeks old
    D. More than 2 months old
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Gross Features of Evolving MI)
   concept: Gross evolution: 0-12 hours shows no visible change (TTC needed); 12-24 hours dark mottling; 1-3 days a pale/yellow center; 3-7 days a soft yellow-tan center with a hyperemic (red-brown) rim of granulation tissue at the margin; 10 days-2 weeks a red-gray depressed border; and weeks to 2 months a firm gray-white scar. The yellow soft center with hyperemic border therefore corresponds to about 3-7 days (B).
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_014 [M] Assertion (A): Contraction band necrosis is a hallmark of reperfusion injury after blood flow is restored to ischemic myocardium. Reason (R): Reperfusion causes massive calcium influx into irreversibly injured myocytes, producing hypercontracted sarcomeres seen as intensely eosinophilic transverse bands.
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Reperfusion Injury / Contraction Band Necrosis)
   concept: When ischemic (but not yet dead) myocardium is reperfused, restored oxygen and a surge of intracellular calcium cause exaggerated hypercontraction of myofibrils in irreversibly injured cells, producing contraction bands: intensely eosinophilic transverse bands. This is a classic sign of reperfusion injury, alongside oxygen free-radical damage and microvascular injury. Both statements are true and R correctly explains A, so option A is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_015 [M] Three days after an inferior-wall myocardial infarction, a patient suddenly develops flash pulmonary edema, hypotension, and a new loud holosystolic apical murmur. The most likely mechanical complication is:
    A. Ventricular septal rupture producing a left-to-right shunt
    B. Dressler (post-cardiac injury) syndrome
    C. True ventricular aneurysm
   *D. Rupture of a papillary muscle causing acute mitral regurgitation
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Mechanical Complications of MI)
   concept: Rupture of a necrotic papillary muscle (classically the posteromedial papillary muscle, supplied solely by the posterior descending artery and therefore vulnerable in inferior/RCA infarcts) occurs around days 2-7 and causes acute severe mitral regurgitation with flash pulmonary edema, a new holosystolic murmur, and cardiogenic shock. Septal rupture gives a shunt with a harsh left-sternal-border murmur, Dressler occurs weeks later, and a true aneurysm is a late complication, so D is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_016 [M] Several weeks after a myocardial infarction, a patient develops fever, pleuritic chest pain, a pericardial friction rub, and a raised ESR. This is most consistent with:
    A. Early peri-infarction fibrinous pericarditis (days 1-3)
   *B. Dressler (post-cardiac injury) syndrome, an autoimmune pericarditis
    C. Recurrent myocardial infarction
    D. Acute pulmonary embolism
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Pericarditis / Dressler Syndrome)
   concept: Dressler syndrome is a delayed, immune-mediated (antibodies to released myocardial antigens) pericarditis appearing weeks to a few months after MI, presenting with fever, pleuropericarditic chest pain, a friction rub, and elevated ESR. Early fibrinous ('epistenocardiac') pericarditis instead overlies a transmural infarct within the first 1-3 days. The latency of weeks and autoimmune features distinguish Dressler, making option B correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_017 [H] A patient who had a myocardial infarction 4 days ago develops recurrent chest pain, raising concern for re-infarction. Which cardiac biomarker is MOST useful to confirm re-infarction at this time?
    A. Cardiac troponin I/T
    B. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
   *C. CK-MB
    D. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST)
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — The Heart (Serum Cardiac Markers)
   concept: Cardiac troponin remains elevated for about 7-10 days after the initial infarct, so at day 4 it may still be high and cannot reliably signal a new event. CK-MB rises within hours and returns to normal by 48-72 hours, so a fresh re-rise of CK-MB is the preferred marker to detect re-infarction in this window. LDH and AST are nonspecific and now obsolete for this purpose, so CK-MB (C) is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_018 [H] A tall young man with long limbs, arachnodactyly, and lens dislocation dies suddenly of a proximal aortic dissection. The underlying change in the aortic media most associated with this condition is:
   *A. Cystic medial degeneration (elastin fragmentation with myxoid/proteoglycan pooling)
    B. Obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum
    C. Transmural granulomatous inflammation
    D. Medial calcification of the Monckeberg type
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Aortic Dissection / Cystic Medial Degeneration)
   concept: Marfan syndrome (defective fibrillin-1) predisposes to cystic medial degeneration: fragmentation and loss of elastic fibers with pooling of amorphous proteoglycan ('cystic' spaces) in the media, weakening the wall and favoring dissection, typically in the ascending aorta/first few centimeters. Obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum underlies syphilitic aneurysm, granulomatous inflammation is seen in GCA/Takayasu, and Monckeberg is unrelated, so A is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_019 [E] Tertiary syphilis classically produces an aneurysm of the ascending aorta and arch with a wrinkled 'tree-bark' intima. The primary vascular lesion responsible for this is:
    A. Intimal lipid accumulation (atheroma)
    B. Cystic degeneration of the media
    C. Fibrinoid necrosis of arterioles
   *D. Obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum with medial ischemia
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Syphilitic/Luetic Aneurysm)
   concept: Syphilitic (luetic) aortitis causes an obliterative endarteritis of the vasa vasorum with perivascular plasma-cell cuffing; the resulting ischemia leads to scarring and loss of medial elastic tissue, weakening the wall and dilating the ascending aorta and arch, producing the wrinkled 'tree-bark' intima and potentially aortic regurgitation or coronary ostial stenosis. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_020 [E] A 28-year-old Asian woman has absent radial pulses, markedly unequal blood pressures between the arms, and visual disturbances; imaging shows narrowing of the aortic arch and its major branches. The most likely diagnosis is:
    A. Polyarteritis nodosa

    D. Kawasaki disease
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Takayasu Arteritis)
   concept: Takayasu arteritis is a granulomatous large-vessel vasculitis affecting the aortic arch and its branches, classically in women younger than 50 (often Asian), producing weak/absent upper-limb pulses ('pulseless disease'), blood-pressure discrepancies, and ocular/neurologic symptoms from arch-vessel stenosis. PAN affects medium arteries, Buerger affects distal limb vessels in smokers, and Kawasaki affects young children with coronary involvement, so B is correct.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_021 [M] A 72-year-old woman has new temporal headache, jaw claudication, transient monocular vision loss, and an ESR of 90 mm/hr; temporal artery biopsy shows granulomatous inflammation with multinucleate giant cells and fragmentation of the internal elastic lamina. Besides biopsy, the most important immediate step is:
   *A. Start high-dose corticosteroids immediately to prevent blindness
    B. Observe and repeat the ESR in 6 weeks
    C. Give cyclophosphamide as first-line monotherapy
    D. Begin warfarin anticoagulation
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Giant Cell/Temporal Arteritis)
   concept: This is giant cell (temporal) arteritis, a granulomatous vasculitis of medium/large arteries in the elderly, presenting with headache, jaw claudication, high ESR, and risk of irreversible blindness from ophthalmic/posterior ciliary artery involvement. High-dose corticosteroids must be started immediately (even before biopsy) to prevent visual loss; biopsy remains positive for a short window and shows skip lesions. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_022 [M] A 45-year-old hepatitis B surface antigen-positive man develops hypertension, abdominal pain, mononeuritis multiplex, and renal impairment; angiography shows microaneurysms of medium-sized arteries, and the lungs are spared. The most likely vasculitis is:
    A. Granulomatosis with polyangiitis
   *B. Polyarteritis nodosa
    C. Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis
    D. Microscopic polyangiitis
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Blood Vessels (Polyarteritis Nodosa)
   concept: Classic polyarteritis nodosa is a necrotizing vasculitis of medium-sized muscular arteries, associated with hepatitis B in about a third of cases, showing transmural fibrinoid necrosis with lesions of different ages and 'rosary-bead' microaneurysms; it characteristically spares the pulmonary arteries and is ANCA-negative, presenting with renovascular hypertension, GI/abdominal pain, and mononeuritis multiplex. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_023 [E] A 32-year-old heavy male smoker presents with painful ischemic ulcers of the toes and fingertips and episodes of migratory superficial thrombophlebitis; angiography shows segmental thrombosing inflammation of small and medium distal extremity arteries. The single most important management step is:

   concept: This is Buerger disease (thromboangiitis obliterans), a segmental thrombosing and inflammatory vasculitis of distal extremity arteries, veins, and adjacent nerves seen almost exclusively in young tobacco users, and strongly tied to smoking (possibly hypersensitivity to tobacco components). Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CVS_024 [H] Which of the following pairings of a vasculitis with its most characteristic ANCA is correct?

Q_c1_019 [H] A patient dies suddenly 5 days after an acute MI with cardiac tamponade found at autopsy. This is due to which complication, occurring at the phase of maximal ventricular wall weakness?

=== Pathology / CNS tumors & basics
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_001 [M] A 45-year-old man presents with new-onset seizures. MRI shows a well-defined, calcified mass in the left frontal cortex. Biopsy reveals sheets of uniform cells with round nuclei surrounded by clear perinuclear halos ('fried-egg' appearance) and a delicate branching ('chicken-wire') capillary network. Which molecular finding in this tumour predicts the best response to chemotherapy?
   *A. Combined 1p/19q codeletion
    B. EGFR gene amplification
    C. Loss of MGMT promoter methylation
    D. Chromosome 22q deletion
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Central Nervous System (Gliomas: Oligodendroglioma)
   concept: Oligodendroglioma classically shows 'fried-egg' cells (perinuclear halo, a formalin-fixation artefact), delicate 'chicken-wire' vasculature, and dystrophic calcification, often frontal with seizures. Combined 1p/19q codeletion (with IDH mutation) defines the tumour and predicts favourable chemosensitivity (PCV/temozolomide) and prognosis. EGFR amplification typifies primary glioblastoma; 22q deletion is seen in meningioma/schwannoma; and it is retention/gain of MGMT methylation, not its loss, that favours temozolomide response.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_002 [M] A 6-year-old child presents with headache, vomiting and ataxia due to obstructive hydrocephalus. A tumour is found filling the fourth ventricle. Histology shows tumour cells arranged around blood vessels with nucleus-free zones of fibrillary processes radiating toward the vessel wall. What is the characteristic histologic structure described?
    A. Homer-Wright rosettes
    B. Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes
   *C. Perivascular pseudorosettes
    D. Verocay bodies
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Central Nervous System (Ependymoma)
   concept: Ependymomas in children arise most often in the fourth ventricle, causing obstructive (non-communicating) hydrocephalus, and characteristically form perivascular pseudorosettes — tumour cells radiating GFAP-positive processes toward a central vessel, creating an anuclear perivascular zone; true ependymal rosettes/canals are less common. Homer-Wright rosettes (neuroblastic, around neuropil) occur in medulloblastoma/neuroblastoma; Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes (true central lumen) in retinoblastoma; Verocay bodies in schwannoma.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_003 [E] A 9-year-old girl has a several-month history of clumsiness and morning headaches. MRI shows a cystic cerebellar mass with an enhancing mural nodule. Histology shows a biphasic pattern of loose and compact areas with bipolar 'hair-like' cells and brightly eosinophilic, corkscrew-shaped condensations. These eosinophilic structures are known as:
    A. Psammoma bodies
   *B. Rosenthal fibres
    C. Negri bodies
    D. Lewy bodies
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Central Nervous System (Pilocytic astrocytoma)
   concept: Pilocytic astrocytoma is the most common glioma of childhood, typically a cystic cerebellar mass with a mural nodule and an indolent WHO grade 1 course. Its hallmark is Rosenthal fibres — thick, eosinophilic, corkscrew-like condensations of GFAP — alongside eosinophilic granular bodies and a biphasic loose/compact architecture; BRAF fusion (KIAA1549-BRAF) is common. Psammoma bodies occur in meningioma, Negri bodies in rabies-infected neurons, and Lewy bodies in Parkinson disease.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_004 [M] A 5-year-old boy develops truncal ataxia and signs of raised intracranial pressure. Imaging shows a midline cerebellar (vermian) mass. Histology reveals sheets of small round blue cells with scant cytoplasm and hyperchromatic nuclei, some arranged around a central tangle of neuropil. The most likely diagnosis is:
    A. Ependymoma
    B. Pilocytic astrocytoma
    C. Hemangioblastoma
   *D. Medulloblastoma
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Central Nervous System (Embryonal/Medulloblastoma)
   concept: Medulloblastoma is a highly malignant WHO grade 4 embryonal tumour of the cerebellar vermis in young children, composed of small round blue cells (high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio) forming Homer-Wright (neuroblastic) rosettes around central neuropil. It disseminates through CSF as 'drop metastases' down the neuraxis. Ependymoma forms perivascular pseudorosettes; pilocytic astrocytoma is cystic with Rosenthal fibres and benign; hemangioblastoma is a vascular tumour of adults with foamy stromal cells.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_005 [H] A 38-year-old man with advanced untreated HIV (CD4 count 40/µL) presents with confusion and focal deficits. MRI shows a periventricular enhancing lesion. CSF and tumour tissue are positive for Epstein-Barr virus, and biopsy shows sheets of atypical lymphoid cells cuffing blood vessels. The most likely diagnosis and cell of origin are:
    A. T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma
    B. Hodgkin lymphoma, nodular sclerosis type
   *C. Primary CNS diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
    D. Burkitt lymphoma, sporadic type
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Central Nervous System (Primary CNS lymphoma)
   concept: Primary CNS lymphoma is the most common CNS neoplasm in immunocompromised (especially AIDS) patients and is almost always an EBV-driven diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. It is typically periventricular/deep and multifocal and shows angiocentric growth — malignant cells cuffing vessels. It is aggressive but steroid-responsive (a 'ghost tumour' that can transiently vanish on steroids). T-lymphoblastic and Hodgkin lymphomas rarely involve the CNS primarily, and sporadic Burkitt is not the classic EBV-associated CNS lymphoma.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_006 [E] A 28-year-old woman presents with amenorrhoea, galactorrhoea and infertility. Serum prolactin is markedly elevated and MRI shows a pituitary microadenoma. This is the most common type of hyperfunctioning pituitary adenoma. From which cell type does it arise?
   *A. Lactotrophs
    B. Corticotrophs
    C. Somatotrophs
    D. Gonadotrophs
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Endocrine System (Pituitary adenomas)
   concept: Prolactinoma (lactotroph adenoma) is the most common functioning pituitary adenoma. Prolactin excess causes amenorrhoea, galactorrhoea, loss of libido and infertility in women, allowing early detection as microadenomas; men present later with mass effects. Dopamine agonists (cabergoline/bromocriptine) shrink them. Corticotroph adenomas cause Cushing disease, somatotroph adenomas cause acromegaly/gigantism, and gonadotroph adenomas are usually clinically silent, presenting via mass effect.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_007 [M] A 50-year-old man reports difficulty seeing objects to either side and repeated collisions with door frames. Perimetry confirms a bitemporal hemianopia, and MRI shows a sellar/suprasellar mass. Compression of which structure produces this visual field defect?
    A. Optic tract
   *B. Optic chiasm (decussating nasal retinal fibres)
    C. Meyer loop in the temporal lobe
    D. Occipital (calcarine) cortex
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Endocrine System (Pituitary mass effects)
   concept: A pituitary macroadenoma expanding out of the sella compresses the overlying optic chiasm, where fibres from the nasal halves of both retinas (carrying the temporal visual fields) decussate; interrupting them produces bitemporal hemianopia. Optic tract lesions cause a contralateral homonymous hemianopia; Meyer loop (temporal) damage gives a 'pie-in-the-sky' superior quadrantanopia; and occipital cortex lesions cause homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_008 [H] A 55-year-old man with a known pituitary macroadenoma suddenly develops a severe ('thunderclap') headache, ophthalmoplegia, acute visual loss and hypotension, and is found to have acute cortisol deficiency. Which process best explains this emergency?
    A. Ischaemic infarction of the postpartum pituitary (Sheehan syndrome)
    B. Autoimmune lymphocytic hypophysitis
    C. Craniopharyngioma cyst rupture spilling motor-oil fluid
   *D. Acute haemorrhage/infarction into the pituitary adenoma (pituitary apoplexy)
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — The Endocrine System (Pituitary apoplexy/hypopituitarism)
   concept: Pituitary apoplexy is sudden haemorrhage or infarction into a (usually pre-existing) pituitary adenoma, causing thunderclap headache, ophthalmoplegia (cavernous sinus cranial nerves), acute visual loss and life-threatening secondary adrenal insufficiency that requires urgent glucocorticoids. Sheehan syndrome is postpartum ischaemic pituitary necrosis with a gradual course; lymphocytic hypophysitis is autoimmune and subacute; and craniopharyngioma fluid is characteristic but does not cause this acute apoplectic syndrome.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_CNS_009 [M] A 10-year-old boy has growth failure and a bitemporal field defect. CT shows a partly cystic, calcified suprasellar mass. At surgery the cyst contains dark, viscous 'machinery-oil' fluid, and histology shows stratified squamous epithelium with peripheral palisading and nodules of 'wet' keratin. This tumour arises from remnants of:
    A. The notochord
    B. Arachnoid cap cells
   *C. Rathke pouch

Vaccination gives isolated anti-HBs with negative anti-HBc (A wrong). A chronic carrier has persistent HBsAg (B wrong). Resolved past infection shows anti-HBs plus IgG (not IgM) anti-HBc (D wrong).
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_012 [E] The presence of HBeAg in a patient with hepatitis B infection primarily indicates:
    A. Recovery and lifelong immunity
    B. A successful response to vaccination
    C. Coexisting hepatitis D infection
   *D. Active viral replication and high infectivity
   key=D ref=Harsh Mohan Textbook of Pathology, 8th ed — The Liver, Biliary Tract and Exocrine Pancreas (hepatitis B markers)
   concept: HBeAg is a secreted protein derived from the precore/core region and is a marker of active HBV replication and high infectivity (high viral load). Seroconversion from HBeAg to anti-HBe signals reduced replication and lower infectivity. Immunity is indicated by anti-HBs, vaccination gives anti-HBs alone, and hepatitis D requires separate anti-HDV/HDV-RNA testing — so A, B and C are incorrect.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_013 [M] A healthcare worker's hepatitis B panel shows: HBsAg negative, anti-HBc (total) negative, anti-HBs positive. The correct interpretation is:
   *A. Immunity due to prior hepatitis B vaccination

    C. Acute hepatitis B in the window period
    D. Chronic inactive carrier state
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (interpretation of hepatitis B serology)
   concept: Isolated anti-HBs positivity with NEGATIVE anti-HBc indicates vaccine-induced immunity: the recombinant vaccine contains only HBsAg, so no anti-core antibody is ever generated. Resolved natural infection would show anti-HBs PLUS anti-HBc (IgG), so B is wrong. The window period is defined by IgM anti-HBc positivity, and a chronic carrier has persistent HBsAg — so C and D are excluded.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_014 [M] Compared with the other hepatitis viruses, hepatitis C virus (HCV) is MOST notable for:
    A. Being a DNA virus preventable by an effective vaccine
   *B. The highest rate of progression to chronic infection, with substantial risk of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma
    C. Exclusive faecal-oral transmission with no chronicity
    D. Causing infection only in the presence of hepatitis B
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (hepatitis C, chronicity and HCC)
   concept: HCV is an RNA flavivirus, chiefly bloodborne, and has the highest chronicity rate of the hepatitis viruses (roughly 50-85% become chronic), driving progression to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. There is no HCV vaccine because of its high genomic variability/quasispecies, so A is wrong. Faecal-oral, non-chronic spread describes HAV/HEV (C), and dependence on HBV is the feature of the defective HDV (D).
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_015 [H] Assertion (A): Hepatitis D superinfection of a chronic hepatitis B carrier tends to be more severe — with higher risk of fulminant hepatitis or accelerated cirrhosis — than simultaneous HBV-HDV coinfection.     C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (hepatitis D coinfection versus superinfection)
   concept: Both statements are individually true: HDV is a defective virus needing HBsAg to assemble and replicate (R true), and superinfection of an established HBV carrier is indeed more severe than co-infection (A true). Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_016 [M] Councilman (acidophil) bodies seen on liver biopsy in acute viral hepatitis represent:
    A. Intracellular accumulation of hepatitis B surface antigen (ground-glass change)

   *C. Apoptotic hepatocytes that have shrunk into rounded, intensely eosinophilic bodies
    D. Iron-laden Kupffer cells
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (histology of viral hepatitis)
   concept: Councilman/apoptotic (acidophil) bodies are individual hepatocytes dying by apoptosis — they shrink into rounded, deeply eosinophilic (acidophilic) globes and are a hallmark of viral hepatitis. Ground-glass hepatocytes reflect cytoplasmic HBsAg accumulation (chronic HBV); Mallory-Denk bodies are damaged keratin intermediate filaments seen in alcoholic/NASH hepatitis; iron-laden Kupffer cells indicate haemosiderosis. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_017 [E] In a patient with a long history of heavy alcohol use and deranged liver enzymes, which biochemical pattern is MOST characteristic of alcoholic liver disease?
   *A. AST elevated to about twice ALT (AST:ALT ratio >2:1), usually with values below 500 U/L
    B. ALT elevated far more than AST (ratio well below 1)
    C. Both transaminases persistently above 2000 U/L
    D. Normal transaminases with an isolated very high alkaline phosphatase
   key=A ref=Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed — Alcoholic Liver Disease (AST:ALT ratio)
   concept: Alcoholic liver disease classically shows an AST:ALT ratio greater than 2:1, with transaminases usually only modestly raised (below ~500 U/L). This is partly because alcohol depletes pyridoxal-5-phosphate needed for ALT synthesis and causes release of mitochondrial AST. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_018 [M] A 48-year-old obese man with type 2 diabetes and dyslipidaemia (denies significant alcohol use) has hepatic steatosis on ultrasound and mildly raised ALT.    concept: NAFLD/NASH is strongly linked to the metabolic syndrome (obesity, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia). Its histology — steatosis, ballooning degeneration, Mallory-Denk bodies and neutrophils — is essentially indistinguishable from alcoholic steatohepatitis, but occurs in the ABSENCE of significant alcohol intake, and can progress to cirrhosis. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_019 [H] Which pairing of cirrhosis morphology with its typical cause is CORRECT?

   concept: Micronodular cirrhosis (uniform nodules under ~3 mm) is classically caused by alcohol (also haemochromatosis and biliary disease), where ongoing injury and metabolic suppression limit regeneration. Macronodular cirrhosis (variably sized nodules over 3 mm) typifies chronic viral hepatitis B/C and Wilson disease. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_020 [E] A 52-year-old man presents with skin hyperpigmentation ('bronze' colour), new-onset diabetes mellitus and hepatomegaly. Which laboratory profile and gene are MOST consistent with hereditary haemochromatosis?

   concept: Hereditary haemochromatosis, most often from HFE C282Y homozygosity, causes systemic iron overload — raised serum ferritin and high transferrin saturation. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_021 [H] A 19-year-old with hepatitis and a new resting tremor is being evaluated for Wilson disease. Which combination of laboratory findings best supports the diagnosis?

    C. Increased serum ceruloplasmin with increased total serum copper
    D. Normal ceruloplasmin with a high transferrin saturation
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (Wilson disease, copper studies)
   concept: In Wilson disease the defective ATP7B impairs both incorporation of copper into ceruloplasmin and biliary copper excretion. This lowers serum ceruloplasmin and lowers TOTAL serum copper (most circulating copper is ceruloplasmin-bound), yet the FREE non-bound copper rises and spills into urine, so 24-hour urinary copper is increased; tissue deposition causes Kayser-Fleischer rings and hepatic/neuropsychiatric disease. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_022 [M] A young non-smoking adult with early panacinar (basal) emphysema is found to have chronic liver disease. Liver biopsy shows cytoplasmic globules in periportal hepatocytes that are PAS-positive and diastase-resistant. The diagnosis is:
    A. Wilson disease
   *B. Alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency
    C. Hereditary haemochromatosis
    D. Primary sclerosing cholangitis
   key=B ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency)
   concept: Alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency (classically the PiZZ genotype) causes accumulation of misfolded AAT within hepatocytes, forming cytoplasmic globules that are PAS-positive and diastase (PAS-D)-resistant, with associated liver disease/neonatal cholestasis. Reduced circulating AAT leaves neutrophil elastase unopposed, producing early panacinar emphysema, especially in non-smokers presenting young. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_023 [M] A 50-year-old woman presents with fatigue, pruritus and xanthelasma. Alkaline phosphatase is markedly elevated and anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA) is positive. Biopsy shows granulomatous destruction of small intrahepatic bile ducts. The diagnosis is:
    A. Autoimmune hepatitis type 1
    B. Primary sclerosing cholangitis
   *C. Primary biliary cholangitis
    D. Alcoholic hepatitis
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed — Liver and Gallbladder (primary biliary cholangitis vs primary sclerosing cholangitis)
   concept: Primary biliary cholangitis typically affects middle-aged women with fatigue, pruritus and a cholestatic profile (high ALP), and is characterised by AMA positivity (anti-M2, highly specific) with granulomatous destruction of small/medium intrahepatic bile ducts (the florid duct lesion). Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_LIVER_024 [M] The MOST common malignant tumour involving the liver overall is:
    A. Hepatocellular carcinoma
    B. Cholangiocarcinoma
    C. Hepatic haemangioma
   *D. Metastatic carcinoma (e.g., from colon, breast or lung)

   concept: Metastatic tumours far outnumber primary liver malignancies; the liver is one of the commonest sites of metastasis, especially from colon, breast, lung and pancreas, classically producing multiple nodules with central umbilication. Q_c1_023 [M] A patient has painless progressive jaundice, dark urine, pale (clay-colored) stools, pruritus and markedly elevated alkaline phosphatase. This pattern indicates which type of jaundice?

Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_001 [M] A 24-year-old man develops an episode of visible (gross) hematuria within 1-2 days of an upper respiratory tract infection, and similar episodes recur with subsequent infections. Renal biopsy immunofluorescence shows mesangial deposits of which immunoglobulin?
   *A. IgA
    B. IgG
    C. IgM
    D. IgE
   key=A ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease — The Kidney
   concept: IgA nephropathy (Berger disease) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_002 [M] A 50-year-old man has nephrotic-range proteinuria, and renal biopsy shows diffuse thickening of the glomerular capillary wall with subepithelial deposits producing a 'spike and dome' pattern on silver stain.    concept: Primary membranous nephropathy is a leading cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults and older Caucasians; roughly 70% are driven by autoantibodies to the podocyte antigen PLA2R. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_003 [M] A 40-year-old African-American man with HIV infection develops heavy proteinuria and full nephrotic syndrome.    concept: Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) is a leading cause of nephrotic syndrome in adults, especially in people of African descent and in HIV, heroin use, sickle cell and obesity. Light microscopy shows sclerosis of segments of some glomeruli; unlike minimal change disease it causes non-selective proteinuria and often responds poorly to steroids.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_004 [H] A 28-year-old woman with a malar rash, arthralgia and positive anti-dsDNA develops hematuria and proteinuria.    concept: Class IV diffuse proliferative lupus nephritis is the most common and most severe form. Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_005 [H] A young adult presents with a mixed nephrotic-nephritic picture and persistently low serum C3.    concept: Membranoproliferative GN presents with a combined nephrotic/nephritic picture and low complement. Duplication/splitting of the GBM from mesangial cell interposition and new basement-membrane synthesis gives the classic 'tram-track' double-contour appearance on silver/PAS stain.
Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_006 [M] A 12-year-old boy has recurrent hematuria, progressive sensorineural hearing loss and anterior lenticonus.    concept: Alport syndrome is a hereditary nephritis, most often X-linked, caused by mutations in type IV collagen (alpha-3/4/5 chains).    concept: Thin basement membrane disease (benign familial hematuria) causes lifelong isolated microscopic hematuria with an excellent prognosis; EM shows diffusely thinned GBM (roughly 150-250 nm versus the normal ~350-400 nm). Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_008 [H] A 60-year-old man presents with rapidly progressive renal failure and hemoptysis. Renal biopsy shows crescentic glomerulonephritis, but immunofluorescence reveals NO immune deposits (pauci-immune). Which serologic test is most likely to be positive?

Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_009 [M] A patient develops acute kidney injury after prolonged hypotension from septic shock. Urinalysis shows muddy-brown granular casts and renal tubular epithelial cells. The most likely diagnosis is:

Q_GEN_PATH_SYS_RENAL_013 [E] Which combination of findings best characterizes the NEPHRITIC syndrome, as opposed to the nephrotic syndrome?

Q_GEN_PATH_HEM_LYMPHOMA_001 [M] A 24-year-old woman presents with an anterior mediastinal mass and lower cervical lymphadenopathy. Q_GEN_PATH_HEM_LYMPHOMA_021 [H] Which finding most reliably distinguishes follicular lymphoma from reactive (benign) follicular hyperplasia on a lymph node biopsy?
    A. Preservation of nodal architecture with well-defined mantle zones
    B. Prominent tingible-body macrophages within the germinal centers
   *C. BCL2 expression by follicle-center cells together with immunoglobulin light-chain restriction (monoclonality)
    D. Marked variation in follicle size and shape
   key=C ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Diseases of White Blood Cells, Lymph Nodes, and Spleen (Follicular Lymphoma vs Reactive Hyperplasia)
   concept: Follicular lymphoma is distinguished from reactive follicular hyperplasia by BCL2 protein expression within follicle-center cells (consequence of t(14;18)) together with monoclonality shown by immunoglobulin light-chain restriction. Reactive germinal centers are BCL2-negative and polyclonal, retain polarity and well-defined mantle zones, contain abundant tingible-body macrophages, and show variation in follicle size and shape — all features of a benign, antigen-driven response. Thus options A, B and D favor a reactive process, not lymphoma.
Q_GEN_PATH_HEM_LYMPHOMA_022 [M] The International Prognostic Index (IPI) used to predict outcome in aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma incorporates all of the following adverse factors EXCEPT:
    A. Patient age over 60 years and poor performance status
    B. Elevated serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)
    C. Advanced Ann Arbor stage and more than one extranodal site
   *D. ABO blood group
   key=D ref=Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease, 10th ed. — Diseases of White Blood Cells, Lymph Nodes, and Spleen (Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma, Prognosis / IPI)
   concept: The International Prognostic Index (IPI) for aggressive NHL uses five adverse factors, each scoring one point: Age over 60 years, elevated serum LDH, ECOG performance status of 2 or worse, Ann Arbor stage III/IV, and more than one extranodal site of disease.
