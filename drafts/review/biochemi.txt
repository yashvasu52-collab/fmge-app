
=== Biochemistry / Amino acid metabolism & urea cycle
Q_A_026 [M] Transamination reactions that transfer amino groups between amino acids and keto acids require which vitamin-derived coenzyme?
    A. Thiamine pyrophosphate (B1)
   *B. Pyridoxal phosphate (B6)
    C. Biotin (B7)
    D. Cobalamin (B12)
   key=B ref=Vasudevan, Textbook of Biochemistry
   concept: Aminotransferases (e.g., ALT, AST) use pyridoxal phosphate (PLP), the active form of vitamin B6, as coenzyme for transamination. B6 deficiency (e.g., with isoniazid) impairs these reactions.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_001 [E] Which enzyme of the urea cycle catalyzes the reaction that directly yields urea?
   *A. Arginase
    B. Argininosuccinate synthetase
    C. Ornithine transcarbamoylase
    D. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — The Urea Cycle (Catabolism of Proteins & of Amino Acid Nitrogen)
   concept: Arginase catalyzes the fifth and final urea-cycle step, hydrolysing arginine into urea plus ornithine, and ornithine re-enters the mitochondrion to keep the cycle turning. CPS-I and ornithine transcarbamoylase form carbamoyl phosphate and citrulline, while argininosuccinate synthetase makes argininosuccinate; none of these liberate free urea, so arginase is the sole direct urea-generating enzyme.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_002 [M] A neonate develops lethargy, poor feeding and hyperammonemia; plasma citrulline is markedly elevated (>1000 µmol/L) while orotic acid is not raised. Deficiency of which enzyme is most likely?
    A. Ornithine transcarbamoylase
    B. Arginase
    C. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I
   *D. Argininosuccinate synthetase
   key=D ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Chapter 19: Removal of Nitrogen from Amino Acids / Urea Cycle Disorders
   concept: Argininosuccinate synthetase condenses citrulline with aspartate, so its deficiency (citrullinemia type I) blocks the cycle just after citrulline and plasma citrulline rises markedly, often above 1000 µmol/L, with hyperammonemia. OTC deficiency instead causes low citrulline with high orotic acid; CPS-I deficiency gives low citrulline and low orotate; arginase deficiency raises arginine — so grossly elevated citrulline is the decisive clue.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_003 [M] An infant with hyperammonemia has abnormally brittle hair that fractures easily (trichorrhexis nodosa) and greatly elevated argininosuccinate in blood and urine. Which enzyme is deficient?
    A. Argininosuccinate synthetase
    B. Arginase
   *C. Argininosuccinate lyase
    D. Ornithine transcarbamoylase
   key=C ref=Vasudevan — Textbook of Biochemistry for Medical Students — Urea Cycle and Metabolism of Ammonia
   concept: Argininosuccinate lyase cleaves argininosuccinate into arginine and fumarate; its deficiency (argininosuccinic aciduria) causes accumulation of argininosuccinate in blood and urine with hyperammonemia, and the characteristic brittle, easily fractured hair (trichorrhexis nodosa) attributed to arginine deficiency. OTC deficiency raises orotate, argininosuccinate synthetase deficiency raises citrulline, and arginase deficiency raises arginine — elevated argininosuccinate is diagnostic here.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_004 [H] Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I, the committed step of the urea cycle, is absolutely dependent on which allosteric activator?
   *A. N-acetylglutamate
    B. Guanosine triphosphate
    C. Aspartate
    D. Fumarate
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — The Urea Cycle: Regulation and N-Acetylglutamate
   concept: Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I, the rate-limiting mitochondrial entry enzyme of the urea cycle, is obligately dependent on the allosteric activator N-acetylglutamate, which is synthesized from glutamate and acetyl-CoA by N-acetylglutamate synthase and is itself stimulated by arginine. Aspartate enters later via argininosuccinate synthetase, fumarate is a downstream product, and GTP is unrelated — only N-acetylglutamate activates CPS-I.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_005 [M] A 3-year-old child has progressive spastic diplegia, developmental regression and only episodic mild hyperammonemia, with elevated plasma arginine. Which urea-cycle enzyme is most likely deficient?
    A. Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency
   *B. Arginase deficiency (argininemia)
    C. Ornithine transcarbamoylase deficiency
    D. Argininosuccinate synthetase deficiency (citrullinemia)
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Chapter 19: Urea Cycle Disorders
   concept: Arginase deficiency (argininemia) is atypical among urea-cycle defects: rather than catastrophic neonatal hyperammonemia it usually presents later with progressive spastic diplegia or tetraplegia, developmental regression and seizures, raised plasma arginine, and only episodic mild hyperammonemia. CPS-I, OTC and citrullinemia typically manifest as severe neonatal encephalopathy, distinguishing them from this milder, spasticity-dominant presentation.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_006 [M] How does sodium benzoate lower blood ammonia in a child with a urea-cycle disorder?
    A. It converts ammonia directly into urea
    B. It inhibits intestinal urease-producing bacteria
    C. It chelates ammonia directly in the blood
   *D. It conjugates with glycine to form hippurate, which is excreted in urine
   key=D ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Metabolic Disorders of the Urea Cycle and Their Treatment
   concept: Sodium benzoate opens a nitrogen-disposal route that bypasses the defective urea cycle: it conjugates with glycine to form hippuric acid, which is excreted renally, removing one nitrogen per glycine molecule. Sodium phenylacetate/phenylbutyrate similarly conjugate glutamine to phenylacetylglutamine. These agents do not synthesize urea, kill bacteria, or directly chelate ammonia — the mechanism is amino-acid conjugation followed by urinary excretion.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_007 [E] By which mechanism does oral lactulose reduce blood ammonia in hepatic encephalopathy?
    A. It neutralizes gastric acid
    B. It acts as a systemic antibiotic killing urease-producing bacteria
   *C. It acidifies the colonic lumen, trapping ammonia as non-absorbable ammonium for excretion
    D. It stimulates hepatic urea synthesis
   key=C ref=Vasudevan — Textbook of Biochemistry for Medical Students — Metabolism of Ammonia and Hepatic Coma
   concept: Lactulose, a non-absorbable disaccharide, is fermented by colonic bacteria to lactic and acetic acids that acidify the lumen; this shifts NH3 to non-absorbable ammonium (NH4+), which is trapped and excreted, while its cathartic effect and reduction of ammoniagenic flora further lower ammonia in hepatic encephalopathy. It is not an antibiotic, an antacid, or a stimulator of hepatic urea synthesis.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_008 [M] Which compound is the principal non-toxic transport form of ammonia in the blood?
   *A. Glutamine
    B. Alanine
    C. Aspartate
    D. Urea
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Catabolism of Proteins & of Amino Acid Nitrogen (Ammonia Transport)
   concept: Glutamine is the principal non-toxic transport form of ammonia in blood; glutamine synthetase incorporates free NH3 into glutamate to form glutamine (especially important in brain and muscle), which is carried to liver and kidney where glutaminase releases the ammonia. Alanine also shuttles nitrogen but chiefly via the glucose-alanine cycle, aspartate donates nitrogen within the urea cycle, and urea is the excretory end-product rather than a transport form.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_009 [H] Assertion (A): Glutamate dehydrogenase is the principal enzyme that liberates free ammonia from amino-acid nitrogen in the liver. Reason (R): Glutamate dehydrogenase catalyzes oxidative deamination of glutamate and can use either NAD+ or NADP+ as its coenzyme. Select the correct option:
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Catabolism of Proteins & of Amino Acid Nitrogen (Oxidative Deamination)
   concept: Glutamate dehydrogenase is the major enzyme performing oxidative deamination: amino groups are first funnelled onto α-ketoglutarate to form glutamate by transamination, then glutamate dehydrogenase releases free ammonia and regenerates α-ketoglutarate, uniquely using either NAD+ or NADP+. It is inhibited by GTP/ATP and activated by ADP/GDP. Both statements are true and the reason correctly explains the mechanism by which ammonia is liberated, so the answer is A.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_010 [M] In the glucose-alanine cycle, which amino acid carries amino-group nitrogen from skeletal muscle to the liver?
    A. Glutamine
    B. Aspartate
    C. Glycine
   *D. Alanine
   key=D ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Chapter 19: The Glucose-Alanine Cycle
   concept: In the glucose-alanine cycle, muscle transaminates pyruvate to alanine using amino groups derived from amino-acid catabolism; alanine travels to the liver, where it is transaminated back to pyruvate for gluconeogenesis while its nitrogen enters the urea cycle. Glutamine is the other major nitrogen carrier but is not the muscle-specific glucogenic shuttle, and aspartate and glycine do not serve this transport role.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_011 [E] Which pair of amino acids is exclusively (purely) ketogenic?
    A. Leucine and isoleucine
   *B. Leucine and lysine
    C. Lysine and valine
    D. Leucine and valine
   key=B ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Catabolism of the Carbon Skeletons of Amino Acids
   concept: Leucine and lysine are the only two amino acids that are purely (exclusively) ketogenic — their carbon skeletons yield only acetyl-CoA/acetoacetate and cannot be used to make glucose. Isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan and threonine are both glucogenic and ketogenic, whereas valine is purely glucogenic; therefore only the leucine-plus-lysine pairing is entirely ketogenic.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_012 [M] Which of the following amino acids is both glucogenic and ketogenic?
    A. Alanine
    B. Leucine
   *C. Isoleucine
    D. Valine
   key=C ref=Vasudevan — Textbook of Biochemistry for Medical Students — Glucogenic and Ketogenic Amino Acids
   concept: Isoleucine is both glucogenic and ketogenic because its skeleton yields acetyl-CoA (ketogenic) and propionyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA (glucogenic). Leucine is purely ketogenic, whereas alanine and valine are purely glucogenic. The classic five amino acids that are both glucogenic and ketogenic are isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan and threonine, so isoleucine is the correct dual-fate answer here.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_013 [E] Which of the following is a nutritionally essential (indispensable) amino acid?
    A. Alanine
    B. Serine
    C. Glutamate
   *D. Methionine
   key=D ref=Satyanarayana — Biochemistry — Metabolism of Amino Acids (Essential and Non-essential Amino Acids)
   concept: Methionine is a nutritionally essential (indispensable) amino acid that must be supplied in the diet because the human body cannot synthesize its carbon skeleton. The essentials are commonly memorized as PVT TIM HALL — phenylalanine, valine, threonine, tryptophan, isoleucine, methionine, histidine, arginine (semi-essential), leucine and lysine. Alanine, serine and glutamate are non-essential and synthesized endogenously, so methionine is the only essential option listed.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_014 [M] An infant with phenylketonuria is placed on a phenylalanine-restricted diet, yet develops seizures and progressive neurological deterioration despite normal blood phenylalanine. What is the most likely underlying cause?
   *A. Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) deficiency
    B. Continued dietary non-compliance
    C. Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) deficiency
    D. Dietary tyrosine deficiency
   key=A ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Phenylketonuria and Tetrahydrobiopterin
   concept: Persisting neurological deterioration despite a phenylalanine-restricted diet and normal blood phenylalanine points to tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) deficiency, so-called malignant PKU, from defects in BH4 synthesis or dihydropteridine reductase. BH4 is the cofactor not only for phenylalanine hydroxylase but also for tyrosine and tryptophan hydroxylases, so its lack cripples dopamine and serotonin synthesis; treatment must add BH4 plus L-DOPA and 5-HTP, not diet alone.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_015 [H] A 45-year-old man notes that his urine turns black on standing and has bluish-black discoloration of the ear cartilage and sclerae with degenerative spinal arthritis. Deficiency of which enzyme is responsible?
    A. Tyrosine aminotransferase
   *B. Homogentisate oxidase (homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase)
    C. Phenylalanine hydroxylase
    D. Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase
   key=B ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Catabolism of the Carbon Skeletons of Amino Acids (Alkaptonuria)
   concept: Alkaptonuria is a deficiency of homogentisate oxidase (homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase) in the tyrosine degradation pathway, causing accumulation of homogentisic acid. Urine darkens on standing or alkalinization, and oxidized pigment deposits in cartilage and connective tissue (ochronosis), producing bluish-black ears and sclerae with a degenerative arthropathy of the spine. Phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency causes PKU, and fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase deficiency causes tyrosinemia type I.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_016 [M] A neonate has poor feeding, vomiting and lethargy, and the urine smells like maple syrup; branched-chain amino acids and their keto acids are elevated. Which enzyme is deficient?
    A. Branched-chain aminotransferase
    B. Propionyl-CoA carboxylase
   *C. Branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase
    D. Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase
   key=C ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Maple Syrup Urine Disease
   concept: Maple syrup urine disease results from deficiency of the branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex, which blocks oxidative decarboxylation of the keto acids of leucine, isoleucine and valine. These branched-chain amino acids and their keto acids accumulate, producing the sweet maple-syrup odour of urine and severe neonatal neurotoxicity; the complex uses thiamine (TPP), lipoate, FAD, NAD and CoA, and some cases are thiamine-responsive.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_017 [H] A young adult with recurrent renal stones has hexagonal crystals in the urine and a positive cyanide-nitroprusside test. Which defect best explains this?
    A. Lysosomal accumulation of cystine within cells
    B. Deficiency of cystathionine β-synthase
    C. Deficiency of cysteine dioxygenase
   *D. Defective renal and intestinal transport of cystine, ornithine, lysine and arginine
   key=D ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Disorders of Amino Acid Transport (Cystinuria)
   concept: Cystinuria is an inherited defect of the renal tubular and intestinal transporter for the dibasic amino acids cystine, ornithine, lysine and arginine (mnemonic COLA); poorly soluble cystine precipitates as characteristic hexagonal crystals and recurrent radio-opaque calculi, with a positive cyanide-nitroprusside test. It must be distinguished from cystinosis, a lysosomal cystine-storage disease causing renal Fanconi syndrome rather than stones, and from cystathionine β-synthase deficiency (homocystinuria).
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_018 [M] Which enzyme catalyzes the rate-limiting step of catecholamine biosynthesis?
    A. DOPA decarboxylase
    B. Dopamine β-hydroxylase
   *C. Tyrosine hydroxylase
    D. Phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase
   key=C ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Conversion of Amino Acids to Specialized Products (Catecholamines)
   concept: Tyrosine hydroxylase catalyzes the rate-limiting step of catecholamine synthesis, converting tyrosine to L-DOPA using tetrahydrobiopterin; L-DOPA is then decarboxylated to dopamine, hydroxylated to noradrenaline by dopamine β-hydroxylase, and N-methylated to adrenaline by phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase. Because tyrosine hydroxylase controls flux into the whole pathway, it is the pace-setting enzyme, not the downstream steps.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_019 [E] Serotonin and melatonin are synthesized from which amino acid?
    A. Tyrosine
   *B. Tryptophan
    C. Histidine
    D. Glutamate
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Amino Acid-Derived Products (Serotonin and Melatonin)
   concept: Tryptophan is the precursor of serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine): tryptophan hydroxylase forms 5-hydroxytryptophan, which is decarboxylated to serotonin, and in the pineal gland serotonin is acetylated and methylated to melatonin. Tyrosine gives rise to catecholamines, histidine yields histamine, and glutamate yields GABA — only tryptophan produces both serotonin and melatonin.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_020 [M] A patient with Hartnup disease develops a pellagra-like photosensitive dermatitis. Reduced endogenous synthesis of which vitamin best explains this?
   *A. Niacin
    B. Riboflavin
    C. Thiamine
    D. Folate
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Conversion of Amino Acids to Specialized Products (Tryptophan and Niacin)
   concept: Tryptophan is converted via the kynurenine pathway to niacin/NAD, with roughly 60 mg tryptophan yielding about 1 mg niacin. In Hartnup disease the intestinal and renal transport of neutral amino acids including tryptophan is impaired, so less tryptophan is available for niacin synthesis and a pellagra-like dermatitis, diarrhoea and neurological signs appear. The deficient vitamin synthesizable from tryptophan is therefore niacin, not riboflavin, thiamine or folate.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_021 [H] Assertion (A): S-adenosylmethionine is the body's principal donor of methyl groups. Reason (R): S-adenosylmethionine is synthesized from methionine and ATP. Select the correct option:
    A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
   *B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=B ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Conversion of Amino Acids to Specialized Products (S-Adenosylmethionine)
   concept: S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), formed from methionine and ATP, is indeed the body's principal methyl donor in reactions such as synthesis of creatine, phosphatidylcholine, adrenaline and in DNA methylation, becoming S-adenosylhomocysteine after methyl transfer. However, the reason given describes only its formation, not why it donates methyl groups (which is the high-energy, reactive methyl-sulfonium bond); hence both statements are true but the reason does not explain the assertion, so the answer is B.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_022 [H] Remethylation of homocysteine to methionine by methionine synthase requires which pair of cofactors?
    A. Pyridoxal phosphate and folate
    B. Biotin and cobalamin
   *C. Methylcobalamin and N5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate
    D. FAD and NAD
   key=C ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Sulfur-Containing Amino Acids; Homocysteine, Folate and B12
   concept: Remethylation of homocysteine to methionine is catalyzed by methionine synthase (homocysteine methyltransferase), which requires methylcobalamin (vitamin B12) as the enzyme cofactor and N5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate as the methyl donor, linking folate and B12 metabolism. B12 deficiency traps folate as methyl-THF (the methyl-folate trap) and raises both homocysteine and methylmalonic acid; pyridoxal phosphate instead serves the alternative transsulfuration route, not remethylation.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_023 [H] Assertion (A): Glutathione is a tripeptide containing an atypical peptide linkage. Reason (R): In glutathione the glutamate residue is joined to cysteine through its γ-carboxyl group rather than its α-carboxyl group. Select the correct option:
   *A. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    B. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    C. A is true but R is false
    D. A is false but R is true
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Conversion of Amino Acids to Specialized Products (Glutathione)
   concept: Glutathione is the tripeptide γ-glutamyl-cysteinyl-glycine and the major intracellular antioxidant, reduced by glutathione reductase using NADPH from the HMP shunt. It contains an atypical peptide bond precisely because glutamate is joined to cysteine through its γ-carboxyl group rather than its α-carboxyl group, which protects it from ordinary peptidases. Both statements are true and the γ-linkage is exactly what makes the bond atypical, so the reason correctly explains the assertion.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_024 [M] Which reaction is the major source of one-carbon units as N5,N10-methylene-tetrahydrofolate?
    A. β-oxidation of fatty acids
   *B. Conversion of serine to glycine by serine hydroxymethyltransferase
    C. Oxidative deamination of glutamate
    D. Decarboxylation of histidine
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Folic Acid and One-Carbon Metabolism
   concept: The largest source of one-carbon units is the conversion of serine to glycine by serine hydroxymethyltransferase, a pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent enzyme that transfers serine's β-carbon to tetrahydrofolate, generating N5,N10-methylene-THF. This folate-bound one-carbon pool feeds thymidylate, purine and methionine synthesis. β-oxidation of fatty acids, oxidative deamination of glutamate and decarboxylation of histidine do not supply the methylene-THF pool.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_AA_025 [H] Creatine is synthesized in the body from which three amino acids?
    A. Glycine, serine and cysteine
    B. Arginine, ornithine and lysine
    C. Glutamate, glycine and cysteine
   *D. Glycine, arginine and methionine
   key=D ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Conversion of Amino Acids to Specialized Products (Creatine and Creatinine)
   concept: Creatine is synthesized from three amino acids: the kidney enzyme arginine-glycine amidinotransferase joins arginine and glycine to form guanidinoacetate (releasing ornithine), and the liver then methylates guanidinoacetate using S-adenosylmethionine derived from methionine to yield creatine. Creatine is phosphorylated to phosphocreatine in muscle and non-enzymatically cyclizes to creatinine, which is excreted. The correct trio is therefore glycine, arginine and methionine.

=== Biochemistry / DNA/RNA, replication, mutations
Q_A_030 [M] Insertion or deletion of a single nucleotide (not a multiple of three) in a coding region produces which type of mutation?
    A. Silent mutation
    B. Missense mutation
   *C. Frameshift mutation
    D. Transition point mutation
   key=C ref=Vasudevan, Textbook of Biochemistry
   concept: Adding or deleting nucleotides not in multiples of three shifts the reading frame (frameshift), altering all downstream codons and often creating a premature stop codon. Point mutations may be silent, missense or nonsense.

=== Biochemistry / Glycolysis, TCA, gluconeogenesis (key enzymes)
Q_A_023 [M] Which enzyme is unique to gluconeogenesis and converts oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate, bypassing the irreversible pyruvate kinase step?
    A. Pyruvate carboxylase
   *B. Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK)
    C. Glucokinase
    D. Aldolase
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry
   concept: Gluconeogenesis bypasses the three irreversible glycolytic steps using pyruvate carboxylase, PEPCK (oxaloacetate to PEP), fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase and glucose-6-phosphatase.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_001 [E] Which statement correctly distinguishes hepatic glucokinase from hexokinase?
    A. Glucokinase has a low Km for glucose and is strongly inhibited by its product glucose-6-phosphate
   *B. Glucokinase has a high Km for glucose and is not inhibited by physiological levels of glucose-6-phosphate
    C. Glucokinase is present in all tissues, whereas hexokinase is confined to the liver
    D. Glucokinase phosphorylates glucose only during hypoglycaemia
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Chapter 8: Glycolysis (Hexokinase vs Glucokinase)
   concept: Glucokinase (hexokinase IV) has a high Km (~10 mmol/L), so it is only active after a carbohydrate meal, has a high Vmax, is induced by insulin, and is NOT inhibited by glucose-6-phosphate (it is regulated by GKRP). Hexokinase I-III are ubiquitous, have a low Km (~0.1 mmol/L) so are saturated at normal glucose, and are product-inhibited by G6P. Option A actually describes hexokinase; C reverses the distributions (hexokinase is ubiquitous); D is wrong because glucokinase acts postprandially when glucose is high, not in hypoglycaemia.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_002 [M] The single most potent allosteric activator of phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) in the liver is:
   *A. Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate
    B. Citrate
    C. ATP
    D. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Chapter 18: Glycolysis & the Oxidation of Pyruvate (Regulation of PFK-1)
   concept: Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate, produced by the kinase activity of PFK-2 in the fed/insulin state, is the most potent allosteric activator of PFK-1; it raises the enzyme's affinity for fructose-6-phosphate and overrides ATP-mediated inhibition. AMP also activates PFK-1. Citrate and ATP are inhibitors, signalling an energy-rich cell (high citrate = abundant biosynthetic precursors). Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate is the PRODUCT of PFK-1 (it feed-forward activates pyruvate kinase, not PFK-1), so it is not the activator asked for.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_003 [M] In glycolysis, ATP is generated by substrate-level phosphorylation at the reactions catalysed by:
    A. Hexokinase and phosphofructokinase-1
    B. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase and enolase
   *C. Phosphoglycerate kinase and pyruvate kinase
    D. Aldolase and triose phosphate isomerase
   key=C ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Chapter 18: Glycolysis & the Oxidation of Pyruvate
   concept: Substrate-level phosphorylation means ATP is formed by direct transfer of a high-energy phosphate from a substrate to ADP. In glycolysis this occurs twice: phosphoglycerate kinase (1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to 3-phosphoglycerate) and pyruvate kinase (phosphoenolpyruvate to pyruvate). Hexokinase and PFK-1 CONSUME ATP. GAPDH creates the high-energy 1,3-BPG and NADH but does not itself make ATP, and enolase only forms PEP; aldolase and triose phosphate isomerase are cleavage/isomerisation steps with no ATP change.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_004 [E] The net ATP yield from the anaerobic conversion of one molecule of glucose to lactate is:
    A. 0 ATP
   *B. 2 ATP
    C. 6 ATP
    D. 8 ATP
   key=B ref=Lippincott's Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry, 7th ed. — Chapter 8: Glycolysis (Energy yield)
   concept: In anaerobic glycolysis, 2 ATP are invested (hexokinase and PFK-1) and 4 ATP are produced (two phosphoglycerate kinase and two pyruvate kinase reactions per glucose), giving a NET of 2 ATP. The NADH made at the GAPDH step is re-oxidised by lactate dehydrogenase (pyruvate to lactate) to regenerate NAD+, so it yields no additional ATP anaerobically. Complete aerobic oxidation yields roughly 30-32 ATP. Option A ignores the two net ATP; 6 and 8 overestimate the anaerobic yield.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_005 [H] Arsenate (a pentavalent arsenic compound) reduces the net ATP yield of glycolysis without stopping the pathway because it:
    A. Irreversibly inhibits enolase so 2-phosphoglycerate accumulates
    B. Blocks lactate dehydrogenase and traps NADH
    C. Chelates the Mg2+ ion required by pyruvate kinase
   *D. Substitutes for inorganic phosphate at the GAPDH step, forming an unstable 1-arseno-3-phosphoglycerate that hydrolyses spontaneously
   key=D ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 31st ed. — Chapter 18: Glycolysis & the Oxidation of Pyruvate (Arsenate/Arsenite)
   concept: Arsenate mimics inorganic phosphate at the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase step, forming 1-arseno-3-phosphoglycerate, which spontaneously hydrolyses to 3-phosphoglycerate without generating 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate. This bypasses the phosphoglycerate kinase substrate-level phosphorylation (arsenolysis), so glycolysis continues and NADH is still made, but the net ATP is lost. Note that trivalent arsenite (not arsenate) is the species that inhibits lipoic-acid enzymes such as pyruvate dehydrogenase; the other options describe unrelated toxic mechanisms.
Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_006 [M] A blood sample for glucose estimation is collected in a grey-top tube containing sodium fluoride. Fluoride preserves the glucose value by inhibiting which glycolytic enzyme?
    A. Hexokinase
    B. Pyruvate kinase
   *C. Enolase
    D. Lactate dehydrogenase
   key=C ref=Vasudevan's Textbook of Biochemistry for Medical Students, 9th ed. — Chapter: Major Metabolic Pathways of Glucose (Glycolysis)
   concept: Sodium fluoride forms a magnesium-fluorophosphate complex that inhibits enolase (2-phosphoglycerate to phosphoenolpyruvate), halting glycolysis in the collected sample so that red cells and leukocytes cannot continue consuming glucose in vitro — this prevents a falsely low reading. Fluoride is usually combined with potassium oxalate (the anticoagulant) in grey-top tubes. Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_008 [H] A child with chronic non-spherocytic haemolytic anaemia is found to have an autosomal recessive enzyme defect. Red cells show low ATP with markedly elevated 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate. The deficient enzyme is:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_010 [H] Chronic arsenic (arsenite) poisoning impairs the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate. The molecular basis is that arsenite:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_011 [M] A neonate presents with severe congenital lactic acidosis and progressive neurological impairment. Blood shows elevated lactate and pyruvate with a normal lactate-to-pyruvate ratio. The most likely defect is:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_015 [H] Fluoroacetate (the rodenticide 'compound 1080') is lethal because it undergoes conversion in vivo to fluorocitrate, which inhibits the citric acid cycle enzyme:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_017 [E] One complete turn of the citric acid cycle, oxidising one molecule of acetyl-CoA, directly yields:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_018 [M] Pyruvate carboxylase, the first committed enzyme of gluconeogenesis from pyruvate, requires which coenzyme and which allosteric activator, respectively?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_019 [M] Which enzyme, present in liver and kidney but absent in skeletal muscle, catalyses the final step — common to both gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis — that releases free glucose into the blood?

    C. Phosphoglucomutase

   concept: Glucose-6-phosphatase, located on the endoplasmic reticulum membrane of liver, kidney and intestine, hydrolyses glucose-6-phosphate to free glucose, allowing these organs to export glucose to maintain blood levels. Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_020 [E] In the Cori cycle, lactate produced by anaerobic glycolysis in exercising muscle is carried to the liver, where it is used mainly to:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_021 [H] A boy of Mediterranean descent develops acute haemolysis with bite cells and Heinz bodies after eating fava beans. The enzyme he is deficient in normally supplies the erythrocyte with which protective molecule?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_CHO_023 [M] A healthy, asymptomatic child is incidentally found to have a urine sample that is positive on a copper-reduction (Benedict's) test but negative on a glucose-oxidase dipstick. The most likely benign explanation is deficiency of:

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_001 [M] Transport of long-chain fatty acyl groups across the inner mitochondrial membrane into the matrix for beta-oxidation depends on the carnitine shuttle. Which enzyme catalyses the rate-limiting step of this shuttle and is physiologically inhibited by malonyl-CoA?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_004 [E] Each round of mitochondrial beta-oxidation of a saturated fatty acyl-CoA shortens the chain by two carbons. Which set of products is generated directly in a single round?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_005 [M] Ketone bodies are synthesised in hepatic mitochondria from acetyl-CoA. After thiolase and HMG-CoA synthase produce HMG-CoA, which enzyme cleaves HMG-CoA to yield acetoacetate plus acetyl-CoA, the committed step of ketogenesis?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_006 [H] The liver synthesises ketone bodies for export to peripheral tissues but is itself unable to use them as a fuel. This inability is best explained by the hepatic absence of which enzyme?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_007 [M] The committed, rate-limiting step of de novo fatty acid synthesis is the carboxylation of acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA. Which statement about the enzyme catalysing this step is correct?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_008 [H] In the well-fed state, active fatty acid synthesis prevents simultaneous futile beta-oxidation of the newly made fatty acids. This reciprocal control is achieved primarily because malonyl-CoA does what?

   key=C ref=Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Fatty Acid and Triacylglycerol Metabolism
   concept: The citrate (tricarboxylate) shuttle exports acetyl units: mitochondrial acetyl-CoA condenses with oxaloacetate to citrate, which leaves the mitochondrion and is split in the cytosol by ATP-citrate lyase into acetyl-CoA (for fatty acid synthesis) and oxaloacetate. Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_013 [E] Humans lack the desaturases needed to introduce double bonds beyond carbon 9 of a fatty acid, so certain fatty acids must be obtained from the diet. Which pair are the true essential fatty acids in humans?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_016 [E] Plasma lipoproteins differ in density according to their protein-to-lipid ratio. Which lipoprotein class has the highest protein content (and thus highest density) and mediates reverse cholesterol transport from peripheral tissues to the liver?

    C. LDL
   *D. HDL
   key=D ref=Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry — Cholesterol, Lipoprotein, and Steroid Metabolism

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_017 [M] ApoB-48 and ApoB-100 are produced from the same gene, differing because of tissue-specific mRNA editing. Which statement about these two apolipoproteins is correct?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_018 [M] Lipoprotein lipase (LPL), anchored to capillary endothelium, hydrolyses the triglycerides carried in chylomicrons and VLDL so that free fatty acids can be taken up by tissues. Which apolipoprotein is the essential activator of lipoprotein lipase?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_019 [H] A 28-year-old man has markedly elevated LDL cholesterol, tendon xanthomas over the Achilles tendons and knuckles, corneal arcus, and a strong family history of myocardial infarction before age 45 in an autosomal dominant pattern. Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_022 [M] Lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT) is a plasma enzyme activated by apolipoprotein A-I. What is its principal role in HDL and reverse cholesterol transport?

Q_GEN_BIOCHEM_LIPID_026 [M] A fasting lipid panel reports total cholesterol 200 mg/dL, HDL cholesterol 50 mg/dL and triglycerides 150 mg/dL. Using the Friedewald equation, what is the calculated LDL cholesterol?

    B. 100 mg/dL
    C. 150 mg/dL
    D. 130 mg/dL
   key=A ref=Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry — Cholesterol Synthesis, Transport, & Excretion (Clinical Laboratory)
   concept: The Friedewald equation estimates LDL cholesterol as LDL = Total cholesterol minus HDL minus (triglycerides divided by 5), where triglycerides/5 approximates VLDL cholesterol in mg/dL.
