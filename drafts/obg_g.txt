@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Abnormal labour: malpresentation, obstructed labour, CPD ~ OBG_ABL ~ DC Dutta's Textbook of Obstetrics 9e, Ch. Abnormal Labour
E ~ A multipara at term has a transverse lie with the fetal back anterior and membranes intact, and the cervix is 2 cm dilated. The correct management is: ~ Immediate caesarean section ~ Internal podalic version ~ Oxytocin augmentation ~ Await spontaneous correction ~ A ~ A persistent transverse lie at term cannot deliver vaginally and risks cord prolapse and uterine rupture once membranes rupture, so delivery is by caesarean; internal podalic version is confined to the second twin.
E ~ On vaginal examination in labour the anterior fontanelle is felt centrally with the sagittal suture transverse. This indicates: ~ Occipito-anterior position ~ Deep transverse arrest with a deflexed head ~ Face presentation ~ Brow presentation ~ B ~ Feeling the anterior fontanelle centrally means the head is deflexed rather than well flexed, and a sagittal suture lying transverse at the level of the ischial spines with no progress defines deep transverse arrest.
E ~ A woman in obstructed labour has a temperature of 39 degrees, a pulse of 130 and offensive liquor after 20 hours of labour at home. The most urgent priority alongside delivery is: ~ Antibiotics and fluid resuscitation before operative intervention ~ Immediate forceps delivery ~ Oxytocin infusion ~ Manual dilatation of the cervix ~ A ~ Obstructed labour with sepsis and dehydration carries high maternal mortality, so resuscitation and broad-spectrum antibiotics run concurrently with arranging caesarean delivery.
M ~ A primigravida at term has a mento-posterior face presentation with no descent after 2 hours of good contractions. The appropriate management is: ~ Caesarean section ~ Await rotation to mento-anterior ~ Forceps rotation ~ Vacuum extraction ~ A ~ A persistent mento-posterior face cannot deliver vaginally because the fetal neck is already maximally extended and cannot extend further to negotiate the pelvic curve, unlike a mento-anterior face which can deliver by flexion.
M ~ In a brow presentation at term, which engaging diameter makes vaginal delivery of a normal-sized fetus impossible? ~ Suboccipito-bregmatic ~ Mento-vertical, about 14 cm ~ Submento-bregmatic ~ Occipito-frontal ~ B ~ The mento-vertical diameter is the largest anteroposterior diameter of the fetal head, so a persistent brow must either convert to a face or a vertex or be delivered by caesarean.
M ~ A woman with a previous caesarean scar in labour develops sudden severe abdominal pain, cessation of contractions, fetal bradycardia and a rising maternal pulse with vaginal bleeding. The diagnosis is: ~ Placental abruption ~ Scar dehiscence with uterine rupture ~ Amniotic fluid embolism ~ Vasa praevia ~ B ~ Loss of contractions with an abnormally palpable fetal presenting part, maternal tachycardia and acute fetal distress in a scarred uterus indicates rupture, requiring immediate laparotomy.
M ~ A short primigravida has a fetus with an estimated weight of 3.8 kg and the head remains high with a poorly applied cervix after 3 hours in the active phase. This picture suggests: ~ Cephalopelvic disproportion ~ Hypotonic uterine inertia alone ~ Cervical dystocia ~ Precipitate labour ~ A ~ Failure of descent with a high head and a poorly applied cervix despite adequate contractions in a small woman with a large fetus indicates disproportion, and trial of labour must be abandoned rather than augmented.
H ~ Which manoeuvre is contraindicated in shoulder dystocia because it worsens impaction? ~ McRoberts manoeuvre ~ Fundal pressure ~ Suprapubic pressure ~ Delivery of the posterior arm ~ B ~ Fundal pressure drives the anterior shoulder harder against the symphysis and increases the risk of uterine rupture and brachial plexus injury; suprapubic pressure directed obliquely is the correct adjunct.
H ~ In a locked twin where the first twin is breech and the second cephalic, the heads impact at the pelvic brim. The correct management is: ~ Immediate caesarean section ~ Vigorous traction on the first twin ~ Destructive operation on the second twin ~ Oxytocin augmentation ~ A ~ Locking is a rare obstetric emergency in which neither twin can descend; caesarean delivery is the safe modern approach, and vigorous traction risks decapitation and uterine rupture.
H ~ A woman with untreated obstructed labour delivers and 7 days later develops continuous urinary leakage with a normal bladder capacity. The most likely lesion is: ~ Vesicovaginal fistula from pressure necrosis ~ Stress urinary incontinence ~ Urethral injury from forceps ~ Detrusor overactivity ~ A ~ Prolonged impaction of the head compresses the bladder base against the symphysis, and the resulting ischaemic slough separates around the seventh to tenth day, producing a fistula rather than immediate leakage.
@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Abortion, ectopic pregnancy, recurrent pregnancy loss ~ OBG_AEP ~ DC Dutta's Textbook of Obstetrics 9e, Ch. Abortion and Ectopic Pregnancy
E ~ A woman at 9 weeks has vaginal bleeding with a closed cervical os, and ultrasound shows a live fetus of appropriate size. The diagnosis is: ~ Threatened abortion ~ Inevitable abortion ~ Missed abortion ~ Complete abortion ~ A ~ Bleeding with a closed os and a viable intrauterine pregnancy defines threatened abortion, and about two-thirds continue to term with expectant management.
E ~ A woman with an intrauterine pregnancy of 10 weeks by dates has an ultrasound showing a gestational sac of 30 mm with no embryo. This indicates: ~ Anembryonic pregnancy (blighted ovum) ~ Threatened abortion ~ Complete molar pregnancy ~ Pseudogestational sac of ectopic pregnancy ~ A ~ A mean sac diameter of 25 mm or more with no embryo meets the criterion for early pregnancy failure, and medical or surgical evacuation is then offered.
M ~ A stable woman has a 2 cm unruptured tubal ectopic pregnancy, a beta-hCG of 1800 IU/L, no fetal cardiac activity and no free fluid. Methotrexate is chosen. Which follow-up finding indicates treatment success? ~ A fall in beta-hCG of at least 15 percent between days 4 and 7 ~ Any fall in beta-hCG by day 2 ~ Resolution of pain within 24 hours ~ Disappearance of the mass on day 7 ultrasound ~ A ~ Beta-hCG characteristically rises between day 1 and day 4 before falling, so the day 4 to day 7 comparison is the accepted measure; separation pain around day 3 is expected and is not failure.
M ~ Which finding is an absolute contraindication to medical management of an ectopic pregnancy with methotrexate? ~ Haemodynamic instability with haemoperitoneum ~ Beta-hCG of 2000 IU/L ~ Adnexal mass of 3 cm ~ Desire for future fertility ~ A ~ Rupture with instability demands immediate surgery; other relative contraindications include cardiac activity in the ectopic, a mass above 4 cm and a beta-hCG above 5000 IU/L.
M ~ A woman with three consecutive first-trimester losses has a normal karyotype in both partners and a normal uterine cavity, but the lupus anticoagulant is positive on two occasions 12 weeks apart. The treatment that improves live birth rate is: ~ Low-dose aspirin with low molecular weight heparin from confirmation of pregnancy ~ Progesterone supplementation alone ~ Corticosteroids alone ~ Intravenous immunoglobulin ~ A ~ Antiphospholipid syndrome is the only clearly treatable cause of recurrent miscarriage, and combined aspirin and heparin raises live birth rates substantially.
M ~ A woman presents with a mid-trimester loss preceded by painless cervical dilatation and bulging membranes, having had a similar loss previously. The management in a future pregnancy is: ~ History-indicated cervical cerclage at 12 to 14 weeks ~ Bed rest alone ~ Tocolysis from 20 weeks ~ Elective caesarean at term ~ A ~ Cervical insufficiency causes painless dilatation without contractions, and a prophylactic cerclage placed early in the next pregnancy, with progesterone if the cervix shortens, is the accepted approach.
H ~ Uterine evacuation for a second-trimester missed abortion is complicated by sudden collapse with bleeding from puncture sites. The likely diagnosis is: ~ Disseminated intravascular coagulation from a retained dead fetus ~ Amniotic fluid embolism ~ Anaphylaxis to anaesthetic ~ Uterine inversion ~ A ~ Thromboplastins released from a fetus retained beyond about four weeks consume clotting factors, so coagulation should be checked before evacuating a long-standing missed abortion.
H ~ Which uterine anomaly carries the highest risk of mid-trimester loss and is amenable to hysteroscopic correction? ~ Septate uterus ~ Bicornuate uterus ~ Unicornuate uterus ~ Arcuate uterus ~ A ~ The poorly vascularised fibromuscular septum implants badly, and hysteroscopic septal resection improves outcomes, whereas a bicornuate uterus requires abdominal metroplasty and an arcuate uterus is a normal variant.
@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Ca cervix / endometrium / ovary; screening ~ OBG_CCE ~ Shaw's Textbook of Gynaecology 17e, Ch. Genital Malignancies
E ~ A woman with cervical carcinoma has disease extending to the lower third of the vagina but no pelvic wall involvement and normal hydronephrosis-free kidneys. Her FIGO stage is: ~ Stage IIIA ~ Stage IIA ~ Stage IIB ~ Stage IVA ~ A ~ Lower-third vaginal involvement defines stage IIIA, extension to the pelvic side wall or hydronephrosis defines IIIB, and bladder or rectal mucosal invasion defines IVA.
E ~ Which histological type of cervical carcinoma is least reliably detected by cytology-based screening? ~ Adenocarcinoma arising in the endocervical canal ~ Large cell keratinising squamous carcinoma ~ Large cell non-keratinising squamous carcinoma ~ Small cell squamous carcinoma ~ A ~ Endocervical adenocarcinoma arises high in the canal beyond the reach of the spatula and lacks a well-defined precursor lesion, which is one reason HPV-based screening has improved detection.
M ~ A 62-year-old woman has grade 1 endometrioid adenocarcinoma confined to the endometrium on hysteroscopic biopsy. The standard primary treatment is: ~ Total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and surgical staging ~ Primary chemoradiation ~ Progestogen therapy alone ~ Radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymphadenectomy in all cases ~ A ~ Endometrial cancer is staged surgically, and even early disease requires removal of the ovaries because they are a site of occult spread and of continuing oestrogen production.
M ~ Which ovarian tumour marker is most useful for monitoring a young woman treated for a dysgerminoma? ~ Lactate dehydrogenase ~ CA-125 ~ Alpha-fetoprotein ~ Carcinoembryonic antigen ~ A ~ Dysgerminoma characteristically secretes lactate dehydrogenase and sometimes placental alkaline phosphatase, whereas alpha-fetoprotein marks yolk sac tumour and CA-125 epithelial disease.
M ~ A postmenopausal woman has a 6 cm complex ovarian cyst with solid areas, ascites and a raised CA-125. The most appropriate next step is: ~ Referral to a gynaecological oncology centre for staging laparotomy ~ Laparoscopic cystectomy ~ Repeat ultrasound in 6 months ~ Aspiration of the cyst under ultrasound ~ A ~ A high risk of malignancy index warrants primary surgery by a subspecialist because complete staging and optimal debulking determine survival, and cyst aspiration risks spilling malignant cells.
H ~ Which criterion in cervical carcinoma makes primary chemoradiation preferable to radical hysterectomy? ~ Involvement of the parametrium (stage IIB or above) ~ Tumour diameter of 2 cm ~ Patient age below 40 ~ Squamous histology ~ A ~ Once the parametrium is involved, surgery cannot achieve clear margins and combining it with radiotherapy compounds morbidity, so concurrent cisplatin-based chemoradiation becomes the treatment of choice.
H ~ A woman with a granulosa cell tumour of the ovary requires which additional endometrial assessment? ~ Endometrial sampling, because oestrogen secretion causes hyperplasia or carcinoma ~ No assessment is needed ~ Cervical conisation ~ Screening for a germline BRCA mutation ~ A ~ Granulosa cell tumours secrete oestrogen and inhibin, so up to a quarter have coexisting endometrial hyperplasia or carcinoma, and inhibin is used for long-term surveillance given the risk of very late recurrence.
@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Contraception ~ OBG_C ~ Shaw's Textbook of Gynaecology 17e, Ch. Contraception
E ~ A woman has a copper intrauterine device in situ and a positive pregnancy test with an intrauterine gestation she wishes to continue. The correct action is: ~ Remove the device if the threads are visible ~ Leave the device and give antibiotics ~ Terminate the pregnancy ~ Push the device higher into the fundus ~ A ~ Leaving a device in place raises the risk of second-trimester loss, chorioamnionitis and preterm birth, so removal is advised when the threads are accessible.
E ~ Which contraceptive method reduces the risk of both endometrial and ovarian carcinoma with increasing duration of use? ~ Combined oral contraceptive pill ~ Copper intrauterine device ~ Male condom ~ Progestogen-only injectable ~ A ~ Suppression of ovulation and of endometrial proliferation gives roughly a 50 percent reduction in ovarian and endometrial cancer that persists for years after stopping, a key counselling point balanced against thrombotic risk.
M ~ A woman using the progestogen-only pill misses a tablet by 5 hours. The advice is: ~ Take it at once and use additional precautions for 48 hours ~ No action is needed ~ Stop the pill and start a new pack after the next period ~ Take two tablets and continue ~ A ~ Traditional progestogen-only pills have only a 3-hour window, so a delay beyond that requires backup contraception; the desogestrel formulation allows a 12-hour window.
M ~ Which finding on counselling would make the levonorgestrel intrauterine system the preferred method? ~ Heavy menstrual bleeding with a desire for contraception ~ Wilson disease ~ Copper allergy ~ Desire for immediate return of fertility after removal ~ A ~ The system reduces menstrual blood loss by up to 90 percent and is licensed for menorrhagia and endometrial protection, so it addresses two problems at once.
M ~ After medical abortion at 8 weeks, when can a combined oral contraceptive pill be started? ~ Immediately, on the day of the procedure ~ After the first normal period ~ Two weeks later ~ After a negative pregnancy test at 6 weeks ~ A ~ Ovulation can occur within 2 to 3 weeks of abortion, so immediate initiation gives no additional risk and avoids the high rate of rapid repeat pregnancy seen with delayed starts.
H ~ Which mechanism explains why the copper intrauterine device is effective as emergency contraception up to 5 days after ovulation is expected? ~ Copper ions are toxic to sperm and the sterile inflammatory reaction prevents implantation ~ It blocks the luteinising hormone surge ~ It causes luteolysis ~ It thickens cervical mucus ~ A ~ Because its action includes an anti-implantation effect, the copper device works even after the fertile window has passed and is the most effective emergency method with a failure rate below 1 in 1000.
@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Diagnosis of pregnancy, antenatal imaging & fetal surveillance ~ OBG_DX ~ DC Dutta's Textbook of Obstetrics 9e, Ch. Antenatal Assessment
E ~ A woman with an uncertain last menstrual period has a first-trimester scan showing a crown-rump length of 45 mm. This corresponds to a gestational age of about: ~ 11 weeks ~ 8 weeks ~ 14 weeks ~ 16 weeks ~ A ~ Crown-rump length dating between 7 and 13 weeks is accurate to within 5 days, and a length of about 45 mm corresponds to 11 weeks; after 14 weeks biparietal diameter and femur length are used instead.
E ~ Which ultrasound soft marker in the second trimester is most strongly associated with trisomy 21? ~ Absent or hypoplastic nasal bone ~ Single umbilical artery ~ Choroid plexus cyst ~ Mild pyelectasis alone ~ A ~ Nasal bone hypoplasia carries the highest likelihood ratio among second-trimester soft markers, whereas isolated choroid plexus cysts and mild pyelectasis have little independent significance.
E ~ A cardiotocograph shows a baseline of 140, variability of 4 beats per minute over 60 minutes and no accelerations. This indicates: ~ Reduced variability requiring further assessment for fetal hypoxia or sedation ~ A normal reactive trace ~ Fetal tachycardia ~ Sinusoidal pattern ~ A ~ Variability below 5 beats for more than 40 minutes is the single most important marker of impaired central autonomic control, though sleep cycles and maternal opioids or magnesium can cause it transiently.
M ~ A growth-restricted fetus at 32 weeks has absent end-diastolic flow in the umbilical artery but a normal ductus venosus and normal computerised short-term variation. The management is: ~ Intensive surveillance with corticosteroids and planned delivery, usually by 34 weeks ~ Immediate caesarean section ~ Reassess in 4 weeks ~ Induction of labour at 40 weeks ~ A ~ Absent end-diastolic flow indicates advanced placental insufficiency but with normal venous Doppler there is time to give steroids; reversed flow or abnormal ductus venosus mandates prompt delivery.
M ~ Which Doppler parameter best predicts fetal anaemia in red cell alloimmunisation? ~ Middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity ~ Umbilical artery pulsatility index ~ Uterine artery notch ~ Ductus venosus a-wave ~ A ~ Anaemia lowers blood viscosity and raises cardiac output, increasing peak systolic velocity in the middle cerebral artery, and a value above 1.5 multiples of the median predicts moderate to severe anaemia.
M ~ First-trimester combined screening for aneuploidy uses nuchal translucency together with which biochemical markers? ~ Free beta-hCG and pregnancy-associated plasma protein A ~ Alpha-fetoprotein and unconjugated oestriol ~ Inhibin A and alpha-fetoprotein ~ Human placental lactogen and progesterone ~ A ~ In trisomy 21 pregnancy-associated plasma protein A is low and free beta-hCG high; the quadruple test with alpha-fetoprotein, oestriol, hCG and inhibin A belongs to the second trimester.
M ~ A woman at 41 weeks has a modified biophysical profile with a non-reactive non-stress test and an amniotic fluid index of 3 cm. The appropriate action is: ~ Delivery, as oligohydramnios with a non-reactive trace indicates chronic placental insufficiency ~ Repeat testing in 1 week ~ Amnioinfusion and continue expectant management ~ Reassurance ~ A ~ Amniotic fluid volume reflects chronic fetal renal perfusion and falls with sustained hypoxaemia, so its reduction with an abnormal trace is an indication to deliver.
H ~ Cell-free fetal DNA screening returns a high-risk result for trisomy 21. The correct counselling is: ~ Confirmatory invasive testing by amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling is required before any irreversible decision ~ The result is diagnostic and termination can be offered directly ~ Repeat the cell-free DNA test ~ No further action is needed if the scan is normal ~ A ~ Cell-free DNA is a screening test with a high but not perfect positive predictive value, which varies with prevalence, and false positives occur from confined placental mosaicism, vanishing twin and maternal malignancy.
H ~ Which finding on a cardiotocograph is most specific for fetal anaemia or acute severe hypoxia? ~ A sinusoidal pattern with a regular smooth undulation of 3 to 5 cycles per minute ~ Early decelerations ~ Variable decelerations with shouldering ~ Accelerations with movement ~ A ~ A true sinusoidal pattern lasting more than 10 minutes is rare and ominous, classically seen in fetomaternal haemorrhage, severe alloimmune anaemia and ruptured vasa praevia.
@ Obstetrics & Gynaecology ~ Endometriosis, adenomyosis & chronic pelvic pain ~ OBG_END ~ Shaw's Textbook of Gynaecology 17e, Ch. Endometriosis
E ~ Which symptom pattern is most characteristic of deep infiltrating endometriosis of the rectovaginal septum? ~ Deep dyspareunia with cyclical dyschezia and painful defaecation during menstruation ~ Postcoital bleeding ~ Painless heavy periods ~ Mid-cycle pain only ~ A ~ Nodules in the rectovaginal septum and uterosacral ligaments are stretched during intercourse and defaecation, and the cyclical timing follows the hormonal responsiveness of the ectopic endometrium.
E ~ The definitive diagnosis of peritoneal endometriosis requires: ~ Laparoscopic visualisation with histological confirmation ~ A raised serum CA-125 ~ Transvaginal ultrasound ~ Response to a therapeutic trial of oral contraceptives ~ A ~ Ultrasound reliably detects endometriomas and deep disease but not superficial peritoneal implants, and CA-125 is neither sensitive nor specific, so laparoscopy remains the reference standard.
E ~ Which pathological feature distinguishes adenomyosis from endometriosis? ~ Endometrial glands and stroma within the myometrium ~ Chocolate cyst of the ovary ~ Powder-burn peritoneal lesions ~ Rectovaginal nodules ~ A ~ Adenomyosis is the presence of endometrial tissue deep within the myometrium with surrounding smooth muscle hyperplasia, producing a diffusely bulky tender uterus rather than discrete pelvic implants.
M ~ A 32-year-old with endometriosis-associated pain wishes to conceive within a year. Which treatment is appropriate? ~ Laparoscopic excision or ablation of endometriosis, which improves both pain and fecundity ~ Continuous combined oral contraceptive pill ~ Gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist for 6 months ~ Levonorgestrel intrauterine system ~ A ~ All medical treatments for endometriosis are contraceptive, so in a woman seeking pregnancy surgical treatment of the disease is the option that addresses pain and subfertility together.
M ~ A gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist is used for 6 months for endometriosis. Add-back therapy is given to: ~ Prevent bone mineral density loss and vasomotor symptoms from hypo-oestrogenism ~ Improve the analgesic effect ~ Maintain fertility during treatment ~ Prevent ovarian cyst formation ~ A ~ Low-dose oestrogen with a progestogen protects bone and relieves menopausal symptoms without abolishing the therapeutic suppression, which allows treatment beyond 6 months.
M ~ Which imaging finding on transvaginal ultrasound suggests adenomyosis rather than a fibroid? ~ An indistinct junctional zone with myometrial cysts and a globular uterus ~ A well-defined hypoechoic mass with peripheral vascularity ~ A pedunculated submucosal lesion ~ Calcified rim ~ A ~ Fibroids are discrete and capsulated with rim vascularity, whereas adenomyosis diffusely thickens and heterogeneously distorts the myometrium with small cystic spaces and no clear plane.
M ~ A woman with a 5 cm ovarian endometrioma is undergoing surgery before assisted reproduction. Which technique best preserves ovarian reserve? ~ Careful cystectomy with stripping of the capsule, minimising thermal energy near the hilum ~ Wide oophorectomy ~ Repeated aspiration ~ Extensive bipolar coagulation of the cyst bed ~ A ~ Endometriomas lack a true capsule so stripping removes some normal cortex, and excessive coagulation destroys follicles, which is why anti-Mullerian hormone falls after surgery and technique matters.
H ~ Which theory best explains endometriosis occurring at sites remote from the pelvis such as the pleura? ~ Lymphatic and haematogenous dissemination of endometrial cells ~ Sampson's retrograde menstruation ~ Coelomic metaplasia ~ Induction theory ~ A ~ Retrograde menstruation explains the pelvic distribution, but thoracic and even cerebral endometriosis require vascular or lymphatic spread, which is why several theories coexist rather than one explaining all cases.
H ~ A woman with chronic pelvic pain has a normal laparoscopy. Which condition should be actively considered? ~ Pelvic floor myofascial pain and central sensitisation, often with coexisting bladder or bowel symptoms ~ Occult endometriosis requiring repeat laparoscopy ~ Ovarian malignancy ~ Cervical stenosis ~ A ~ Up to a third of women with chronic pelvic pain have a normal laparoscopy, and the pain is then usually musculoskeletal or neuropathic with central amplification, so repeated surgery is unhelpful and multidisciplinary management is needed.
