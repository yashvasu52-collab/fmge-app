@ Medicine ~ Leptospirosis, rickettsial & scrub typhus; zoonoses ~ MED_ZOO ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 182 (Rickettsial Diseases) and Ch. 179 (Leptospirosis)
E ~ An eschar at the site of a mite bite with fever, lymphadenopathy and raised transaminases in a rice-field worker suggests: ~ Scrub typhus ~ Leptospirosis ~ Dengue ~ Brucellosis ~ A ~ Orientia tsutsugamushi is inoculated by trombiculid mite larvae and the painless necrotic eschar with regional nodes is the single most useful clinical clue.
E ~ The drug of choice for scrub typhus is: ~ Doxycycline ~ Ceftriaxone ~ Ciprofloxacin ~ Amoxicillin ~ A ~ Rickettsiae are obligate intracellular organisms, so cell-penetrating agents such as doxycycline or azithromycin work while beta-lactams are useless; defervescence within 48 hours supports the diagnosis.
E ~ Conjunctival suffusion with jaundice, renal failure and myalgia after wading through floodwater indicates: ~ Weil disease from leptospirosis ~ Hepatitis A ~ Falciparum malaria ~ Yellow fever ~ A ~ Leptospires penetrate abraded skin and mucosa from rodent urine, and the combination of hepatic and renal involvement with severe calf tenderness is characteristic.
M ~ The most useful confirmatory test for leptospirosis in the second week of illness is: ~ Microscopic agglutination test on paired sera ~ Blood culture ~ Dark-field microscopy of urine ~ Peripheral smear ~ A ~ Leptospiraemia is confined to the first week while antibodies appear in the second, so serology with a fourfold rise is the practical reference standard.
M ~ The characteristic renal lesion of leptospirosis is: ~ Non-oliguric hypokalaemic acute kidney injury from tubulointerstitial nephritis ~ Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis ~ Renal papillary necrosis ~ Renal vein thrombosis ~ A ~ Direct tubular injury impairs sodium and potassium handling, so unlike most causes of acute kidney injury urine output is preserved and potassium is low.
M ~ Undulant fever, sacroiliitis and hepatosplenomegaly in a person consuming unpasteurised milk suggests: ~ Brucellosis ~ Typhoid ~ Tuberculosis ~ Kala-azar ~ A ~ Brucella is a facultative intracellular organism requiring prolonged combination therapy with doxycycline and either streptomycin or rifampicin to prevent relapse.
M ~ Indian tick typhus caused by Rickettsia conorii characteristically produces: ~ A maculopapular rash involving palms and soles with a tache noire ~ A rash sparing the extremities ~ Vesicular rash only ~ No rash ~ A ~ The vasculitic rash of spotted fever group rickettsiae involves the extremities including palms and soles, which distinguishes it from typhus group infections.
H ~ Thrombocytopenia with a normal or low white count, raised transaminases and a negative dengue NS1 in a patient with an eschar should prompt: ~ Empirical doxycycline for scrub typhus while awaiting IgM ELISA ~ Empirical artesunate ~ Empirical steroids ~ Observation only ~ A ~ Untreated scrub typhus progresses to acute respiratory distress syndrome, myocarditis and meningoencephalitis, and mortality falls sharply with early empirical doxycycline in endemic settings.
H ~ The Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction after starting penicillin in leptospirosis is due to: ~ Massive release of spirochaetal antigen triggering cytokine surge ~ Drug allergy ~ Endotoxin from Gram-negative superinfection ~ Serum sickness ~ A ~ Rapid killing releases lipoproteins that provoke fever, rigors and hypotension within hours, and it is managed supportively rather than by stopping the antibiotic.
@ Medicine ~ Leukaemias, lymphomas & plasma cell disorders ~ MED_HEM ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 106 (Malignancies of Lymphoid Cells)
E ~ The Philadelphia chromosome in chronic myeloid leukaemia results from: ~ Translocation t(9;22) creating BCR-ABL1 ~ Translocation t(15;17) ~ Translocation t(8;14) ~ Trisomy 12 ~ A ~ The fusion gene encodes a constitutively active tyrosine kinase, which is precisely why imatinib and later tyrosine kinase inhibitors transformed the prognosis.
E ~ Bone pain, anaemia, renal failure and hypercalcaemia in an older adult should prompt evaluation for: ~ Multiple myeloma ~ Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia ~ Hodgkin lymphoma ~ Aplastic anaemia ~ A ~ Clonal plasma cells produce a paraprotein and activate osteoclasts, giving the CRAB features that define symptomatic disease requiring treatment.
E ~ Painless cervical lymphadenopathy with pain after alcohol and Reed-Sternberg cells indicates: ~ Hodgkin lymphoma ~ Non-Hodgkin lymphoma ~ Tuberculous lymphadenitis ~ Sarcoidosis ~ A ~ Hodgkin disease spreads contiguously through nodal groups, which is why staging and radiotherapy planning differ from the multicentric spread of non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
M ~ Disseminated intravascular coagulation at presentation is most characteristic of which leukaemia: ~ Acute promyelocytic leukaemia ~ Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia ~ Hairy cell leukaemia ~ Chronic myeloid leukaemia ~ A ~ Granule procoagulants released from abnormal promyelocytes trigger consumptive coagulopathy, and all-trans retinoic acid is started on suspicion before cytogenetics return.
M ~ Smudge cells on the peripheral smear with absolute lymphocytosis in an elderly patient indicate: ~ Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia ~ Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia ~ Infectious mononucleosis ~ Chronic myeloid leukaemia ~ A ~ Fragile mature B lymphocytes rupture during smear preparation, and flow cytometry showing CD5 and CD23 co-expression confirms the clone.
M ~ Tumour lysis syndrome is characterised by: ~ Hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hyperuricaemia and hypocalcaemia ~ Hypokalaemia and hypercalcaemia ~ Hyponatraemia alone ~ Hypophosphataemia ~ A ~ Rapid cell death releases intracellular contents; phosphate binds calcium and precipitates in tubules, so hydration with rasburicase or allopurinol is given prophylactically.
M ~ Monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance is distinguished from myeloma by: ~ Paraprotein under 3 g/dL, marrow plasma cells under 10 percent and no end-organ damage ~ Presence of Bence Jones protein ~ Any detectable paraprotein ~ Age above 70 ~ A ~ It progresses to myeloma at about 1 percent per year, so it needs surveillance rather than chemotherapy.
H ~ A starry-sky appearance on lymph node biopsy with a translocation involving MYC indicates: ~ Burkitt lymphoma ~ Follicular lymphoma ~ Mantle cell lymphoma ~ Hodgkin lymphoma ~ A ~ Tingible-body macrophages among sheets of proliferating cells give the pattern, the proliferation index approaches 100 percent, and it is one of the fastest growing but most curable human tumours.
H ~ The single best initial investigation in suspected multiple myeloma with normal serum electrophoresis is: ~ Serum free light chain assay ~ Repeat electrophoresis ~ Bone scan ~ Serum calcium ~ A ~ Light chain only myeloma produces no detectable heavy chain band, and light chains are cleared renally so serum electrophoresis misses them while the free light chain ratio detects them.
@ Medicine ~ Movement disorders, Parkinson's disease & dementia ~ MED_MOV ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 437 (Parkinson's Disease)
E ~ The cardinal triad of Parkinson disease is: ~ Bradykinesia, rest tremor and rigidity ~ Intention tremor, ataxia and nystagmus ~ Chorea, hypotonia and dementia ~ Spasticity, clonus and hyperreflexia ~ A ~ Bradykinesia is obligatory for the diagnosis, and the pathology is loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta with Lewy bodies.
E ~ The most effective symptomatic drug in Parkinson disease is: ~ Levodopa with carbidopa ~ Amantadine ~ Trihexyphenidyl ~ Selegiline ~ A ~ Peripheral decarboxylase inhibition allows more levodopa to reach the brain and reduces nausea, and no other agent matches its effect on bradykinesia and rigidity.
E ~ Early prominent visual hallucinations with fluctuating cognition and parkinsonism suggest: ~ Dementia with Lewy bodies ~ Alzheimer disease ~ Vascular dementia ~ Frontotemporal dementia ~ A ~ Marked neuroleptic sensitivity is a further clue, and antipsychotics can cause severe irreversible deterioration in these patients.
M ~ A young adult with tremor, dysarthria, Kayser-Fleischer rings and deranged liver function has: ~ Wilson disease ~ Huntington disease ~ Parkinson disease ~ Multiple sclerosis ~ A ~ Impaired biliary copper excretion from ATP7B mutation deposits copper in liver, basal ganglia and cornea, and treatment is chelation with penicillamine or trientine plus zinc.
M ~ Progressive dementia with early personality change, disinhibition and preserved memory suggests: ~ Frontotemporal dementia ~ Alzheimer disease ~ Dementia with Lewy bodies ~ Normal pressure hydrocephalus ~ A ~ Behavioural variant disease begins in the frontal and anterior temporal lobes, so social conduct fails long before episodic memory does.
M ~ The triad of gait apraxia, urinary incontinence and dementia with ventriculomegaly indicates: ~ Normal pressure hydrocephalus ~ Alzheimer disease ~ Parkinson disease ~ Subdural haematoma ~ A ~ It is one of the few reversible dementias, and improvement in gait after a high-volume lumbar tap predicts benefit from ventriculoperitoneal shunting.
M ~ Chorea with progressive dementia and a positive family history of autosomal dominant inheritance indicates: ~ Huntington disease ~ Sydenham chorea ~ Wilson disease ~ Tardive dyskinesia ~ A ~ CAG trinucleotide expansion in the huntingtin gene shows anticipation, and caudate atrophy widens the frontal horns on imaging.
H ~ Parkinsonism with early falls and vertical supranuclear gaze palsy suggests: ~ Progressive supranuclear palsy ~ Multiple system atrophy ~ Corticobasal degeneration ~ Idiopathic Parkinson disease ~ A ~ Poor levodopa response, axial rigidity and downgaze restriction with midbrain atrophy on MRI distinguish this tauopathy from idiopathic disease.
H ~ Parkinsonism with early severe autonomic failure and cerebellar signs indicates: ~ Multiple system atrophy ~ Progressive supranuclear palsy ~ Vascular parkinsonism ~ Drug-induced parkinsonism ~ A ~ Alpha-synuclein inclusions in glia affect striatonigral, olivopontocerebellar and autonomic pathways, giving orthostatic hypotension and urinary failure early in the course.
@ Medicine ~ Muscular dystrophies & neuromuscular disorders ~ MED_MDN ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 448 (Muscular Dystrophies)
E ~ Gower sign in a boy with calf pseudohypertrophy indicates: ~ Proximal muscle weakness of Duchenne muscular dystrophy ~ Cerebellar ataxia ~ Peripheral neuropathy ~ Myasthenia gravis ~ A ~ The child climbs up his own thighs because hip and knee extensors are too weak to straighten the trunk directly, and creatine kinase is grossly elevated.
E ~ A woman whose brother has Duchenne muscular dystrophy asks about risk to her sons. The expected risk if she is a carrier is: ~ One in two of her sons affected ~ All sons affected ~ One in four of all children affected regardless of sex ~ No sons affected ~ A ~ The dystrophin gene lies at Xp21, so a carrier mother transmits the mutant X to half her sons, who are affected, and to half her daughters, who become carriers.
E ~ Fatigable ptosis and diplopia worsening through the day indicate: ~ Myasthenia gravis ~ Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome ~ Botulism ~ Miller Fisher syndrome ~ A ~ Antibodies to the postsynaptic acetylcholine receptor reduce endplate potentials, so repeated use exhausts transmission and rest restores it.
M ~ Decremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation at low frequency is characteristic of: ~ Myasthenia gravis ~ Lambert-Eaton syndrome ~ Motor neuron disease ~ Myopathy ~ A ~ Postsynaptic receptor loss makes successive endplate potentials fall below threshold, whereas presynaptic disorders show an incremental response after brief exercise.
M ~ A patient with myasthenia develops respiratory failure. The immediate priority is: ~ Airway support with plasma exchange or intravenous immunoglobulin ~ Increasing pyridostigmine ~ Starting azathioprine ~ Thymectomy ~ A ~ Rapid-acting immunotherapy plus ventilatory support treats the crisis; more anticholinesterase risks cholinergic crisis and steroid-sparing agents take months to act.
M ~ Proximal weakness with a heliotrope rash and Gottron papules indicates: ~ Dermatomyositis ~ Polymyositis ~ Inclusion body myositis ~ Polymyalgia rheumatica ~ A ~ It is a complement-mediated microangiopathy with perifascicular atrophy, and in adults it carries a significant association with underlying malignancy.
M ~ Asymmetric distal weakness with fasciculations and brisk reflexes without sensory loss suggests: ~ Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis ~ Guillain-Barre syndrome ~ Cervical radiculopathy ~ Myasthenia gravis ~ A ~ Combined upper and lower motor neuron signs in the same region with intact sensation is the hallmark, and riluzole gives a modest survival benefit.
H ~ Grip myotonia, frontal balding, cataract and cardiac conduction defects indicate: ~ Myotonic dystrophy type 1 ~ Duchenne muscular dystrophy ~ Facioscapulohumeral dystrophy ~ Limb-girdle dystrophy ~ A ~ A CTG expansion in the DMPK gene affects multiple systems, and the conduction disease can cause sudden death, so annual electrocardiography is essential.
H ~ Proximal weakness that improves briefly after exercise, with autonomic dryness and an underlying small cell lung cancer, indicates: ~ Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome ~ Myasthenia gravis ~ Polymyositis ~ Thyrotoxic myopathy ~ A ~ Antibodies against presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels reduce acetylcholine release, and brief maximal contraction facilitates calcium entry, giving post-exercise improvement.
@ Medicine ~ Nutritional & vitamin deficiency states in adults ~ MED_NUT ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 331 (Vitamin and Trace Mineral Deficiency)
E ~ Confusion, ophthalmoplegia and ataxia in a chronic alcoholic indicate: ~ Wernicke encephalopathy from thiamine deficiency ~ Hepatic encephalopathy ~ Alcohol withdrawal ~ Cerebellar degeneration ~ A ~ Thiamine must be given before any glucose load because carbohydrate metabolism consumes the remaining thiamine and can precipitate irreversible Korsakoff psychosis.
E ~ Perifollicular haemorrhage, corkscrew hairs and bleeding gums indicate deficiency of: ~ Vitamin C ~ Vitamin K ~ Vitamin A ~ Niacin ~ A ~ Ascorbate is the cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase in collagen synthesis, so defective collagen weakens vessel walls and impairs wound healing.
E ~ Dermatitis, diarrhoea and dementia in a maize-eating population indicate deficiency of: ~ Niacin ~ Riboflavin ~ Thiamine ~ Pyridoxine ~ A ~ Maize niacin is bound and its tryptophan content is low, and Casal necklace is the classic photosensitive dermatitis of pellagra.
M ~ Night blindness with Bitot spots and conjunctival xerosis indicate deficiency of: ~ Vitamin A ~ Vitamin B12 ~ Zinc ~ Vitamin D ~ A ~ Retinal is required to regenerate rhodopsin and retinoic acid maintains mucosal differentiation, so both dark adaptation and epithelial integrity fail.
M ~ A malnourished patient on antitubercular therapy develops burning feet and glove-and-stocking sensory loss after two months. The deficiency responsible is: ~ Pyridoxine, depleted by isoniazid ~ Thiamine, depleted by rifampicin ~ Folate, depleted by ethambutol ~ Niacin, depleted by pyrazinamide ~ A ~ Isoniazid forms a hydrazone with pyridoxal phosphate and increases its urinary loss, so 10 to 25 mg of pyridoxine daily is given prophylactically to those at risk.
M ~ Refeeding syndrome after starting nutrition in a severely malnourished patient is characterised by: ~ Hypophosphataemia, hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia ~ Hyperphosphataemia ~ Hypernatraemia ~ Hypercalcaemia ~ A ~ Insulin release drives phosphate, potassium and magnesium intracellularly for ATP synthesis, which can cause fatal arrhythmias unless calories are increased slowly with electrolyte monitoring.
M ~ Angular stomatitis, glossitis and normocytic anaemia after prolonged phenytoin therapy suggests deficiency of: ~ Folate ~ Vitamin K ~ Vitamin D ~ Iron ~ A ~ Phenytoin impairs folate absorption and induces its metabolism, which is also why the drug can precipitate megaloblastic change and worsen seizure control in a vicious cycle.
H ~ Selenium deficiency is classically associated with: ~ Keshan cardiomyopathy ~ Acrodermatitis enteropathica ~ Wilson disease ~ Menkes syndrome ~ A ~ Selenium is required for glutathione peroxidase, and its deficiency in low-selenium soil regions of China produced an endemic congestive cardiomyopathy.
H ~ Diarrhoea, alopecia and a perioral and acral vesiculobullous rash after prolonged parenteral nutrition indicate deficiency of: ~ Zinc ~ Copper ~ Selenium ~ Chromium ~ A ~ Zinc is essential for many metalloenzymes and for epithelial turnover, and the picture resembles the inherited acrodermatitis enteropathica.
@ Medicine ~ Obesity, dyslipidaemia & metabolic syndrome ~ MED_OBM ~ Harrison 21e, Ch. 407 (Obesity) and Ch. 407 (Disorders of Lipoprotein Metabolism)
E ~ The body mass index cut-off defining obesity for Asian Indians is: ~ 25 kg per square metre ~ 30 kg per square metre ~ 27 kg per square metre ~ 23 kg per square metre ~ A ~ Indians develop visceral adiposity and insulin resistance at a lower body mass index, so the consensus guidelines lower both the overweight and obesity thresholds.
E ~ A man with central obesity and impaired fasting glucose has triglycerides of 280 mg/dL, HDL of 32 mg/dL and an LDL of 110 mg/dL. This pattern is termed: ~ Atherogenic dyslipidaemia of the metabolic syndrome ~ Familial hypercholesterolaemia ~ Type I hyperlipoproteinaemia ~ Isolated hypoalphalipoproteinaemia ~ A ~ Excess free fatty acid flux drives hepatic VLDL output and cholesteryl ester transfer remodels HDL and LDL into small dense particles, so the LDL number understates the risk.
E ~ A 55-year-old man with established coronary artery disease has an LDL of 150 mg/dL despite diet. The agent that will most reduce his risk of further events is: ~ A high-intensity statin ~ Fenofibrate ~ Omega-3 fatty acids ~ Cholestyramine ~ A ~ HMG-CoA reductase inhibition upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, and only statins have consistent secondary-prevention outcome evidence at this level of risk.
M ~ Tendon xanthomata with an LDL above 190 mg/dL and a strong family history of early coronary disease indicate: ~ Familial hypercholesterolaemia ~ Familial hypertriglyceridaemia ~ Metabolic syndrome ~ Secondary hyperlipidaemia ~ A ~ LDL receptor mutations are usually autosomal dominant, and cascade screening of relatives with early high-intensity statin therapy is the key intervention.
M ~ A fasting triglyceride level above 885 mg/dL primarily risks: ~ Acute pancreatitis ~ Stroke ~ Aortic dissection ~ Peripheral arterial disease ~ A ~ Chylomicronaemia releases free fatty acids in pancreatic capillaries causing inflammation, so fibrates, dietary fat restriction and control of diabetes take priority over LDL lowering.
M ~ Statin therapy should be temporarily stopped if creatine kinase rises above: ~ Ten times the upper limit of normal with muscle symptoms ~ Twice normal ~ Any elevation ~ Only if myoglobinuria occurs ~ A ~ Mild elevations are common and benign, but marked elevation with symptoms risks rhabdomyolysis, and interacting drugs such as macrolides, azoles and fibrates increase that risk.
M ~ An Asian Indian man with a body mass index of 34 and poorly controlled type 2 diabetes despite three agents is being considered for metabolic surgery. He: ~ Meets the Indian consensus threshold, since comorbidity lowers it to 32.5 ~ Does not qualify until his body mass index exceeds 40 ~ Qualifies only if he is above 45 ~ Should never be offered surgery with diabetes ~ A ~ Indian thresholds are set lower than Western ones because visceral adiposity and insulin resistance appear at a lower body mass index, and diabetes remission is a principal indication.
H ~ Which lipid-lowering agent works by inhibiting the intestinal sterol transporter NPC1L1: ~ Ezetimibe ~ Atorvastatin ~ Fenofibrate ~ Evolocumab ~ A ~ Blocking cholesterol absorption lowers LDL by about 20 percent and adds to statin effect, as shown in the IMPROVE-IT trial.
H ~ A patient with familial hypercholesterolaemia remains at an LDL of 160 mg/dL on maximal statin and ezetimibe. Adding evolocumab lowers LDL because it: ~ Prevents lysosomal degradation of recycled hepatic LDL receptors ~ Further blocks HMG-CoA reductase ~ Activates lipoprotein lipase ~ Binds bile acids in the gut ~ A ~ PCSK9 normally escorts the internalised receptor to the lysosome, so blocking it increases receptor density on the hepatocyte surface and clearance of circulating LDL.
