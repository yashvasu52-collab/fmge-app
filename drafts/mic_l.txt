@ Microbiology ~ Helminths: intestinal & tissue nematodes, cestodes, trematodes ~ MIC_HEL ~ Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 11e, Ch. Helminthology
E ~ Which helminth causes autoinfection and can produce disseminated hyperinfection in a patient given corticosteroids? ~ Strongyloides stercoralis ~ Ascaris lumbricoides ~ Trichuris trichiura ~ Taenia saginata ~ A ~ Rhabditiform larvae can mature to infective filariform larvae within the gut, so immunosuppression permits massive autoinfection with larvae carrying enteric bacteria into the blood, causing Gram-negative sepsis.
E ~ Ingestion of Taenia solium eggs rather than cysticerci in pork produces which disease? ~ Cysticercosis, including neurocysticercosis ~ Intestinal taeniasis only ~ Hydatid disease ~ Sparganosis ~ A ~ Humans become the accidental intermediate host when they ingest eggs, so faecal-oral spread, including autoinfection, causes the tissue cysts, whereas eating undercooked pork gives only the adult tapeworm.
E ~ Which nematode is diagnosed by finding eggs on perianal cellophane tape rather than in stool? ~ Enterobius vermicularis ~ Hookworm ~ Ascaris lumbricoides ~ Trichinella spiralis ~ A ~ The gravid female migrates at night to lay eggs on the perianal skin, causing pruritus ani and autoinfection, so the NIH swab is far more sensitive than stool microscopy.
M ~ Which two hookworm species are distinguished by their buccal armature, and which causes greater blood loss per worm? ~ Ancylostoma duodenale with teeth causes more loss than Necator americanus with cutting plates ~ Necator has teeth and causes more loss ~ Both have teeth and equal loss ~ Both have cutting plates and no loss ~ A ~ Ancylostoma removes about 0.2 mL of blood per worm daily against about 0.03 mL for Necator, which explains the iron deficiency anaemia of heavy infection and guides the public health burden estimate.
M ~ Loeffler syndrome with transient pulmonary infiltrates and marked eosinophilia is characteristic of the migratory phase of: ~ Ascaris lumbricoides ~ Enterobius vermicularis ~ Taenia solium ~ Fasciola hepatica ~ A ~ Larvae penetrate the intestinal wall and pass through liver and lungs before being swallowed again, and the same lung passage occurs with hookworm and Strongyloides.
M ~ A hydatid cyst of the liver should not be aspirated without precautions because: ~ Spillage of protoscolices risks anaphylaxis and secondary dissemination ~ It contains pyogenic bacteria ~ Aspiration always causes bleeding ~ The cyst wall is calcified ~ A ~ The PAIR technique with a scolicidal agent and albendazole cover, or surgery, is used; the Casoni skin test is obsolete and serology with imaging makes the diagnosis.
M ~ Which trematode is acquired by eating raw freshwater fish and causes cholangitis and cholangiocarcinoma? ~ Clonorchis sinensis ~ Schistosoma haematobium ~ Paragonimus westermani ~ Fasciolopsis buski ~ A ~ Chronic biliary infestation with the liver fluke is a recognised carcinogen; Schistosoma haematobium is instead linked with squamous carcinoma of the bladder and Paragonimus with pulmonary disease.
H ~ Which feature of Schistosoma haematobium infection explains terminal haematuria and later bladder malignancy? ~ Egg deposition in the vesical venous plexus with chronic granulomatous inflammation and fibrosis ~ Adult worms invading the bladder mucosa ~ Toxin production by cercariae ~ Direct larval penetration of the urothelium ~ A ~ The spined eggs excite granulomas that scar the bladder and ureters, producing calcification, obstruction and squamous metaplasia, and eggs are sought in a terminal urine specimen collected around midday.
@ Microbiology ~ Herpesviruses, CMV & EBV ~ MIC_HRP ~ Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 11e, Ch. Herpesviruses
E ~ Where does herpes simplex virus type 1 establish latency? ~ Trigeminal ganglion ~ Sacral dorsal root ganglia ~ Anterior horn cells ~ Regional lymph nodes ~ A ~ Latency in sensory ganglia with periodic reactivation along the nerve explains recurrent herpes labialis, while type 2 latency in sacral ganglia explains recurrent genital disease.
E ~ Which cell receptor does Epstein-Barr virus use to enter B lymphocytes? ~ CD21, the complement receptor 2 ~ CD4 ~ CD19 ~ CXCR4 ~ A ~ Binding CD21 with HLA class II as a co-receptor allows B cell infection and immortalisation, which underlies both the atypical lymphocytosis of glandular fever and the lymphoproliferation seen after transplantation.
E ~ Which herpesvirus causes exanthem subitum (roseola infantum) with high fever followed by a rash as the fever settles? ~ Human herpesvirus 6 ~ Varicella zoster virus ~ Cytomegalovirus ~ Human herpesvirus 8 ~ A ~ It is a common cause of febrile seizures in infancy, and the rash appearing as the temperature falls is the characteristic sequence that distinguishes it from measles.
M ~ Which inclusion body is characteristic of cytomegalovirus infection on histology? ~ Large intranuclear owl-eye inclusions with cytomegaly ~ Cowdry type A inclusions only ~ Negri bodies ~ Guarnieri bodies ~ A ~ The enlarged cell with a central basophilic intranuclear inclusion surrounded by a halo is the classic finding, seen in urine, lung and gastrointestinal biopsies in immunocompromised patients.
M ~ Which test is used to confirm infectious mononucleosis by detecting heterophile antibody? ~ Paul-Bunnell or monospot test ~ Widal test ~ Weil-Felix test ~ Rose-Waaler test ~ A ~ Heterophile antibodies agglutinate sheep or horse erythrocytes and are absorbed by ox but not guinea pig kidney, and they are often negative in young children, in whom specific EBV serology is needed.
M ~ Why should amoxicillin be avoided in suspected infectious mononucleosis? ~ It precipitates a florid maculopapular rash in most patients ~ It causes haemolysis ~ It reactivates the virus ~ It prolongs the illness ~ A ~ The rash is a non-allergic immune phenomenon and does not indicate permanent penicillin allergy, but the association is a well-known clinical trap when a sore throat is treated empirically.
M ~ Which malignancy is associated with human herpesvirus 8 in immunosuppressed patients? ~ Kaposi sarcoma and primary effusion lymphoma ~ Burkitt lymphoma ~ Nasopharyngeal carcinoma ~ Cervical carcinoma ~ A ~ Epstein-Barr virus is linked with Burkitt and nasopharyngeal carcinoma and human papillomavirus with cervical cancer, so mapping each oncogenic virus to its tumours is a recurring examination point.
H ~ Why does varicella zoster virus cause chickenpox on primary infection but a dermatomal rash on reactivation? ~ Primary infection is viraemic and generalised, whereas reactivation spreads from a single dorsal root ganglion along one dermatome ~ The virus mutates between episodes ~ Antibody is absent in zoster ~ Zoster is caused by a different virus ~ A ~ Cell-mediated immunity contains but does not eliminate ganglionic latency, so waning immunity with age or immunosuppression permits reactivation, which is the rationale for zoster vaccination in older adults.
@ Microbiology ~ Hospital-acquired infection, biomedical waste & infection control ~ MIC_HAI ~ Park's Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine 27e, Ch. Hospital Waste Management and Ananthanarayan 11e, Ch. Hospital Infection
E ~ Which is the single most effective measure to prevent healthcare-associated infection? ~ Hand hygiene at the WHO five moments ~ Routine environmental fogging ~ Prophylactic antibiotics ~ Universal gowning ~ A ~ Compliance with the five moments interrupts transmission via the commonest vehicle, the hands of staff, and alcohol-based rub is preferred except where spores or visible soiling require soap and water.
E ~ A ventilator-associated pneumonia bundle includes which measure to reduce aspiration? ~ Head-of-bed elevation to 30 to 45 degrees ~ Routine ventilator circuit changes daily ~ Prophylactic systemic antibiotics ~ Continuous neuromuscular blockade ~ A ~ Bundles combine elevation, daily sedation interruption with assessment for extubation, oral care with chlorhexidine and peptic ulcer prophylaxis, and their value comes from applying all elements together.
E ~ Which colour-coded bag receives contaminated recyclable plastic waste such as intravenous tubing under the Bio-Medical Waste Rules 2016? ~ Red ~ Yellow ~ White translucent ~ Blue ~ A ~ Yellow takes anatomical, soiled and laboratory waste, white puncture-proof containers take sharps and blue takes glassware and metallic implants, so red is specifically for contaminated recyclable plastics.
M ~ Which organism is the commonest cause of central line-associated bloodstream infection through biofilm formation on the catheter? ~ Coagulase-negative staphylococci ~ Escherichia coli ~ Streptococcus pyogenes ~ Clostridioides difficile ~ A ~ Skin commensals adhere to the polymer surface and elaborate a glycocalyx, which is why maximal sterile barrier precautions at insertion and chlorhexidine skin preparation are central to prevention.
M ~ Which measure most reduces catheter-associated urinary tract infection? ~ Avoiding unnecessary catheterisation and removing the catheter as early as possible ~ Routine bladder irrigation with antiseptic ~ Prophylactic antibiotics for the duration ~ Daily catheter change ~ A ~ Risk rises by roughly 3 to 7 percent per catheter day, so the only reliable interventions are avoiding insertion and prompt removal, with a closed drainage system and aseptic insertion.
M ~ Which type of isolation is required for a patient with pulmonary tuberculosis? ~ Airborne precautions with a negative-pressure room and an N95 respirator ~ Contact precautions with gown and gloves ~ Droplet precautions with a surgical mask ~ Standard precautions only ~ A ~ Droplet nuclei remain suspended and travel beyond a metre, so ventilation engineering and respirator use are needed, unlike influenza or meningococcus which need droplet precautions.
M ~ Post-exposure prophylaxis after a needlestick from an HIV-positive source should be started: ~ As soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours and not beyond 72 hours, for 28 days ~ Only after the source result returns ~ Within 2 weeks ~ Only if seroconversion occurs ~ A ~ Efficacy falls sharply with delay, so the first dose is given on clinical assessment without waiting for baseline results, and a three-drug regimen is continued for 4 weeks with follow-up testing.
H ~ Why is surveillance of surgical site infection rates fed back to individual surgical teams? ~ Confidential feedback of risk-adjusted rates is itself associated with a measurable fall in infection ~ It identifies staff for disciplinary action ~ It replaces the need for prophylaxis ~ It is a statutory requirement only ~ A ~ The Study on the Efficacy of Nosocomial Infection Control demonstrated that surveillance with feedback reduces infection rates, which is why surveillance is regarded as an intervention and not merely a measurement.
@ Microbiology ~ Innate & adaptive immunity, antigen-antibody reactions, vaccines ~ MIC_IMM ~ Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 11e, Ch. Immunity and Immune Response
E ~ Which cell links innate to adaptive immunity by presenting antigen on MHC class II to naive T cells? ~ Dendritic cell ~ Neutrophil ~ Erythrocyte ~ Mast cell ~ A ~ Only professional antigen-presenting cells with co-stimulatory molecules can prime a naive T cell, which is why dendritic cell maturation is the pivotal step in initiating an adaptive response.
E ~ Which vaccine type generally produces the strongest and most durable immunity from a single dose? ~ Live attenuated vaccine ~ Killed whole-cell vaccine ~ Subunit vaccine ~ Toxoid ~ A ~ Replication mimics natural infection and generates both humoral and cell-mediated memory, at the cost of a small risk of vaccine-associated disease and a contraindication in immunodeficiency and pregnancy.
E ~ Which immunoglobulin crosses the placenta and provides passive protection to the newborn? ~ IgG ~ IgM ~ IgA ~ IgD ~ A ~ Transfer is by FcRn-mediated active transport in the third trimester, which is why preterm infants have lower levels and why detecting IgM rather than IgG in a neonate indicates true intrauterine infection.
M ~ Why is a conjugate polysaccharide vaccine effective in infants under 2 years whereas a plain polysaccharide vaccine is not? ~ Conjugation to a protein carrier recruits T cell help, producing memory and a booster response ~ Conjugation increases the antigen dose ~ Plain polysaccharide is destroyed in the gut ~ Conjugation adds an adjuvant effect only ~ A ~ Polysaccharides are T-independent antigens that infants respond to poorly, so linking them to a protein such as tetanus toxoid converts the response to a T-dependent one with affinity maturation and herd protection through reduced carriage.
M ~ Which type of antigen-antibody reaction underlies the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay? ~ Detection of an antigen-antibody complex by an enzyme-linked conjugate producing a colour change ~ Precipitation in gel ~ Complement fixation ~ Direct agglutination ~ A ~ Solid-phase capture with an enzyme-labelled second antibody gives high sensitivity and permits automation, which is why ELISA replaced complement fixation for most serological work.
M ~ Which finding indicates a positive result in the Widal tube agglutination test? ~ Visible clumping of a bacterial suspension by specific antibody at a defined titre ~ A precipitin ring in gel ~ Lysis of red cells ~ Colour change of a substrate ~ A ~ Agglutination requires particulate antigen and is read as the highest serum dilution producing visible clumping; the prozone phenomenon can cause a false negative at low dilutions with very high antibody.
M ~ Which cytokine drives differentiation of naive CD4 T cells towards a Th1 phenotype important in tuberculosis and leprosy? ~ Interleukin-12 from activated macrophages and dendritic cells ~ Interleukin-4 ~ Interleukin-5 ~ Transforming growth factor beta ~ A ~ Interleukin-12 with interferon-gamma establishes the Th1 loop that controls intracellular organisms, and defects in this axis, such as interferon-gamma receptor mutations, cause disseminated mycobacterial disease.
H ~ Why does the immune system fail to make antibody to a hapten unless it is coupled to a carrier? ~ A hapten is too small to cross-link B cell receptors or engage T cell help, so it is immunogenic only when conjugated ~ Haptens are toxic to B cells ~ Haptens cannot enter lymph nodes ~ Haptens are digested by complement ~ A ~ This principle explains penicillin allergy, where the drug acts as a hapten binding host protein, and it is the same principle exploited in conjugate vaccine design.
@ Microbiology ~ Malaria & other protozoa ~ MIC_MOP ~ Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 11e, Ch. Protozoa
E ~ Which stage of the malaria parasite is infective to the mosquito? ~ Gametocyte ~ Sporozoite ~ Trophozoite ~ Schizont ~ A ~ Gametocytes taken up in the blood meal undergo sporogony in the mosquito, which is why gametocytocidal primaquine reduces onward transmission even after the patient is cured.
E ~ Which malarial species most often causes severe disease and why? ~ Plasmodium falciparum, because it infects erythrocytes of all ages and causes cytoadherence and rosetting ~ Plasmodium vivax, because it prefers reticulocytes ~ Plasmodium malariae, because of its long cycle ~ Plasmodium ovale, because of hypnozoites ~ A ~ Knob-mediated adhesion to endothelium causes microvascular obstruction and organ dysfunction, and the absence of an age restriction on host cells permits very high parasitaemia.
E ~ Which protozoan causes dysentery with flask-shaped colonic ulcers and may spread to form a liver abscess containing anchovy-sauce pus? ~ Entamoeba histolytica ~ Giardia lamblia ~ Balantidium coli ~ Cryptosporidium parvum ~ A ~ Trophozoites lyse tissue with amoebapores and cysteine proteases and reach the liver by the portal vein; serology is useful in extraintestinal disease where stool examination is often negative.
M ~ Duffy blood group negativity confers resistance to which malarial species and why? ~ Plasmodium vivax, because the Duffy antigen is its erythrocyte receptor ~ Plasmodium falciparum, because of glycophorin binding ~ Plasmodium malariae ~ Plasmodium knowlesi ~ A ~ The near-absence of Duffy antigen in West Africa explains the historical rarity of vivax malaria there, though Duffy-independent vivax strains have now been described.
M ~ Which protozoan is diagnosed by string test or duodenal aspirate when stool examination is repeatedly negative, and causes fat malabsorption? ~ Giardia lamblia ~ Entamoeba histolytica ~ Trichomonas vaginalis ~ Isospora belli ~ A ~ Trophozoites adhere to the duodenal mucosa with a ventral sucking disc causing villous blunting, and cyst excretion is intermittent, so sampling the duodenum raises the yield.
M ~ Kala-azar is transmitted by which vector, and which diagnostic test is most widely used in the field? ~ Phlebotomus sandfly, with the rK39 rapid antibody test ~ Anopheles mosquito, with a blood smear ~ Reduviid bug, with xenodiagnosis ~ Tsetse fly, with lymph node aspirate ~ A ~ Splenic or bone marrow aspirate showing Leishman-Donovan bodies remains the reference standard, but rK39 gives high sensitivity non-invasively in the Indian subcontinent.
M ~ Which protozoan infection in pregnancy causes fetal hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis and intracranial calcification? ~ Toxoplasma gondii ~ Trypanosoma cruzi ~ Plasmodium vivax ~ Trichomonas vaginalis ~ A ~ The cat is the definitive host and humans acquire infection from oocysts or from tissue cysts in undercooked meat; risk of transmission rises with gestation while severity falls.
H ~ Why does Plasmodium falciparum malaria not relapse, whereas vivax malaria does? ~ Falciparum forms no hepatic hypnozoites, so recurrence is recrudescence from persisting blood forms ~ Falciparum is cleared by immunity ~ Vivax has a longer erythrocytic cycle ~ Falciparum gametocytes persist in the liver ~ A ~ The distinction determines treatment: vivax needs primaquine or tafenoquine for radical cure of hypnozoites, whereas recurrence after falciparum indicates inadequate treatment or resistance.
@ Microbiology ~ Medically important fungi ~ MIC_MIF ~ Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 11e, Ch. Medical Mycology
E ~ Which fungus appears as broad, ribbon-like, sparsely septate hyphae with wide-angle branching on tissue examination? ~ Mucorales such as Rhizopus ~ Aspergillus fumigatus ~ Candida albicans ~ Cryptococcus neoformans ~ A ~ Aspergillus by contrast shows narrow septate hyphae branching at acute angles, and the distinction matters because mucormycosis needs amphotericin B and urgent surgical debridement rather than voriconazole.
E ~ Which fungus is identified by a positive germ tube test and the production of chlamydospores on cornmeal agar? ~ Candida albicans ~ Candida glabrata ~ Cryptococcus neoformans ~ Malassezia furfur ~ A ~ Germ tube formation in serum at 37 degrees and terminal chlamydospore formation together identify Candida albicans, which matters because non-albicans species are commonly azole-resistant.
E ~ Which stain best demonstrates the capsule of Cryptococcus neoformans in cerebrospinal fluid? ~ India ink or nigrosin negative stain ~ Ziehl-Neelsen stain ~ Gram stain ~ Albert stain ~ A ~ The capsule excludes the particulate ink, giving a clear halo around a budding yeast, though cryptococcal antigen testing on serum and cerebrospinal fluid is more sensitive and is now the preferred test.
M ~ A diabetic with poorly controlled ketoacidosis develops black necrotic nasal turbinates with orbital pain and proptosis. The likely organism and why ketoacidosis predisposes are: ~ Mucorales, because acidosis impairs neutrophil function and releases iron from transferrin ~ Aspergillus, because of spore inhalation ~ Candida, because of glycosuria ~ Histoplasma, because of macrophage entry ~ A ~ Free iron is a growth factor for Mucorales and the organism is angioinvasive, causing thrombosis and tissue necrosis, so control of acidosis is part of treatment alongside amphotericin and debridement.
M ~ Which mycosis presents with a subcutaneous nodule ascending along lymphatics after a thorn prick, and is treated with itraconazole? ~ Sporotrichosis ~ Chromoblastomycosis ~ Mycetoma ~ Rhinosporidiosis ~ A ~ Sporothrix schenckii is a dimorphic fungus of soil and plant matter, and the linear chain of nodules following lymphatic drainage is the characteristic gardener's disease pattern.
M ~ Which fungal infection is diagnosed by demonstrating intracellular yeasts within macrophages in a bone marrow aspirate from a patient with hepatosplenomegaly and pancytopenia? ~ Histoplasmosis ~ Blastomycosis ~ Coccidioidomycosis ~ Paracoccidioidomycosis ~ A ~ Histoplasma capsulatum is an intracellular pathogen of the mononuclear phagocyte system, endemic along river valleys and associated with bird and bat droppings, and urinary antigen testing aids rapid diagnosis.
M ~ Which laboratory finding supports invasive aspergillosis in a neutropenic patient with a halo sign on CT? ~ Serum or bronchoalveolar galactomannan antigen ~ Cryptococcal antigen ~ Beta-D-glucan specific to Candida only ~ Urinary Histoplasma antigen ~ A ~ Galactomannan is a cell-wall component released during growth and is useful for both diagnosis and monitoring, while beta-D-glucan is broadly positive across many fungi and is not species specific.
H ~ Why is Pneumocystis jirovecii, although classified as a fungus, treated with cotrimoxazole rather than an azole? ~ Its cell membrane lacks ergosterol, containing cholesterol instead, so azoles are ineffective ~ It is intracellular ~ It has no cell wall at all ~ It is resistant by efflux ~ A ~ The absence of ergosterol removes the azole and amphotericin target, so folate pathway inhibition with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is used, and the organism cannot be cultured, so diagnosis rests on silver or immunofluorescent staining of induced sputum or lavage.
H ~ Which feature of dermatophyte infection explains the raised active border with central clearing of tinea corporis? ~ Centrifugal growth of hyphae in keratin with a host inflammatory response at the advancing edge ~ Deep dermal invasion ~ Toxin production ~ Vascular occlusion ~ A ~ Because the fungus lives in dead keratin and the inflammation is greatest where it is actively invading, samples for microscopy and culture must be taken from the scaly active margin rather than the clearing centre.
